Innovation Strategy

Our innovation strategy consulting helps leaders decide where to innovate, balance a portfolio of core, adjacent and new bets, and build the processes, governance and team conditions that turn ideas into results.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Innovation efforts often generate activity without results. Organizations run idea challenges, establish labs or fund pilots, but projects remain stuck in experimentation, lack a path to scale or are disconnected from strategic priorities. Funding follows the annual budget cycle, which favors predictable projects over uncertain ones. Leaders struggle to compare initiatives at different stages, so the portfolio becomes either too conservative to matter or too scattered to sustain, and promising ideas fail to secure owners in the core business.

The deeper challenges are human. Teams may lack the psychological safety to propose untested ideas or report failed experiments honestly. Middle managers, measured on current performance, have little incentive to release people for innovation work. Innovation teams can become isolated from the operations that must eventually adopt their work. And new products are sometimes designed without meaningful input from the customers and communities they are intended to serve. Innovation strategy consulting must address incentives, culture and the people who will carry ideas forward.

Our approach

How we deliver innovation strategy.

  1. 01Listen

    Finding unmet needs and internal barriers

    We conduct research with customers, non-customers and communities to identify unmet needs and emerging behaviors, and interview leaders, innovators and operational managers about how ideas currently move or stall. A short employee survey assesses innovation climate, including psychological safety, resource availability and leadership support for experimentation.

  2. 02Measure

    Assessing the portfolio and capabilities

    We inventory current innovation initiatives and map them across core, adjacent and transformational horizons, assessing stage, investment, strategic fit and evidence of customer demand. We also assess innovation capabilities, such as research, prototyping, venture processes, partnerships and metrics, identifying where the system breaks down between idea generation and scaled adoption.

  3. 03Design

    Defining the innovation strategy and system

    We help leaders define strategic innovation priorities and the desired portfolio balance, then design the innovation system: stage-gate or venture-style funding, evidence criteria at each stage, governance, team structures and pathways from pilot to scale. Human-centered design and lean experimentation methods are embedded so teams test assumptions with real users early.

  4. 04Deliver

    Launching and building capability

    We support the first cycle of the new process, coaching teams through discovery, prototyping and experimentation on priority opportunities. Leaders and managers learn how to sponsor innovation, evaluate evidence and make continue, pivot or stop decisions. We help create incentives and time allocations that enable people across the organization to participate meaningfully.

  5. 05Sustain

    Governing and learning

    We establish innovation portfolio reviews, leading indicators such as learning velocity and validated assumptions, and lagging indicators such as adoption and revenue from new offerings. Lessons from stopped projects are captured and shared. This governance keeps innovation connected to strategy and ensures the portfolio evolves as markets, technologies and customer needs change.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Innovation shapes which problems get solved and for whom. When grounded in research with customers and communities, including those often overlooked, new products and services can expand access and address unmet needs. Employees gain opportunities to contribute ideas, learn new skills and work in environments where experimentation is valued. A disciplined portfolio uses resources responsibly, and innovation that creates social as well as commercial value strengthens the organization's long-term relevance.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is an innovation portfolio?

An innovation portfolio is the set of innovation initiatives an organization is pursuing, managed as a whole rather than project by project. Portfolios are commonly categorized by horizon or ambition, from improvements to the core business through adjacent opportunities to transformational bets. Managing them as a portfolio helps leaders balance risk and return, allocate resources deliberately and ensure the mix fits the strategy.

How do you measure innovation?

Effective innovation measurement uses different metrics by stage. Early-stage initiatives are measured on learning: assumptions tested, customer evidence gathered and speed of iteration. Later stages are measured on adoption, revenue, cost savings or impact. Applying mature business metrics to early ideas tends to end promising work prematurely, while never applying them allows weak initiatives to continue indefinitely.

Why do innovation labs often fail to deliver results?

Common reasons include isolation from the core business, unclear mandates, lack of sustained funding, no pathway to scale and incentives that discourage operational leaders from adopting new ideas. Labs succeed more often when they work on strategic priorities, involve business owners from the start, use clear evidence criteria and have agreed routes for transferring successful work into operations.

How does psychological safety affect innovation?

Innovation requires people to propose untested ideas, admit uncertainty and report failures honestly. Research on psychological safety, notably Amy Edmondson's work, shows that teams who believe they will not be punished for speaking up are more likely to learn and experiment. Leaders build it by inviting input, responding constructively to bad news and treating well-designed failed experiments as useful learning.
