Technology Adoption Strategy

A technology adoption strategy treats usage as a behavior to be designed, not a training checkbox. We identify the signals that predict lasting adoption, address the real barriers for each user group and test what makes new tools stick.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Organizations invest substantial sums in new platforms, from customer-facing apps and patient portals to CRM systems, clinical tools and AI assistants. Yet usage often falls short of the business case. Employees revert to spreadsheets and old workflows. Customers download an app once and never return. Clinicians find workarounds that bypass a new system. The technology works as specified, but the human behavior it depends on never fully materializes, and the expected return quietly disappears.

Adoption problems are rarely solved by more training sessions or louder announcements. People adopt technology when it offers a clear advantage for their own work, fits their existing routines, is easy to try, is visibly used by respected peers and works reliably at the moments that matter. Research on diffusion of innovation and technology acceptance has shown this for decades. A technology adoption strategy applies those insights deliberately and measures adoption as behavior rather than as logins.

Our approach

How we deliver technology adoption strategy.

  1. 01Observe

    Learn how people work today

    We study current workflows, informal tools and workarounds through contextual inquiry and interviews with users at every level, including skeptics and early enthusiasts. We map where the new technology changes routines, who gains and who loses effort, and what users believe about its usefulness, ease and risk.

  2. 02Signal

    Define and track real adoption

    We define adoption as specific, valuable behaviors, not logins, and build measures of depth and breadth of use. Usage data reveals activation moments that predict sustained use, adopter types from early adopters to resisters, and teams where adoption stalls. Survival analysis shows when and where people abandon the tool.

  3. 03Design

    Design for each adopter type

    Drawing on diffusion of innovation research and the motivation, ability and prompt lens, we design targeted interventions: workflow integration, simplified first tasks, peer champions, manager prompts, just-in-time help and changes to the tool itself. Messaging emphasizes relative advantage and compatibility with what users already value.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot adoption interventions

    We pilot interventions with selected teams or user groups, using comparison groups or staged rollouts to measure their effect on activation and sustained use. Usability testing addresses friction in the tool itself. Guardrails monitor workload, error rates and satisfaction so adoption does not come at the cost of performance.

  5. 05Scale

    Roll out with leaders and champions

    Successful approaches are scaled through phased rollout, peer champion networks, leader coaching and adoption dashboards that show where support is needed. We help leaders model use, remove legacy alternatives at the right time and maintain momentum after launch, when adoption often quietly erodes once attention moves elsewhere.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

When adoption is designed well, people gain tools that genuinely make work and life easier, rather than another burden. Clinicians recover time for patients, employees spend less effort on duplicate systems, and customers access services more conveniently. We pay attention to people who are often left behind, including older adults and those with limited digital access, and design adoption paths that include them rather than leaving them with fewer options.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Why do employees resist new technology?

Resistance usually reflects reasonable concerns: the new tool adds effort, disrupts familiar routines, seems risky, offers unclear personal benefit or does not work reliably in real conditions. Treating resistance as information rather than opposition reveals what needs to change. Addressing these barriers directly, and involving users early, is far more effective than mandates or repeated training.

What is diffusion of innovation and why does it matter?

Diffusion of innovation research describes how new ideas and technologies spread through groups over time, from early adopters to later majorities. It identifies the characteristics that speed adoption, such as relative advantage, compatibility, simplicity, trialability and observability. These principles help organizations sequence rollout, choose champions and shape the technology and messaging to accelerate uptake.

How do you measure technology adoption?

We move beyond logins and downloads to measure the specific behaviors that create value, such as completing key workflows in the new system, frequency and depth of use, and abandonment of legacy alternatives. We also identify activation moments that predict long-term use. These behavioral measures are tracked by team and user type so support can be targeted.

Can you help with AI adoption?

Yes. AI tools raise particular adoption challenges, including uncertainty about accuracy, fear of job change and unclear norms about appropriate use. We combine adoption strategy with human-computer interaction design and responsible AI practices, helping organizations build calibrated trust, clear guidelines and workflows where AI genuinely supports people rather than adding risk or confusion.
