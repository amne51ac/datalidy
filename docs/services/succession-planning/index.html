Succession Planning

Succession planning consulting for boards and executive teams who want ready successors for the CEO and critical roles, built on clear success profiles, objective assessment of internal talent and deliberate development.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Leadership transitions are among the riskiest moments in an organization's life, yet many boards and executive teams lack credible succession plans. Plans may exist on paper, listing names next to roles, but they often rest on informal impressions, favor candidates who resemble current leaders and overlook talent below the senior team. When a CEO departs unexpectedly or a critical executive resigns, the organization discovers that its bench is thinner than assumed and turns to an external search under time pressure.

Succession planning is also sensitive. Incumbents may be reluctant to discuss their departure, high-potential candidates may leave if they cannot see a path forward, and processes that feel opaque can damage trust. Boards need to fulfill their responsibility for CEO succession without destabilizing the current leadership. Organizations need succession planning that is rigorous, fair and forward-looking, designed around what future roles will require rather than what current incumbents do today.

Our approach

How we deliver succession planning.

  1. 01Listen

    Define future role requirements

    We interview board members, the CEO and executives to understand strategic direction and what future leaders will need to deliver. For each critical role, we develop a success profile describing the experiences, capabilities and attributes required, looking ahead to the organization's next phase rather than backward at the incumbent.

  2. 02Measure

    Objective bench assessment

    We assess internal candidates against the success profiles using structured interviews, multi-rater feedback and, where appropriate, validated assessments. We map readiness levels, such as ready now or ready in a few years, and analyze the diversity and depth of each bench, including risks where single candidates or none exist.

  3. 03Design

    Succession plan and development

    We build succession plans for each critical role, with targeted development for each candidate: stretch assignments, broader exposure, coaching and board visibility. We design emergency succession arrangements and define when external benchmarking or search should complement internal candidates. Plans also address knowledge transfer, the relationships successors must build and how candidates will be told about their development without implied promises.

  4. 04Deliver

    Development and transition support

    We support candidates through development, including coaching and assignment design, and help boards run CEO selection processes when the time comes. For transitions, we plan the handover, the onboarding of the new leader and the role of the departing executive, so continuity is preserved. Departing leaders are supported thoughtfully.

  5. 05Sustain

    Regular talent reviews

    We establish an annual or semiannual talent review process for the board and executive team, with metrics such as bench coverage, readiness progress and internal fill rates. This keeps succession a living process rather than a document reviewed once and forgotten. Calibration sessions help reduce bias in readiness ratings.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

Our succession planning clients include board chairs, nominating and governance committees, chief executives and chief people officers in companies, family businesses, private equity portfolio companies, health systems, universities and nonprofits. Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Thoughtful succession planning protects the people who depend on an organization. Employees experience less disruption when leadership transitions are well prepared, customers see continuity in relationships and service, and communities that rely on hospitals, universities or major employers benefit from stability. Objective, transparent succession processes also widen opportunity, helping talented people from varied backgrounds be recognized and developed. Planning ahead for transitions honors the organization's mission by ensuring it outlasts any single leader.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is succession planning?

Succession planning is the process of identifying and developing people to fill critical leadership roles when they become vacant. It involves defining what each role will require in the future, assessing internal candidates, creating development plans to close readiness gaps and preparing for both planned and emergency transitions. Effective succession planning is ongoing rather than a one-time exercise.

What is the board's role in CEO succession?

Boards are responsible for ensuring there is a credible plan for CEO succession, both for planned transitions and emergencies. That includes agreeing on the future leadership profile, overseeing assessment and development of internal candidates, deciding whether to consider external candidates and ultimately selecting the next CEO. Boards should review succession regularly, not only when a transition is imminent.

How far in advance should CEO succession planning begin?

Ideally, succession planning is continuous, with a meaningful plan in place several years before an expected transition. That timeframe allows internal candidates to gain the experience they need and gives the board time to evaluate them. An emergency succession plan should exist at all times, regardless of the expected timing of the CEO's departure.

How do you make succession planning fair and inclusive?

We use success profiles tied to future needs rather than the incumbent's style, structured assessment methods that reduce bias, and calibration sessions that challenge assumptions. We look deeper into the organization for potential candidates and examine whether development opportunities are distributed fairly. Transparent criteria help candidates understand how decisions are made.
