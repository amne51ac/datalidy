Customer & Employee Journey Mapping

Our customer journey mapping and employee journey mapping services combine field research with operational data to show where experiences break down, why they break, and which fixes will matter most to people and performance.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Journey maps have become common, and many organizations have several on the wall. Too often they were built in a single workshop from internal opinion, describe an idealized path that few customers actually follow, and stop at the edge of the customer experience without connecting to the operations behind it. They look polished but do not answer the questions leaders care about: where are we losing people, which pain points matter most, who owns the fix, and how will we know if it worked?

Employee journeys receive even less rigor. Hiring, onboarding, internal mobility and exit are typically designed by separate teams and rarely viewed from the employee's perspective as a single experience. Yet the employee journey and the customer journey are connected: a frontline worker who cannot get answers from internal systems cannot give answers to customers. Useful customer journey mapping links both perspectives, is built on evidence, quantifies impact, and connects to the people and processes responsible for change.

Our approach

How we deliver customer & employee journey mapping.

  1. 01Listen

    Gather journey evidence from real people

    We interview and observe customers and employees across segments, channels and outcomes, including people who abandoned the journey, not only those who completed it. We capture actions, thoughts, emotions and touchpoints at each stage, and we talk with frontline staff who see where journeys go wrong every day. This grounds the map in lived experience rather than assumed paths.

  2. 02Measure

    Quantify drop-off and pain points

    We connect journey stages to data: funnel conversion, time between stages, contact reasons, complaint themes, survey scores and attrition. We look at variation across segments to identify where particular groups struggle more. Quantification turns a descriptive map into a decision tool, showing which pain points affect the most people and which are most strongly linked to outcomes leaders track.

  3. 03Design

    Build maps that connect to operations

    We create current-state maps for each priority journey, layering customer or employee experience over the touchpoints, teams and systems involved. Where useful we extend maps into service blueprints. We facilitate working sessions with journey owners to identify root causes and design future-state journeys, prioritizing opportunities by impact on people, effort to deliver and strategic fit.

  4. 04Deliver

    Translate maps into a change portfolio

    We turn journey opportunities into a prioritized portfolio of initiatives with owners, scope and measures. We help teams test priority fixes quickly and sequence larger changes within existing planning cycles. Maps are designed to be shared widely, giving executives, frontline teams and technology partners a common reference for why each change matters and who it serves.

  5. 05Sustain

    Manage journeys as living assets

    We help assign journey owners, define journey-level measures, and set a regular review rhythm. Maps are kept in editable formats and updated as new research and data arrive. We often connect journey stages to dashboards so leaders can track experience and outcomes by stage, and we train teams to maintain and extend the maps without outside support.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Journey mapping makes visible the burden that organizations quietly place on people: the forms, waits, repeated explanations and uncertainty. Seeing journeys from the perspective of the person living them helps organizations remove barriers that fall hardest on those with the least time, money or support. Mapping employee and customer journeys together recognizes that staff wellbeing and service quality are linked. For mission-driven organizations, journey maps often reveal where eligible people fall out of programs designed to help them.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is customer journey mapping?

Customer journey mapping is a method for visualizing the steps, interactions, thoughts and emotions a customer goes through to achieve a goal with an organization, such as opening an account or resolving a problem. A good journey map is based on research rather than assumptions, highlights pain points and moments that matter, and connects the customer experience to the teams and systems responsible. It helps leaders prioritize improvements and align functions around the customer.

What is an employee journey map?

An employee journey map applies the same method to the experience of working in an organization, covering stages such as recruitment, onboarding, development, internal moves and exit. It shows how employees experience processes owned by different functions, where friction and disengagement occur, and how experiences differ across roles and groups. Employee journey maps help HR and operations leaders design a more coherent, fair and supportive experience of work.

What is the difference between a journey map and a service blueprint?

A journey map focuses on the experience from the customer or employee perspective, including actions, emotions and touchpoints. A service blueprint extends below the surface to show the frontstage staff interactions, backstage processes and support systems that produce that experience. Journey maps are useful for prioritizing what to improve. Blueprints are useful for understanding how to change the operations that deliver it.

How many journeys should we map?

Fewer than most organizations expect. We usually recommend starting with a small number of high-priority journeys tied to strategic goals, such as onboarding new customers or retaining early-tenure employees, and mapping them in depth with research and data. A broad catalog of shallow maps is less useful than a few rigorous ones with clear owners and measures. Additional journeys can follow once the approach is established.
