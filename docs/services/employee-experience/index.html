Employee Experience Design

Our employee experience design work researches and redesigns the moments that shape how people join, work, grow and leave your organization, so that employees can do their best work and serve customers well.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most organizations know their employee experience matters but manage it in fragments. Recruiting owns the candidate experience, HR operations owns onboarding paperwork, IT owns equipment, managers own everything else, and no one owns the whole. New hires spend their first week waiting for system access. Internal transfers feel like starting over. Policies on flexibility, leave or promotion are applied inconsistently because managers interpret them differently. Engagement surveys signal that something is wrong, but they rarely show exactly where the experience breaks down or what to change.

The consequences extend beyond HR. Early tenure turnover is often the most preventable and most costly. Friction in everyday work, such as redundant approvals or poor tools, consumes time and energy that should go to customers. In frontline-heavy organizations, the experience of employees directly shapes the experience of customers. Employee experience design addresses these problems by treating work itself as a service that the organization provides to its people, with the same rigor applied to customer experience.

Our approach

How we deliver employee experience design.

  1. 01Listen

    Research working life as it is

    We conduct interviews, focus groups and job shadowing across roles, locations and tenure bands, with deliberate inclusion of frontline, remote and hourly employees who are often underrepresented in corporate research. Diary studies capture experiences such as onboarding or return from leave over time. We listen for the moments that shape whether people feel capable, respected and connected to their work.

  2. 02Measure

    Connect experience to outcomes

    We analyze survey results, HR system data, exit data and operational metrics together to identify where experience gaps relate to attrition, absence, time to productivity or service quality. We look at differences across demographic groups, roles and managers to find where experiences diverge. This evidence focuses design effort on moments with the strongest link to outcomes employees and leaders both care about.

  3. 03Design

    Co-design the moments that matter

    We map the employee lifecycle and blueprint priority moments such as hiring, onboarding, first promotion, becoming a manager, parental leave and exit. Employees and managers co-design improvements with us, which raises the quality of ideas and builds ownership. We prototype changes, from onboarding sequences to manager guides and policy wording, and test them with real employees before rollout.

  4. 04Deliver

    Coordinate across HR, IT and operations

    Employee experience improvements cross functional lines, so we help set up cross-functional teams with clear ownership for each moment. We support pilots, manager enablement, communications and changes to HR technology configuration. We sequence work to deliver visible early improvements, which builds credibility with employees who may be skeptical after previous survey cycles with little follow-through.

  5. 05Sustain

    Build continuous listening into the lifecycle

    We design lifecycle pulse surveys at key moments, define a small set of experience and outcome measures, and build dashboards for HR and line leaders. We help establish an employee experience governance forum and a cadence for reviewing results and acting on them, so the organization keeps improving the experience as roles, policies and workplaces change.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Work occupies a large share of people's lives, and the way it is designed affects their health, dignity and economic security. Better employee experiences reduce unnecessary stress, make growth opportunities more visible and fair, and help people feel valued. Those effects reach customers through better service and reach communities through more stable employment and healthier workers. Designing work with employees, rather than for them, also strengthens the voice of people whose perspectives are often missing from organizational decisions.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is employee experience design?

Employee experience design applies human-centered design methods to working life. It studies how employees experience key moments, such as joining, learning, changing roles and leaving, and redesigns the policies, processes, tools and manager behaviors that shape those moments. Unlike engagement initiatives that focus on survey scores, employee experience design treats work as a service the organization provides and improves it through research, prototyping and measurement.

How is this different from an engagement survey program?

Engagement surveys measure how employees feel overall and can highlight problem areas, but they rarely explain what is causing the issue or what to change. Employee experience design starts with research into specific moments, connects experiences to outcomes like retention, and designs and tests concrete improvements. We often integrate survey programs into the work as one source of evidence and as a way to track whether changes are working.

Which moments should we focus on first?

It depends on your workforce and goals, but onboarding, the transition into management, internal mobility, leave and return, and exit are common priorities because they strongly influence retention and performance. We use your data to identify where experience gaps are most closely linked to outcomes, and we listen to employees about where friction is greatest. That combination points to the moments where improvement will matter most.

Can employee experience design work for frontline and hourly workforces?

Yes, and it is often most valuable there. Frontline employees experience scheduling, pay accuracy, equipment, training and supervisor relationships very directly, and these factors strongly affect retention and customer service. We adapt research methods to shift patterns and work settings, including on-site observation, short mobile surveys and paid participation, so that frontline voices shape the design rather than being represented by corporate assumptions.
