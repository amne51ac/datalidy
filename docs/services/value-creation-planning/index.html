Value Creation Planning

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Value creation plans are usually built from the financial model outward: pricing initiatives, procurement savings, add-on acquisitions, new market entry, operational improvement. Each lever looks sound on a spreadsheet. What is often missing is a rigorous view of who will do the work, whether they have the capacity and capability, and what organizational changes are required. Plans that ask an already stretched leadership team to run five major initiatives simultaneously tend to stall, regardless of the quality of the analysis behind them.

The first hundred days set the tone for the entire hold period. Employees are watching for signals about the new owner, managers are uncertain about their roles, and the leadership team is absorbing new reporting requirements. A value creation plan that ignores this human context can trigger the very attrition and disengagement that undermine it. Sponsors and management teams need plans that are ambitious about outcomes and honest about the people and organizational conditions needed to get there.

Our approach

How we deliver value creation planning.

  1. 01Listen

    Test the plan with those who will run it

    We interview the leadership team, key managers and selected frontline employees about each value creation lever: what it will take, what has been tried before and what might get in the way. These conversations surface execution risks and hidden capacity constraints that do not appear in the financial model.

  2. 02Measure

    Capacity and capability baseline

    We map initiatives against the leaders and teams responsible, assess bandwidth and skills gaps, and establish a baseline of people indicators such as turnover in critical roles, manager spans and engagement. This shows where the plan is under-resourced and which capabilities must be hired, built or borrowed.

  3. 03Design

    An integrated, sequenced plan

    We co-design a value creation plan that pairs each lever with named owners, required organizational changes, talent moves and milestones. Initiatives are sequenced to respect change capacity. The 100-day plan sets out early decisions, quick wins and communications, with clear governance for how the sponsor and management will review progress.

  4. 04Deliver

    Program management and enablement

    We help stand up a value creation office or equivalent governance rhythm, support leaders in launching initiatives, and coach managers on communicating change. Where new roles are created, we help define them and integrate new hires. The emphasis is on building internal capability to run the plan.

  5. 05Sustain

    Leading indicators and course correction

    We build a dashboard that tracks both financial milestones and the people indicators that predict them, such as hiring velocity for growth roles or retention of key operators. Quarterly reviews use this evidence to adjust priorities before problems show up in the results. Boards see causes, not only results.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

A well-designed value creation plan can raise the quality of work as well as the quality of earnings. When plans account for workload, invest in managers and create clearer roles, employees experience less chaos and more opportunity to grow. Customers benefit from steadier service and fewer disruptions during change. Plans that consider local hiring, fair pay progression and community relationships can also strengthen the places where portfolio companies operate. These outcomes support the financial thesis rather than compete with it.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is a value creation plan in private equity?

A value creation plan sets out how an investor and management team intend to increase the value of a business during the holding period. It typically covers revenue growth, margin improvement, add-on acquisitions, capital efficiency and organizational strengthening, with milestones, owners and governance. The best plans connect each initiative to the people and capabilities required, because execution capacity is often the binding constraint.

What should a 100-day plan include?

A strong 100-day plan covers early leadership decisions, confirmation of the value creation priorities, key talent retention, reporting and governance changes, quick wins that build credibility, and a communication plan for employees, customers and other stakeholders. It should be specific about owners and dates, and realistic about how much change the organization can absorb while still running the business.

How do you account for people in a value creation plan?

For each initiative, we identify who will lead it, what skills and capacity it requires, which organizational changes it depends on, and which employees are critical to its success. We then compare those requirements with the current organization. Gaps become explicit actions, such as a new hire, a role change, training or retention arrangements, and are tracked like any other milestone.

Can you support a plan that has already been written?

Yes. Many engagements begin with a plan that exists but is not progressing as expected. We review it with the leadership team, diagnose where people and organizational constraints are slowing execution, and recommend adjustments to sequencing, ownership, resourcing or governance. This is often faster and more useful than starting again.
