The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Dashboards are easy to build and hard to make useful. Many organizations have dozens of reports that are rarely opened, show too many metrics without context, and answer questions nobody asked. Leaders still request one-off analyses because existing dashboards do not reflect the decisions in front of them. Metrics such as turnover, engagement or people served are defined differently across systems, so numbers do not reconcile and confidence erodes. Boards and investors receive human capital data in inconsistent formats from one quarter to the next.

Impact reporting presents an additional challenge. Mission-driven organizations, impact investors and companies with social commitments need to show outcomes for people and communities, not just activity counts. That requires a clear theory of change, outcome indicators, and data that may come from surveys, program records or partners. People data also brings privacy risk: dashboards that slice results too finely can expose individuals. Well-designed people dashboards are built around decisions, use a limited set of meaningful indicators, protect privacy, and include a plan for who acts on what.

Our approach

How we deliver impact & people dashboards.

  1. 01Listen

    Start with users and decisions

    We interview dashboard users, from board members and executives to HR partners, program managers and frontline supervisors, to understand the decisions they make, how often, and what information would change them. We review existing reports and how they are used. Users help define what a useful dashboard looks like before any design begins.

  2. 02Measure

    Define indicators that mean something

    We select a focused set of leading and lagging indicators linked to strategy or theory of change, and write precise metric definitions with formulas, sources, refresh frequency and owners. For impact dashboards, we align indicators with recognized frameworks where relevant. We assess data quality for each indicator and resolve inconsistencies across systems before anything is visualized.

  3. 03Design

    Design for clarity and privacy

    We design dashboards that answer questions in order of importance, with context such as targets, trends and comparisons, and with accessible visual design that meets WCAG contrast and screen reader needs. We apply minimum group sizes and role-based views to protect privacy. Prototypes are tested with users and refined until the key message is clear at a glance.

  4. 04Deliver

    Build, integrate and launch

    We build dashboards in your chosen business intelligence platform or recommend one if needed, with automated data pipelines where feasible. We document data lineage and calculations, test accuracy against source systems, and train users to interpret the information and to recognize its limits. Launches include working sessions that connect dashboards to real decisions and meeting rhythms.

  5. 05Sustain

    Govern metrics and keep them relevant

    We establish metric ownership, a data dictionary, change control for definitions, and a periodic review to retire unused views and add new questions. We monitor usage to see which dashboards inform decisions. Internal teams receive documentation and training to maintain dashboards, and we help design board and investor reporting packages that stay consistent over time.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Dashboards shape attention, and attention shapes action. When organizations track employee wellbeing, fairness and development alongside financial results, those outcomes receive the management focus they deserve. Impact dashboards help mission-driven organizations see who they are reaching and whether people's lives are improving, and direct resources toward what works. Clear, honest reporting builds trust with employees, communities and funders. Privacy-conscious design ensures that measuring people never comes at the cost of exposing them.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What should a people dashboard include?

A people dashboard should include a focused set of indicators tied to strategic priorities, typically covering workforce composition, hiring, retention, internal mobility, engagement or experience, and fairness measures such as pay equity or promotion rates. It should combine leading indicators, which signal future outcomes, with lagging indicators that confirm results. Each metric needs a clear definition, a target or benchmark for context, and an owner responsible for action.

What is the difference between a people dashboard and an impact dashboard?

A people dashboard focuses on the workforce: talent, experience, fairness and capability. An impact dashboard tracks outcomes for the people and communities an organization serves, such as program reach, client outcomes or social and environmental results, usually guided by a theory of change. Many mission-driven organizations and impact investors benefit from both, viewed together, because workforce health often shapes the quality of impact.

Which dashboard tools do you work with?

We work with common business intelligence platforms as well as the reporting modules within HR and program management systems. We are not tied to any vendor. Where clients do not yet have a platform, we help assess options based on existing technology, budget, data sources, security requirements and internal skills, and we design dashboards so that definitions and logic are documented independently of the tool.

How do you keep people dashboards from exposing individuals?

We apply minimum group sizes so that results for small teams or demographic groups are suppressed, use role-based access so users see only what their role requires, and avoid filters that allow combinations to isolate individuals. Sensitive measures such as pay or demographic data receive additional controls. These safeguards are documented in governance policies and tested before launch.
