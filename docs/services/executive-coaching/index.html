Executive Coaching

Executive coaching for CEOs, senior executives and high-potential leaders facing new roles, larger mandates or difficult transitions, grounded in candid feedback and focused on measurable changes in how you lead.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Senior leaders rarely receive honest feedback. The higher they rise, the more people filter what they hear, and the fewer peers they have with whom to think through difficult decisions. Executives stepping into larger roles, such as a first CEO appointment, a functional leader joining the executive team or a founder leading through institutional investment, often discover that the habits that brought them success now limit them. Without support, they may adapt slowly, at real cost to their teams and organizations.

Organizations also struggle to get value from coaching. Engagements drift without clear goals, the sponsor has little visibility into progress, and coaches with mixed qualifications offer advice disconnected from the business context. Coaching is sometimes used as a remedial last step rather than as an investment in leaders with significant potential. Executives and their sponsors need coaching that is confidential yet accountable, grounded in evidence about how the leader is experienced, and tied to the outcomes the role demands.

Our approach

How we deliver executive coaching.

  1. 01Listen

    Understand the leader and the context

    We begin with conversations with the leader and their sponsor, such as the CEO, board chair or chief people officer, to understand the role, the challenges ahead and what success would look like. We agree on confidentiality boundaries and how progress will be shared. Expectations are written down.

  2. 02Measure

    Multi-source feedback

    We gather structured feedback through interviews with direct reports, peers, board members and others who work closely with the leader, often complemented by a validated assessment. This produces a candid, specific picture of strengths, blind spots and how the leader's behavior affects others. The leader receives it in a facilitated session.

  3. 03Design

    A focused development plan

    With the leader, we translate feedback into two or three development goals tied to the demands of the role, each with observable behaviors that colleagues would notice. The plan is shared with the sponsor so expectations are aligned and the organization can support the leader's growth.

  4. 04Deliver

    Regular coaching on real situations

    Coaching sessions focus on the leader's live challenges: an upcoming board meeting, a difficult team conversation, a restructuring decision. We help the leader prepare, practice, reflect and adjust. Between sessions, the leader applies new approaches and gathers feedback, building durable habits. Sessions can be in person or virtual, with access between sessions for urgent moments.

  5. 05Sustain

    Review progress and hand over

    At midpoint and close, we gather follow-up feedback from colleagues to assess change. A final review with the sponsor agrees on next steps, so development continues after the engagement through the leader's own practices and support network. That may include peer support, a mentor, ongoing feedback routines or periodic check-ins with the coach.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We coach chief executives, executive team members, founders, managing partners, executive directors of nonprofits and senior public officials. Engagements are often sponsored by boards, CEOs, chief people officers or private equity operating partners. Common trigger moments include a promotion or new appointment, onboarding an executive hired from outside, a founder transitioning to a professional CEO role, preparation for succession, a leadership team under strain, or feedback indicating that a strong performer's style is limiting their impact.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

When senior leaders change how they lead, the effects reach far beyond them. A CEO who listens better, delegates more clearly or handles conflict more constructively shapes the experience of everyone who reports to them, and of those further down the line. That improves retention, reduces stress and creates room for people to contribute fully. Executive coaching for leaders of mission-driven organizations and public agencies can also strengthen services that customers and communities depend on.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is executive coaching?

Executive coaching is a confidential, one-on-one development relationship between a senior leader and a qualified coach. It focuses on specific goals tied to the leader's role, typically informed by feedback from colleagues. Unlike training, coaching works on the leader's real situations and decisions. Good coaching builds self-awareness, expands the leader's options and produces behavior changes that colleagues notice.

How long does an executive coaching engagement last?

Most engagements run between six and twelve months, with sessions every two to four weeks. That timeframe allows for feedback, goal setting, sustained practice and a follow-up assessment of change. Shorter engagements can support a specific transition, such as the first months in a new role, while longer relationships sometimes suit CEOs who value a consistent thinking partner.

Is executive coaching confidential?

Yes. The content of coaching conversations remains confidential between the leader and coach. At the outset, we agree with the leader and sponsor on what will be shared, typically the development goals and overall progress. This balance protects the trust coaching requires while giving the organization appropriate visibility into its investment.

How do you measure coaching results?

We measure change against the goals agreed at the start, using follow-up feedback from the same colleagues who contributed to the initial assessment. We also consider evidence such as team engagement, retention or progress on business objectives connected to the goals. The sponsor and leader review results together at the end of the engagement.
