Pay Equity Analysis

Our pay equity analysis uses regression-based statistical models to identify pay differences by gender, race and ethnicity that legitimate factors do not explain, then guides fair remediation and stronger pay practices going forward.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Pay equity has moved from a values question to a compliance and reputational priority. Pay transparency laws in a growing number of jurisdictions require salary ranges in job postings, and some require employers to report pay data by demographic group. Employees can now compare pay more easily than ever. At the same time, many organizations do not know whether they have unexplained pay gaps, or they rely on simple average comparisons that mix up differences in job, level and tenure with differences in treatment.

Pay gaps usually emerge from ordinary processes rather than deliberate decisions: starting salaries set by negotiation, inconsistent merit increases, off-cycle adjustments, acquisitions that brought different pay structures, and job architecture that leaves similar roles in different grades. Fixing individual salaries without addressing these root causes means gaps reappear. Effective pay equity analysis requires sound statistical methods, careful definition of comparable groups, attorney-client privilege where appropriate, defensible remediation, and changes to the pay practices themselves.

Our approach

How we deliver pay equity analysis.

  1. 01Listen

    Understand pay practices and context

    We interview compensation, HR and legal leaders to understand pay philosophy, job architecture, pay decision processes and recent organizational changes. We review policies for starting pay, merit, promotion and exceptions. Where appropriate, we listen to employee perceptions of pay fairness through focus groups or survey data, because perceived fairness matters alongside statistical findings.

  2. 02Measure

    Build defensible statistical models

    We define similarly situated employee groupings, select legitimate pay factors such as job level, function, location, tenure and performance, and test them for potential bias. We use multiple regression to estimate pay differences by gender, race and ethnicity after controlling for those factors, and we flag statistically significant gaps and individual outliers. Cohort reviews complement regression where groups are too small for modeling.

  3. 03Design

    Design remediation and process fixes

    We model remediation scenarios, including the cost and effect of adjusting individuals below predicted pay, and help leaders choose an approach that is fair and defensible. We trace gaps to root causes and recommend process changes: structured starting pay guidelines, merit calibration, promotion pay rules, job architecture adjustments and controls on exceptions.

  4. 04Deliver

    Implement adjustments and communicate

    We support implementation of pay adjustments in coordination with payroll, legal counsel and managers. We help prepare communications for leaders, managers and employees, including guidance for managers on discussing pay. Where transparency or reporting requirements apply, we help prepare disclosures and pay range documentation consistent with your compensation structure.

  5. 05Sustain

    Monitor pay decisions continuously

    We build pay equity checks into compensation cycles so that new hires, promotions and merit increases are reviewed before decisions are final. We set up a recurring analysis cadence, typically annual, with dashboards for compensation teams and documented methodology for audit purposes. We train internal analysts to run models and interpret results consistently.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Fair pay is foundational to economic security and dignity at work. Correcting unexplained gaps puts money directly in the hands of employees who were underpaid, and the effects reach their families and communities. Fixing the underlying pay practices prevents the next generation of gaps. Transparent, consistent pay decisions strengthen trust and help organizations attract and keep talent from all backgrounds. For society, employer action on pay equity contributes to narrowing broader economic disparities by gender, race and ethnicity.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is a pay equity analysis?

A pay equity analysis is a statistical review of whether employees doing comparable work are paid fairly, regardless of protected characteristics such as gender, race or ethnicity. It typically uses multiple regression to account for legitimate factors like job level, location and tenure, then tests whether any remaining pay differences are associated with demographic group. The analysis identifies gaps, individual outliers and the pay practices that contribute to them.

What is the difference between the pay gap and pay equity?

The raw pay gap compares average pay between groups across the whole organization, without accounting for differences in jobs or levels. It often reflects representation, such as fewer women in senior roles. Pay equity asks whether people in comparable roles with comparable qualifications are paid the same. Both matter: the adjusted gap reveals unequal treatment, while the raw gap points to opportunity and advancement issues.

Should pay equity analysis be conducted under attorney-client privilege?

Many organizations conduct the analysis at the direction of legal counsel to protect findings while they are reviewed and remediated. Whether privilege is appropriate depends on your legal context, jurisdiction and goals, including any public reporting commitments. We work with your internal or external counsel to structure the engagement appropriately and ensure that documentation supports both legal protection and practical action.

How often should we conduct a pay equity analysis?

We recommend a full analysis at least annually, ideally timed before the merit cycle so adjustments can be integrated into regular pay decisions. Organizations undergoing rapid growth, acquisitions or restructuring may need more frequent reviews. Building checks into hiring, promotion and merit processes provides continuous monitoring between full analyses and helps prevent new gaps from forming.
