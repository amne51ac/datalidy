Team Effectiveness

Team effectiveness consulting for leadership teams and critical cross-functional teams that need to make better decisions together, work through tension productively and deliver shared priorities rather than parallel agendas.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Many senior teams are groups of capable individuals rather than teams. Each executive runs their function well, but the team spends meeting time on updates rather than decisions, avoids difficult conversations and leaves cross-functional problems unresolved. Members may agree in the room and act differently afterward. These dynamics are rarely discussed openly, yet employees feel them through conflicting priorities, slow decisions and signals that leaders are not aligned. The cost is slower execution and lost confidence.

Cross-functional teams face similar challenges with less authority. Product teams, transformation programs, clinical units and project teams must coordinate across reporting lines, often with unclear decision rights and competing incentives. Hybrid and distributed work adds complexity. Generic team-building events can create goodwill but rarely change how a team operates. Teams need a clear diagnosis of what is limiting their effectiveness and practical changes to purpose, structure, routines and relationships. The work must change, not only the mood.

Our approach

How we deliver team effectiveness.

  1. 01Listen

    Individual interviews

    We interview each team member and selected stakeholders who work with the team, exploring purpose, priorities, decision-making, meeting quality, trust and how conflict is handled. Confidential conversations surface views members may not share in the group, including perceptions of the leader's role. All input is reported in themes, not by name.

  2. 02Measure

    Team diagnostic

    We use a validated team effectiveness survey and observe the team in action, often in its regular meetings. Where useful, we analyze meeting time allocation and decision flow. Findings show how the team rates itself on key conditions for effectiveness, such as clear purpose, right membership, psychological safety and supportive context.

  3. 03Design

    Team charter and operating rhythm

    In facilitated working sessions, the team agrees on its shared purpose and priorities, which decisions belong to the team versus individuals, and how it will work together, including meeting design, norms for debate and commitments to each other. The output is a practical team charter, not an abstract values list.

  4. 04Deliver

    Work on real issues

    We facilitate sessions where the team applies new practices to its actual agenda: making a difficult resource decision, resolving a cross-functional conflict or setting annual priorities. We coach the team leader and give real-time feedback on dynamics, helping new habits take hold. Sessions are short, focused and regular.

  5. 05Sustain

    Pulse checks and team reviews

    We conduct brief pulse surveys and periodic team reviews to track progress against the baseline. The team learns to review its own effectiveness regularly, so improvement continues as membership and priorities change. We equip the team leader to facilitate these reviews independently, including how to raise difficult topics and renegotiate commitments, so the practice continues without outside facilitation as the team evolves.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

Team effectiveness work serves CEOs and their executive teams, functional leadership teams, newly formed teams after a reorganization or merger, private equity portfolio management teams, clinical and operational leadership groups in health systems, and cross-functional program teams. Common triggers include a new leader forming a team, a team integrating new members, visible tension or misalignment, a demanding strategy that requires joint ownership, or feedback from employees that leaders send mixed messages.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Teams at the top of an organization set the conditions for everyone else. When leadership teams align on priorities, make clear decisions and model respectful disagreement, employees face fewer conflicting demands and more coherent direction. Psychological safety within teams supports learning and helps people raise concerns early, which protects customers and, in fields like healthcare, patient safety. Effective cross-functional teams also deliver better experiences for customers whose needs span organizational boundaries.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What makes a team effective?

Research on team effectiveness consistently points to a few conditions: a compelling shared purpose, the right membership, clear roles and decision rights, sound norms for how the team works, a supportive organizational context and psychological safety. Teams that have these conditions in place tend to perform better. Our team effectiveness work diagnoses which conditions are weak and helps the team strengthen them.

How is this different from team building?

Team building events typically focus on relationships and goodwill and are often disconnected from the team's actual work. Team effectiveness work addresses purpose, decision rights, meeting design and how the team handles real issues, alongside trust and relationships. We work on the team's live agenda so improvements carry directly into how the team operates.

How do you help a leadership team that does not trust each other?

Low trust often has specific causes, such as unclear decision rights, competing incentives, past conflicts or inconsistent behavior. We identify those causes through confidential interviews, then help the team address them directly in facilitated conversations. Trust builds when members make and keep explicit commitments to each other, which we help the team practice and review over time.

How long does a team effectiveness engagement take?

A typical engagement runs several months, beginning with a diagnostic, followed by a series of working sessions and periodic follow-up. Some teams start with a single offsite and then continue with quarterly sessions. Sustained work over time produces more durable change than a single event, because new practices need repetition to become habits.
