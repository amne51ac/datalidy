Behavioral Change Management

Behavioral change management treats every transformation as a set of specific behaviors that people must start, stop or do differently. We identify those behaviors, diagnose what blocks them, design interventions and measure adoption in real time.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most change programs rely on communication and training. Leaders announce the new system, process or structure, people attend sessions, and the organization assumes behavior will follow. Often it does not. Employees revert to familiar workarounds, adoption stalls at the enthusiasts, and the business case quietly erodes. The problem is not resistance in the abstract. It is that the new behavior is harder, less rewarding or less prompted than the old one, and nobody designed for that.

Change efforts also tend to measure activity rather than behavior: emails sent, sessions delivered, licenses provisioned. These measures say little about whether people are actually working differently. By the time lagging outcomes reveal the problem, momentum is lost and skepticism has grown. Behavioral change management starts from the behaviors themselves, uses established models to understand barriers, and tracks early signals of adoption so leaders can intervene while there is still time.

Our approach

How we deliver behavioral change management.

  1. 01Observe

    Specify the target behaviors

    We work with leaders to translate the change into concrete behaviors for each affected group: who needs to do what, when and how often. Contextual interviews and observation reveal the current workflow, the workarounds people rely on and the informal leaders who shape how colleagues respond. This replaces vague goals with a clear picture of what must change.

  2. 02Signal

    Diagnose barriers and adoption signals

    Using the COM-B model, we assess whether each behavior is blocked by capability, opportunity or motivation. We analyze system logs, workflow data and surveys to find early adoption signals and behavioral segments, from early adopters to quiet holdouts. These become predictive KPIs that show whether change is taking hold long before final outcomes arrive.

  3. 03Design

    Design for the barrier

    We design interventions matched to each barrier: simplifying steps and removing friction where ability is the issue, adjusting incentives, recognition and social proof where motivation is weak, and adding timely prompts in the flow of work. Leaders and managers receive coaching on the behaviors they must model, since people watch what leaders do more closely than what they say.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot before broad rollout

    We test interventions with pilot groups, comparing adoption signals against similar groups where possible. Short feedback cycles show which prompts, supports and messages actually change behavior. We refine the approach before full rollout, reducing the risk of scaling a change plan that looks reasonable on paper but fails in practice.

  5. 05Scale

    Sustain the new behaviors

    We support rollout across the organization, equip managers with dashboards that track adoption signals, and coach leaders to reinforce new habits. Change agents and peer networks help spread practices. We plan for the predictable dip after launch, so that support continues until the new behaviors are routine and the old ways are no longer easier.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We support chief executives, transformation and operations leaders, chief information officers and human resources leaders who are implementing new technology, restructuring, integrating acquisitions or changing core processes. Typical clients include health systems introducing new clinical workflows, financial services firms adopting new platforms, and growing companies formalizing processes. The trigger is often a stalled implementation, low adoption after a costly launch, or a leadership team determined not to repeat a past change effort that faded.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Poorly managed change is exhausting for employees, who absorb the confusion, extra workload and repeated reversals. Behavioral change management respects people by making new ways of working genuinely easier and by listening to the barriers they face. When change succeeds, customers and patients benefit from improved processes and technology. In public-facing and health organizations, successful adoption of better practices can directly improve service, safety and access for the communities they serve.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How is behavioral change management different from traditional change management?

Traditional change management often focuses on stakeholder communication, training and readiness assessments. Behavioral change management keeps those elements but starts from specific behaviors and uses behavioral science to understand why people do or do not adopt them. It designs interventions for the actual barriers, tests them in pilots, and measures adoption through behavioral signals rather than activity counts.

What is the COM-B model?

COM-B is a behavior change framework that holds that behavior depends on capability, opportunity and motivation. Capability includes knowledge and skills. Opportunity includes physical and social factors in the environment. Motivation includes both reflective intentions and automatic habits and emotions. Diagnosing which component is weak for a given behavior helps us select interventions that fit, instead of defaulting to more training.

How do you measure adoption during a change?

We define leading indicators tied to the target behaviors, such as frequency of use of a new feature, completion of a new process step, or quality of handoffs. These often come from system logs and workflow data, supplemented by short pulse surveys. Segmenting by behavior shows who has adopted, who is struggling and who has not started, so support can be targeted.

Does this approach work for technology implementations?

Yes, technology adoption is one of the most common applications. New systems usually change many small daily behaviors, and adoption often fails because the new tool adds steps or disrupts familiar routines. We combine behavioral change management with usability insight to reduce friction, design prompts in the workflow and give managers clear signals about adoption progress.
