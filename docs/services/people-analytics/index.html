The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most organizations hold more people data than ever, across applicant tracking, HRIS, payroll, learning, performance and engagement systems. Yet many HR teams still spend most of their analytics effort producing descriptive reports: headcount, turnover rates, time to fill. Those reports rarely answer the questions leaders actually ask. Why are we losing high performers in one region but not another? Does our manager training change anything? Which roles limit our growth plan? Data sits in disconnected systems with inconsistent definitions, and analysts lack time or statistical depth to go further.

At the same time, the stakes for doing people analytics well have risen. Boards and investors ask for human capital metrics. Regulators in several jurisdictions expect transparency on pay and workforce composition. Employees are more aware of how their data is used. Poorly designed analysis can mislead leaders or erode trust, for example by treating correlation as causation or exposing small groups. Effective people analytics consulting brings statistical rigor, business judgment and ethical discipline together around a clear set of decisions.

Our approach

How we deliver people analytics.

  1. 01Listen

    Define the questions that matter

    We interview executives, HR leaders, finance partners and line managers to identify the decisions where better workforce evidence would change outcomes. We prioritize questions by business value, feasibility and ethical sensitivity. We also listen to employees, through focus groups or existing feedback, to understand what they consider fair and acceptable uses of their data.

  2. 02Measure

    Assemble and analyze the evidence

    We integrate data across HR, finance and operational systems, resolve definitional inconsistencies, and assess data quality. Analyses are matched to the question: regression and multilevel models to isolate drivers, quasi-experimental designs to evaluate programs, survival analysis for tenure and attrition, and text analytics for open comments. We report effect sizes and uncertainty honestly, not just statistical significance.

  3. 03Design

    Translate findings into choices

    We work with leaders to turn analysis into practical options: changes to hiring criteria, manager development priorities, scheduling practices, or program investments. Each option is framed with its expected impact, risks and the evidence behind it. Where findings are uncertain, we design small tests to learn more before committing to large changes.

  4. 04Deliver

    Build the analytics function

    We help design or strengthen the people analytics function itself: operating model, roles and skills, intake and prioritization process, technology choices and partnerships with finance and IT. We work alongside internal analysts on live projects, transferring methods, code and documentation, so capability grows through real work rather than classroom training alone.

  5. 05Sustain

    Govern and keep improving

    We help establish people data governance covering definitions, access, privacy, model review and ethical use, aligned to applicable regulations. We set up a measurement agenda with a small number of leading and lagging indicators, a cadence for revisiting priority questions, and a roadmap for advancing analytical maturity as data and skills improve.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

People analytics, used responsibly, makes organizations fairer and better run. It can reveal where some groups face barriers to hiring, advancement or pay, show which practices actually support employees, and redirect investment toward what works. Better workforce decisions mean steadier staffing and better service for customers. Strong governance protects employees' privacy and dignity. When findings are shared transparently, employees gain insight into how decisions are made, which strengthens trust between people and the institutions they work for.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does a people analytics consultant do?

A people analytics consultant helps organizations use workforce data to make better decisions. That includes defining priority questions, integrating and cleaning data from HR and business systems, conducting statistical analysis, translating findings into recommendations, and building the governance and capability to sustain the work. Good people analytics consulting focuses on decisions and outcomes rather than reports, and treats ethics and privacy as part of the method.

What data do we need to get started?

Most organizations can start with data they already have: HRIS records of hires, moves and exits, compensation data, performance ratings, and engagement survey results. Linking these to operational or financial data, such as productivity, sales or service quality, adds significant value. Data rarely needs to be perfect to begin. We assess quality early, identify gaps that matter for priority questions, and improve data foundations alongside early analysis.

How do you protect employee privacy?

We apply data minimization, collecting only what is needed for a defined purpose, and we use pseudonymization, role-based access, and minimum group sizes for any reporting that could identify individuals. We document lawful bases and retention rules in line with applicable regulations, and we review analyses for potential harm before results are shared. We also recommend clear communication with employees about how their data is used.

How do we know if our people analytics function is mature?

Maturity typically progresses from operational reporting to advanced analysis, then to predictive and prescriptive insight, with governance and business partnership growing alongside. Signs of maturity include consistent definitions, analysts who spend time on questions rather than data cleaning, findings that regularly change decisions, and executive demand for analysis. We assess maturity across data, technology, skills, governance and influence, and build a realistic roadmap.
