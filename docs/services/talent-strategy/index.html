Talent Strategy

Our talent strategy consulting helps leaders define the capabilities their strategy requires, build an employee value proposition people believe, and design hiring, development, mobility and retention practices that deliver the right talent.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Many organizations manage talent through a collection of HR programs, recruiting campaigns, training catalogs, performance processes and compensation reviews, each managed separately and rarely connected to strategy. When the business shifts, such as entering new markets, adopting new technology or changing its operating model, talent practices lag behind. Critical roles remain vacant, skills gaps grow, high performers leave for clearer opportunities elsewhere and costly external hiring becomes the default response to every capability need.

Employees experience the gap directly. The promises made in recruitment do not match the reality of the job. Career paths are unclear, internal moves are harder than external ones, and development depends on which manager you happen to have. Groups of employees may face different barriers to advancement. Talent strategy consulting must connect the business need for capabilities with what employees need to join, grow and stay, using evidence rather than assumptions about what people value.

Our approach

How we deliver talent strategy.

  1. 01Listen

    Understanding what people value

    We interview leaders about strategic capability needs and conduct focus groups and interviews with employees across levels, including recent hires and leavers, about why they joined, stay or leave. Candidate perspectives reveal how the organization is perceived in the talent market. This research forms the basis of an authentic employee value proposition.

  2. 02Measure

    Analyzing the talent system

    We analyze workforce data on hiring, internal mobility, promotion, performance, engagement, pay and attrition, disaggregated to identify differences across groups. We identify critical roles, those with outsized impact on strategy, and assess supply, risk and skills gaps. Labor market analysis shows competition for key skills and realistic expectations for building versus buying talent.

  3. 03Design

    Building the talent strategy

    We define the capabilities required by the strategy, a build, buy, borrow and redeploy approach for each critical capability and an employee value proposition grounded in research. We then design aligned practices, such as skills-based hiring, career architecture, an internal talent marketplace, leadership development and targeted retention, testing proposals with employees and managers.

  4. 04Deliver

    Implementing priority practices

    We support implementation of priority initiatives, including employer brand updates consistent with the EVP, career framework rollout, manager training on development conversations and internal mobility processes. We work with HR, finance and business leaders to align budgets and accountability, and use pilots to test practices before scaling them across the organization.

  5. 05Sustain

    Measuring talent outcomes

    We establish a talent dashboard tracking leading and lagging indicators such as critical role coverage, internal fill rate, quality of hire, regretted attrition, development participation and equity of outcomes. Regular talent reviews connect these measures to business planning, and periodic employee listening checks whether the value proposition matches lived experience.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Talent strategy determines who gets opportunity inside an organization. Employees gain clear career paths, fair access to development and a workplace that matches its promises. Customers benefit from capable, engaged employees who stay long enough to build expertise and relationships. Inclusive hiring and mobility practices can open opportunity to people historically excluded from good jobs. And a talent system aligned with strategy supports performance that sustains employment and investment.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is an employee value proposition?

An employee value proposition, or EVP, describes the total value employees receive in exchange for their work, including compensation, benefits, career growth, work environment, culture, purpose and flexibility. A strong EVP is grounded in research with current employees and candidates, differentiated from competitors for talent and honest about the experience. It guides recruitment messaging and helps prioritize investments in employee experience.

What is the difference between talent strategy and workforce planning?

Strategic workforce planning analyzes future workforce demand and supply, identifying gaps in numbers and skills over time. Talent strategy uses those insights, along with research on what people value, to decide how the organization will attract, develop, deploy and retain talent. Workforce planning answers what talent you need; talent strategy answers how you will secure and grow it.

What does build, buy, borrow mean in talent strategy?

It is a framework for closing capability gaps. Build means developing existing employees through training, experience and mobility. Buy means hiring externally. Borrow means using contractors, partners or temporary talent. Many organizations also consider redeployment, moving people from roles with declining demand to roles with growing demand, and automation. The right mix depends on time, cost, risk and the strategic importance of the capability.

How do you identify critical roles?

Critical roles are those with a disproportionate effect on strategy execution, customer outcomes or risk, not necessarily the most senior roles. We assess roles by strategic impact, performance variability, difficulty of replacement and supply in the labor market. Critical roles then receive focused attention in succession, development, retention and workforce planning, so the organization concentrates effort where it matters most.
