The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Investors and companies face a growing volume of ESG and impact data requests from limited partners, lenders, customers and regulators. Disclosure regimes and industry frameworks differ in scope and definitions, and portfolio companies with lean finance and HR teams often spend significant effort completing questionnaires that produce little insight. Environmental metrics have matured faster than social ones, leaving workforce, community and customer outcomes measured inconsistently or reduced to simple headcounts.

The deeper problem is that much ESG data is collected to satisfy external audiences rather than to improve decisions. Metrics are selected for availability, not relevance. Data quality is uneven across the portfolio, and few organizations can explain how their indicators connect to material risks or to the outcomes they claim. Investors who want measurement that is credible to outside stakeholders and useful to management need a framework grounded in materiality, sensible data practices and the perspectives of the people affected.

Our approach

How we deliver esg & impact measurement.

  1. 01Listen

    Clarify purpose and materiality

    We interview investment teams, portfolio leaders and, where relevant, employees and community stakeholders to understand what decisions measurement should inform and which issues are material. We consider both financial materiality and the impact on people and the environment, the perspective often described as double materiality.

  2. 02Measure

    Data audit and baselines

    We audit existing ESG data collection across the fund or company: what is collected, how it is defined, how reliable it is and who uses it. For social indicators such as turnover, pay equity, health and safety, training and employee voice, we establish baselines with clear definitions and documented methods.

  3. 03Design

    Proportionate measurement framework

    We design a core indicator set aligned to applicable frameworks and disclosure regimes, with sector-specific additions where material. Each indicator has a definition, owner, data source and collection frequency. We keep the burden proportionate to company size so that portfolio teams can report reliably without diverting attention from running the business.

  4. 04Deliver

    Roll out data collection and training

    We implement collection templates or platforms, train portfolio company contacts, and support the first reporting cycle. We help companies interpret their results and identify improvement priorities, so measurement leads to action rather than ending at submission. Early cycles surface data gaps and definitional issues, which we resolve with companies before they become recurring problems.

  5. 05Sustain

    Reporting, assurance readiness and review

    We build dashboards and report structures for boards, investment committees and limited partners, and prepare data processes for external assurance where required. An annual review refines indicators as frameworks, regulation and portfolio composition change. Changes are made deliberately, so year-over-year comparisons remain meaningful and reporting stays credible to limited partners and other stakeholders.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Measurement shapes attention. When investors and companies measure job quality, fair pay, safety and employee voice with care, those outcomes receive management attention and resources. Good customer and community indicators help companies notice when their products or operations cause harm or exclude people. Proportionate frameworks also respect the time of portfolio teams, many of whom are small. Credible, honest ESG and impact measurement builds trust with stakeholders and helps capital markets reward businesses that serve people well.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is the difference between ESG and impact measurement?

ESG measurement typically assesses how environmental, social and governance factors affect a company's risk and performance, and how the company manages them. Impact measurement focuses on the outcomes a company or investment produces for people and the planet, including whether those outcomes would have occurred anyway. Many investors need both, and a well-designed framework can serve both purposes with a shared core of indicators.

How do you measure the S in ESG?

Social measurement covers workforce, customers, supply chain and communities. For the workforce, useful indicators include turnover and retention, pay equity, health and safety, training, employee engagement and representation. For customers and communities, indicators depend on the business and might cover access, affordability, product safety or local employment. We combine quantitative data with structured employee and stakeholder input to capture what numbers alone miss.

What is double materiality?

Double materiality considers two perspectives: how sustainability issues affect a company's financial position, and how the company affects people and the environment. Some disclosure regimes require both perspectives. In practice, applying double materiality helps companies identify issues that may not yet affect financial results but matter significantly to employees, communities or ecosystems, and that may become financially material over time.

How do you reduce the ESG reporting burden on portfolio companies?

We start by eliminating indicators that no one uses, aligning definitions across frameworks so one data point can satisfy several requests, and drawing on data the company already holds in payroll, HR and finance systems. We tailor requirements to company size and sector. Clear guidance, templates and a single annual collection cycle also reduce the repeated ad hoc requests that frustrate portfolio teams.
