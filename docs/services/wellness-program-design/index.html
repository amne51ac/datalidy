Workplace Wellbeing Program Design

A workplace wellbeing program works when it addresses both how work is designed and the health behaviors employees want to build. We design, test and measure programs that employees actually use and that leaders can evaluate honestly.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Many workplace wellbeing programs are collections of benefits: an app subscription, a fitness challenge, a meditation webinar, an employee assistance program, each chosen separately. Participation is often concentrated among employees who were already healthy and engaged, while those under the most strain do not use the offerings. Programs can feel disconnected from daily reality, especially when workload, scheduling and management practices, rather than personal habits, are the main sources of stress.

Evaluation is another challenge. Organizations track enrollment and app downloads, but struggle to tell whether wellbeing, engagement or retention is improving because of the program. Survey fatigue, privacy concerns and fragmented vendor data make measurement harder. A well-designed workplace wellbeing program starts from a clear diagnosis of what is affecting employee health, combines organizational changes with individual supports, and defines measures that show whether behavior and experience are actually changing.

Our approach

How we deliver workplace wellbeing program design.

  1. 01Observe

    Understand employee realities

    We conduct interviews and focus groups across roles, shifts and locations, and review benefits use, absence and engagement data. We explore what employees value, what gets in the way of healthy routines at work, and which aspects of workload, scheduling and management most affect wellbeing. We look for groups whose needs current offerings miss.

  2. 02Signal

    Identify drivers and participation patterns

    We analyze participation and survey data to find behavioral types, such as enthusiastic early adopters, occasional users and non-participants, and the drivers that predict wellbeing and retention in this workforce. This reveals where to focus and defines predictive KPIs that go well beyond enrollment counts and app downloads.

  3. 03Design

    Combine work design and personal support

    We design a program that addresses organizational drivers, such as meeting load, recovery time, manager practices and flexibility, alongside individual supports for movement, sleep, stress and mental health. Behavioral design makes participation easy: defaults, well-timed prompts, social support and manager encouragement, with careful attention to privacy and voluntary participation.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot and measure

    We pilot program elements with selected teams or sites, measuring participation, behavior and experience against baseline and comparison groups where possible. Feedback from employees, including those who chose not to participate, shows what to refine. Testing prevents broad investment in offerings that look attractive but do not reach those who need them.

  5. 05Scale

    Integrate and sustain

    We help roll out effective components, integrate wellbeing into management practices and leadership development, and coordinate vendors around shared measures. A wellbeing dashboard tracks predictive KPIs while protecting individual privacy. Regular reviews with employee input keep the program responsive as the workforce, its needs and the organization change.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We work with chief people officers, total rewards and benefits leaders, wellbeing program managers and occupational health teams in employers of many kinds, including health systems, professional services firms, technology companies, associations and organizations with hourly or shift-based workforces. Typical triggers include low participation in existing programs, rising burnout or absence, a benefits redesign, contract renewal with wellbeing vendors, or leadership interest in demonstrating whether wellbeing investment is making a difference.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Employees spend a large share of their waking lives at work, and how work is organized affects their physical and mental health, relationships and sense of purpose. Thoughtful wellbeing programs can reduce strain and support healthier routines, especially for workers with less flexibility. Customers and patients benefit from rested, engaged teams. Programs that respect privacy and voluntary choice build trust, and healthier workforces contribute to healthier families and communities.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Why do workplace wellbeing programs often have low participation?

Common reasons include offerings that do not fit employees' schedules or needs, unclear value, privacy concerns, and programs that ask stressed people to add more tasks to their day. When workload or management practices drive stress, individual programs can also feel tone-deaf. Understanding what different groups need and making participation easy and voluntary helps reach more employees.

Should wellbeing programs focus on individuals or the organization?

Both. Individual supports for sleep, movement, stress and mental health are valuable, but they work best alongside organizational changes that reduce unnecessary strain, such as manageable workloads, predictable schedules, recovery time and supportive managers. We help organizations address both levels, so employees are not asked to cope individually with problems that originate in how work is designed.

How do you measure the impact of a workplace wellbeing program?

We define measures linked to the program's goals, such as participation among target groups, specific health behaviors, perceived wellbeing, burnout risk, engagement and retention. We establish baselines, use comparison groups where feasible and combine surveys with operational data. Measurement is designed to protect individual privacy, with reporting at group levels only.

How do you protect employee privacy in wellbeing programs?

Privacy is essential to trust. We design programs so participation is voluntary, health information is handled by appropriate parties under applicable regulations, and employers see only aggregated data. Communication explains clearly what is collected and why. We avoid designs that pressure employees to share health details or tie participation to penalties.
