Cross-Sell & Sales Process Design

A cross-sell strategy fails or succeeds in small moments: a banker deciding whether to introduce a colleague, a client deciding whether to take the meeting. We map those moments, measure them and redesign the process around them.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most firms with more than one line of business believe their clients should buy more than one thing from them. Leadership sets cross-sell targets, launches incentive plans and builds shared CRM fields. Yet the referrals stay thin. The reasons are rarely about effort. Producers worry that a colleague will mishandle a relationship they spent years building. Clients are not sure what the second service is or why it matters to them now. Credit for the outcome is ambiguous, so the safest choice is to do nothing.

Those are behavioral problems, and a cross-sell strategy that ignores them tends to produce activity without results. In firms with several service lines, for example, the decisive moments often sit in handoffs between teams, where accountability blurs and information gets lost. Until a firm can see which handoffs convert, which stall and why, incentive changes and training programs are educated guesses rather than interventions aimed at a known cause.

Our approach

How we deliver cross-sell & sales process design.

  1. 01Observe

    Shadow the real handoffs

    We interview producers, analysts and client-facing leaders across each line of business and walk through recent deals end to end. The goal is to see how referrals actually happen, including the informal ones, what people fear when they pass a client along, and which client moments open the door to a second conversation.

  2. 02Signal

    Measure stage conversion

    We assemble CRM records, pipeline history and deal data, often from systems that were never integrated, and calculate conversion at each sales stage and handoff. We look for behavioral types among producers and clients, and for the early signals, such as joint meetings or shared pitch materials, that predict whether a cross-sell opportunity closes.

  3. 03Design

    Redesign the process and prompts

    Using swimlane maps, we redesign the sales process so that ownership, timing and information transfer are explicit at every handoff. We design prompts at the moments clients are most receptive, address loss aversion among producers through shared credit rules, and draft talk tracks that explain the second service in the client's terms.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot with selected teams

    We pilot the redesigned process with a few teams or regions and compare them against a matched group. We track the predictive KPIs defined earlier, such as introduction rate and time from referral to first meeting, so leaders can see whether behavior is changing well before revenue figures confirm it.

  5. 05Scale

    Build it into the operating rhythm

    Successful changes are written into playbooks, CRM workflows and pipeline reviews. We coach sales leaders to run cross-sell conversations in their regular meetings and build a simple dashboard of leading indicators, so the process keeps improving after the engagement ends rather than fading with the next priority.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Good cross-selling serves clients first. When handoffs are clear, clients get coordinated advice instead of fragmented pitches and spend less time repeating their story to different teams. Employees benefit from a process that protects their relationships and recognizes collaboration fairly, which reduces internal friction and turf conflict. Firms that build trust this way earn deeper relationships honestly, and we deliberately avoid pressure tactics that sell products clients do not need.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Why do cross-sell programs so often underperform?

Most programs focus on targets and incentives while the real barriers are behavioral. Producers fear losing control of a relationship, credit rules are unclear, and clients do not see why the second service matters to them. Handoffs between teams also lose information. A cross-sell strategy that addresses those specific frictions, measured at each stage, tends to outperform one that simply raises the target.

What data do you need to start?

CRM opportunity records and pipeline history are the usual starting point, supplemented by deal or transaction data, billing records and calendar or meeting data where available. The data is rarely clean or integrated, and that is expected. Interviews and deal walkthroughs fill in what the systems miss, and part of our work is identifying which gaps matter enough to fix.

Do you redesign incentive and compensation plans?

We advise on the behavioral effects of incentives, such as how credit-sharing rules affect willingness to refer, and we work with your compensation and finance leaders on changes. We do not treat incentives as the only lever. Process clarity, social proof from respected peers and well-timed prompts often change referral behavior faster and at lower cost than compensation redesign.

How is this different from sales training?

Sales training improves individual skill. Cross-sell process design changes the system people work within: who owns each step, what information moves with the client, when prompts appear and what gets measured. Training can be part of the solution, but it works far better once the process supports the behavior you are training for.
