Customer Experience Research

Customer experience research explains the why behind your numbers. We use interviews, contextual inquiry, diary studies and well-designed surveys to understand how customers decide, what frustrates them and what would earn their continued loyalty.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most organizations know a great deal about what customers do and comparatively little about why. Satisfaction scores rise and fall, but the comments behind them are skimmed rather than analyzed. Research is often commissioned to confirm a decision already made, with leading questions and convenience samples that tell stakeholders what they hope to hear. Insights end up in a deck that describes customers in broad terms but offers no clear direction for product, service or marketing teams.

Meanwhile, the forces that shape customer behavior are often subtle: a moment of uncertainty during setup, a perceived lack of respect from a service representative, an expectation set by a competitor, an emotional need hidden beneath a functional request. Uncovering them requires research methods applied with rigor and humility, careful sampling, and analysis that separates individual anecdotes from consistent patterns. Customer experience research done well changes decisions, not just presentations.

Our approach

How we deliver customer experience research.

  1. 01Observe

    Study customers in context

    We design research around the decisions you need to make, then recruit carefully to include both satisfied and dissatisfied customers, recent defectors and prospects who chose a competitor. Methods include in-depth interviews, contextual inquiry at home or work, diary studies across weeks of use, and observation of service interactions.

  2. 02Signal

    Connect what people say to what they do

    We compare research findings with behavioral data, such as usage, purchase, service contacts and churn, to test whether stated reasons match observed behavior. Text analysis of open-ended feedback and support logs reveals recurring themes at scale. This triangulation identifies which insights are widespread and which outcomes they actually influence.

  3. 03Design

    Turn insight into direction

    We synthesize findings into clear insight statements, customer needs frameworks and behavioral types, each linked to specific design implications and opportunity hypotheses. Recommendations are prioritized by evidence strength and expected impact, so product, marketing and service teams know what to do first and why it matters.

  4. 04Test

    Validate with broader evidence

    Qualitative findings are validated through psychometrically sound surveys with representative samples, concept tests and early prototypes. We test proposed changes with customers before investment, and design measurement to confirm whether implemented improvements change satisfaction, loyalty and behavior in the way the research predicted, closing the loop between insight and result.

  5. 05Scale

    Build a listening system

    We help design an ongoing customer experience research program: regular interviews, continuous feedback analysis, a searchable insight repository and routines that bring customer evidence into leadership meetings. Training helps your teams conduct unbiased interviews and synthesize findings themselves, so customer understanding becomes continuous rather than episodic.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Rigorous research gives customers a genuine voice in decisions that affect them. Including people who are often underrepresented, such as those with disabilities, limited digital access or lower incomes, leads to experiences that work for more people. Frontline employees benefit when their observations are captured and taken seriously. We obtain informed consent, protect participant privacy and compensate people fairly for their time.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How many customers do you need to interview?

For qualitative research, the goal is to reach saturation, the point where new interviews stop producing new themes, within each meaningful group. That usually means a modest number of interviews per behavioral type or segment, chosen deliberately. Qualitative findings are then validated with larger surveys or behavioral data when you need to know how widespread a pattern is.

What is contextual inquiry?

Contextual inquiry is a research method in which we observe and talk with people while they perform real tasks in their own environment, such as at home, at work or in a showroom. It reveals workarounds, interruptions and environmental factors that people rarely mention in interviews because they have become routine. It is especially valuable for complex products and services.

How is this different from our existing customer satisfaction survey?

Satisfaction surveys measure how customers feel at a point in time, usually with limited explanation. Customer experience research investigates why, through deep qualitative methods, and connects findings to behavior. We can also improve your existing survey so it measures more reliably and relates to outcomes such as retention, making it a stronger part of the overall system.

Can you include customers who have already left?

Yes, and we strongly recommend it. Former customers and prospects who chose a competitor often provide the most candid and valuable insight. We use careful recruiting, neutral positioning and appropriate incentives to reach them. Their perspectives frequently reveal problems that current, satisfied customers have learned to tolerate or no longer notice at all.
