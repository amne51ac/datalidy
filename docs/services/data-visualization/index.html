Data Visualization & Decision Dashboards

Decision dashboards should help people decide, not just display data. We design visualizations around specific decisions, predictive KPIs and the way people actually perceive information, so leaders see what matters and what to do next.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Many organizations have more dashboards than decisions. Each team builds its own views, metrics are defined slightly differently across them, and leaders flip between screens dense with charts, gauges and color. The visualization tools are powerful, but design choices often work against human perception: too many colors, truncated axes, cluttered layouts and no indication of whether a change is meaningful or noise. Usage typically peaks at launch and then declines.

The deeper problem is that most dashboards are designed around available data rather than around decisions. They answer what happened, but not whether it matters or what to do about it. When a dashboard does not connect to a decision and a predictive measure, people revert to spreadsheets and intuition. Good decision dashboards begin with the user, the decision and the behavior they are meant to support, and only then turn to the data required to inform it.

Our approach

How we deliver data visualization & decision dashboards.

  1. 01Observe

    Study how decisions are made

    We observe meetings, interview decision makers and watch how people currently use reports, spreadsheets and dashboards. Think-aloud sessions with existing tools reveal where people misread charts, lose context or abandon the screen. We document the recurring decisions, their timing and the questions that must be answered for each.

  2. 02Signal

    Select the measures that matter

    Working from your KPI framework, we identify the predictive and lagging measures relevant to each decision, standardize definitions across teams, and determine how to represent uncertainty and normal variation. Where predictive KPIs do not yet exist, we flag the gap and link this work to KPI design.

  3. 03Design

    Design for perception and action

    We apply principles of visual perception, cognitive load and usability: appropriate chart types, consistent encodings, restrained color, clear hierarchy and annotations that explain change. Each view leads with the decision it supports, shows whether variation is meaningful, and points to the next action or drill-down.

  4. 04Test

    Usability test with real users

    Prototypes are tested with the people who will use them, using think-aloud protocols and task-based evaluation. We measure whether users can answer key questions accurately and quickly, identify misinterpretations, and iterate before engineering effort is committed. Accessibility checks ensure dashboards work for people with color vision differences and other needs.

  5. 05Scale

    Build, adopt and govern

    We work with your BI or engineering team to implement the dashboards in your existing platform, then support adoption through training, meeting redesign and usage tracking. A governance model keeps metric definitions consistent and prevents the gradual clutter that undermines most dashboard programs over time.

Leaders reach decisions faster because dashboards answer their specific questions directly.

Dashboard use persists after launch because the views are embedded in meetings and decisions.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Clear visualization makes evidence accessible to more people, not only analysts. Frontline staff can see how their work connects to outcomes, which supports engagement and fairness. Patients and communities benefit when health and program data are presented honestly, with uncertainty shown rather than hidden. We design for accessibility and resist visual choices that exaggerate or obscure, because misleading charts erode trust in data itself.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Do you build dashboards or only design them?

We do both, depending on your needs. Many clients have capable BI teams and want design, user research and KPI expertise, so we deliver tested prototypes and specifications for their implementation. Others want end-to-end support, in which case we build within your existing platform. Either way, we design for maintainability by your team after the engagement.

Which BI tools do you work with?

We work within the platforms clients already own, including common commercial business intelligence tools and custom web visualizations. Good decision design principles apply across tools. If your platform imposes limits that undermine the decisions you need to support, we will describe the trade-offs and suggest pragmatic workarounds before recommending any change of platform.

How do you show uncertainty without confusing executives?

We use simple, tested conventions: shaded ranges for expected variation, clear markers when a change exceeds normal noise, and plain-language annotations. Usability testing confirms that leaders interpret these correctly. Showing uncertainty well actually reduces confusion, because it prevents overreaction to random fluctuations and focuses attention on changes that genuinely deserve a response.

Why do usability testing for an internal dashboard?

Internal users are still users, and their misreadings can cost far more than a confused website visitor. A misinterpreted chart can redirect a budget or a staffing plan. A few short think-aloud sessions typically reveal significant problems that designers and analysts could not see because they already know what the data means.
