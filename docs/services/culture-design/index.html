High-Performance Culture Design

A high-performance culture is the sum of behaviors people repeat because the organization makes them easy, expected and rewarded. We help leaders define those behaviors, design the systems that reinforce them and measure whether culture is moving.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most organizations can recite their values, but few can say what those values look like on an ordinary Tuesday. Statements such as integrity, customer focus or ownership are open to interpretation, so each team quietly develops its own version. Meanwhile, the real culture is shaped by what gets promoted, funded, tolerated and celebrated. When those signals contradict the stated values, employees learn quickly which ones really count, and cynicism soon follows.

Culture work is often treated as a branding exercise or an offsite activity, disconnected from the systems that shape daily behavior: hiring criteria, performance reviews, meeting practices, decision processes and recognition. A high-performance culture cannot be declared. It must be designed into those systems and modeled by leaders, then measured over time. Without that, culture initiatives produce posters and slogans, while the behaviors that drive performance and wellbeing stay the same.

Our approach

How we deliver high-performance culture design.

  1. 01Observe

    Understand the culture as lived

    We conduct interviews, focus groups and observation across levels and locations, and review artifacts such as performance criteria, promotion decisions and meeting practices. We ask employees to describe the behaviors that actually get rewarded and the ones that get people in trouble. This reveals the gap between stated values and lived culture, with concrete examples.

  2. 02Signal

    Measure culture as behavior

    We design culture measures rooted in observable behaviors rather than abstract sentiment, using psychometrically sound survey items, network patterns and operational data where available. We identify which cultural behaviors predict outcomes such as retention, quality or customer satisfaction, so leaders know which aspects of culture most deserve investment.

  3. 03Design

    Translate values into behaviors and systems

    With leaders and employee representatives, we define each value as a few specific behaviors, including what it looks like at different levels. We then redesign the systems that shape those behaviors: hiring questions, onboarding, recognition, performance conversations, meeting norms and decision rights. Leaders commit to visible role-modeling, which matters more than any written statement.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot culture interventions

    We pilot new rituals, recognition practices or performance approaches in selected teams and compare behavioral measures with other groups. Employee feedback shows whether changes feel authentic or performative. Testing helps the organization avoid broad culture launches that look impressive but generate cynicism when daily experience does not match the message.

  5. 05Scale

    Embed and sustain

    We help leaders roll successful practices across the organization, build culture into leadership development and onboarding, and track culture indicators on a regular dashboard. We coach senior leaders on consistency, since a single high-profile exception can undo months of work. Culture becomes something managed with the same discipline as any other performance driver.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Culture shapes whether people feel respected, safe to raise concerns and able to do meaningful work. A well-designed high-performance culture supports both results and wellbeing, rather than trading one for the other. Customers and patients feel culture directly in how they are treated. Cultures that reward candor and learning also tend to catch ethical and safety problems earlier, protecting the communities an organization serves and the integrity of its mission.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Can culture really be designed?

Culture cannot be dictated, but it can be shaped deliberately. Culture emerges from repeated behaviors, and behaviors respond to what is easy, expected, modeled and rewarded. By designing the systems around people, including hiring, recognition, decision processes and leadership example, organizations can make desired behaviors more likely. Measurement then shows whether the culture is actually moving in the intended direction.

What makes a culture high-performance?

A high-performance culture combines high standards with high support. People know what excellent work looks like, hold each other accountable, speak up about problems and learn from mistakes. Psychological safety and accountability reinforce each other rather than compete. The specific behaviors vary by organization and strategy, which is why we define them through research rather than adopting a generic list.

How do you measure culture?

We measure culture through observable behaviors rather than general sentiment. Survey items describe specific actions, such as whether colleagues raise concerns in meetings or share credit, and are tested for reliability. Where possible we add operational signals and qualitative evidence. We also analyze which cultural behaviors predict outcomes such as retention or quality, so leaders focus on what matters most.

How long does culture change take?

Meaningful culture change usually unfolds over one to three years, although early shifts in specific behaviors can appear within months when systems and leadership behavior change together. We structure engagements in phases, with a diagnostic and design period followed by pilots and staged rollout, and regular measurement so leaders can see progress and adjust.
