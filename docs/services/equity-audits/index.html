Equity Audits & DEI Analytics

Our equity audit work examines hiring, promotion, performance, pay and employee experience data to identify where outcomes differ across groups and which policies and practices drive those differences, so leaders can act on evidence.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Organizations make commitments to fairness and inclusion, but many lack the evidence to know whether their practices produce equitable outcomes. Representation counts show who is in the organization, not why. Leaders cannot tell whether differences in promotion rates reflect qualifications, access to opportunity or bias in evaluation. Programs are launched without baselines and are hard to evaluate. Meanwhile the legal and political environment around diversity, equity and inclusion has become more complex, and leaders need approaches that are lawful, defensible and focused on fair processes for everyone.

An equity audit addresses these issues by examining the employee lifecycle as a system. Where in the hiring funnel do candidates from different groups drop out? Are performance ratings distributed differently, and do ratings predict outcomes equally well? Who gets stretch assignments, sponsorship and access to flexible work? Answering these questions requires careful statistics, sound handling of sensitive demographic data, qualitative insight into how processes actually work, and recommendations focused on practices and systems rather than on individuals.

Our approach

How we deliver equity audits & dei analytics.

  1. 01Listen

    Understand processes and experiences

    We interview HR process owners, managers and employees across groups to understand how hiring, evaluation, promotion and assignment decisions are actually made. Focus groups and interviews surface experiences that data alone cannot show, such as informal sponsorship or access to information. We work with legal counsel to confirm the audit's scope and lawful use of demographic data.

  2. 02Measure

    Analyze outcomes across the lifecycle

    We analyze adverse impact at each stage of the hiring funnel, promotion and exit rates, performance rating distributions, pay outcomes and survey results by demographic group, using appropriate statistical tests and controls. Intersectional analysis is applied where sample sizes allow. Where data is incomplete, we document limitations and recommend improvements to self-identification processes.

  3. 03Design

    Identify root causes and redesign practices

    We trace outcome differences to specific practices, such as unstructured interviews, vague performance criteria, informal promotion nominations or uneven access to development. We design improvements grounded in evidence: structured interviews and scoring rubrics, calibration protocols, transparent promotion criteria and fair allocation of opportunities. Redesigns are tested with managers and employees for practicality.

  4. 04Deliver

    Implement changes with managers

    We support implementation of redesigned processes, including manager training, updated guidance, and changes to HR systems and workflows. We help leaders communicate findings and actions appropriately to employees and boards. Changes are piloted where possible, with outcome measures defined in advance so leaders can see whether new practices are reducing unexplained differences.

  5. 05Sustain

    Monitor equity as an ongoing discipline

    We build equity measures into regular people reporting, with dashboards that track outcomes at each lifecycle stage while protecting small groups through minimum reporting thresholds. We establish governance for demographic data use, recurring audit cadence and accountability for process owners, so that equity becomes part of routine management rather than a periodic initiative.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Equity audits help organizations live up to the principle that opportunity should depend on merit and effort, not background. Fairer hiring and advancement processes widen access to good jobs and leadership roles, with effects that extend to families and communities. Consistent, transparent processes benefit all employees, who gain clarity about how decisions are made. For customers and communities, organizations whose workforce reflects and understands them are often better able to serve them well.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is an equity audit?

An equity audit is a systematic review of an organization's people data, policies and practices to determine whether outcomes such as hiring, promotion, pay, performance ratings and retention differ across demographic groups, and why. It combines statistical analysis with interviews and process review to identify root causes. The goal is to find and fix practices that produce unfair outcomes, using evidence rather than assumptions.

What is adverse impact analysis?

Adverse impact analysis tests whether a selection process, such as a hiring screen or promotion decision, results in substantially different selection rates for different groups. Common approaches include the four-fifths rule of thumb and statistical significance tests. Adverse impact does not on its own prove discrimination, but it signals that a practice deserves closer examination to confirm it is job-related and to consider fairer alternatives.

How do you handle demographic data responsibly?

We use demographic data only for defined purposes such as auditing fairness, with access restricted to a small analytics team. We apply minimum group sizes in reporting to avoid identifying individuals and suppress small intersectional cells. We work with legal counsel on lawful use in each jurisdiction and recommend voluntary, well-explained self-identification processes so employees understand why data is collected and how it is protected.

Is an equity audit the same as a DEI program?

No. An equity audit is a diagnostic. It examines whether existing processes produce fair outcomes and identifies what to change. DEI programs are interventions, such as training, mentoring or employee groups. An audit helps leaders decide which interventions are warranted and evaluate whether existing ones are working. Many of the most effective changes it identifies involve redesigning core talent processes rather than adding new programs.
