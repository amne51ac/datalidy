Leadership & Executive Coaching

Leadership for High-Growth Companies

Leadership for high-growth companies requires leaders to reinvent their roles every time the organization doubles. We coach founders and executive teams through those transitions, building the delegation, structure and team habits that let growth continue.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

In a fast-growing company, the behaviors that created early success often become constraints. Founders who made every decision now slow the organization. Informal communication that worked for a small team fails as new people join. Early employees promoted into leadership roles struggle to manage teams they once worked alongside. The organization outgrows its leadership habits faster than anyone can adapt, and the symptoms appear as missed targets, burnout and the departure of valued people.

Growth also brings investors, boards and new executives with different expectations. Founders must learn to lead through others, build a leadership team they trust, and let go of work they love without losing the culture and product insight that made the company distinctive. These transitions are predictable, but each feels new to the people living through it. Leadership for high-growth companies requires preparing for the next stage before the current one breaks.

Our approach

How we deliver leadership for high-growth companies.

  1. 01Observe

    Read the growth stage

    We interview founders, executives, board members and a cross-section of employees, and review organizational structure, decision flow and hiring plans. We identify where the company sits on its growth path and which leadership habits are already becoming bottlenecks. Founders often recognize the problems but have not seen how they connect.

  2. 02Signal

    Spot the early warnings

    We look for signals that leadership capacity is falling behind growth: decision queues around one person, rising cycle times, regretted attrition, engagement dips in specific teams, or customer issues that cross functional boundaries. These leading indicators help leaders anticipate problems instead of reacting after a key person leaves or a critical launch slips.

  3. 03Design

    Design the next-stage leadership model

    We help founders and executives define their evolving roles, clarify decision rights and design the structure, rhythm and leadership layers the next stage requires. This includes hiring profiles for key roles, onboarding plans for new executives and individual coaching plans for leaders whose roles are changing faster than their habits.

  4. 04Test

    Try the new model in practice

    We help leaders experiment with delegation, new meeting structures and decision processes over defined periods, then review together what actually happened. Pulse feedback from teams shows whether changes are reducing friction or simply moving it. Adjustments are made quickly, matching the pace of a fast-growing organization.

  5. 05Scale

    Grow leaders ahead of the curve

    We build leadership capability in the layers below the executive team through team coaching, first-time manager development and shared management practices. Founders receive continuing coaching as their roles evolve. The goal is an organization where leadership capacity grows slightly ahead of the business rather than perpetually trailing it.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We work with founders, chief executives and leadership teams in venture-backed startups, scaling technology and SaaS companies, fast-growing professional and knowledge services firms, digital health companies and private equity portfolio companies. Typical triggers include a significant funding round, rapid headcount growth, hiring a first professional executive, a founder stepping into a new role, board pressure to strengthen the leadership team, or early signs of burnout and turnover among key people.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Growth that outpaces leadership is hard on people. Employees face shifting priorities, unclear roles and overloaded managers, and early team members can burn out. Thoughtful scaling protects their wellbeing while creating new opportunities for advancement. Customers benefit from a company that can keep its promises as it grows. When growing companies are building products for health, education or financial wellbeing, stronger leadership helps their social impact reach more people.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

When should a founder start working with a leadership coach?

Ideally before a major transition rather than after a crisis. Good moments include right after a significant funding round, when hiring the first senior executives, or when the founder notices they have become the bottleneck for decisions. Early coaching helps founders anticipate predictable challenges, but coaching is also valuable mid-transition, when the pressure is most visible.

How do you help founders learn to delegate?

We treat delegation as a set of behaviors, not a personality trait. Founders identify which decisions and tasks to hand off, define clear outcomes and boundaries, choose appropriate check-in points and resist the urge to take work back. We use small experiments, starting with lower-risk decisions, and gather feedback from the people receiving the delegated work to refine the approach.

Do you work with boards and investors?

Yes, with the leader's knowledge and agreement. Boards and investors are important stakeholders in a growing company's leadership development, and their perspective often helps clarify expectations. We contract carefully so that confidentiality is respected, and we can support conversations between founders and boards about leadership roles, succession and executive hiring.

How is this different from general executive coaching?

It includes executive coaching, but it also addresses the organizational design questions that growth raises: structure, decision rights, leadership layers and executive hiring. We focus on the specific transitions high-growth companies face and draw on direct experience founding and building companies. The work typically spans the founder, the executive team and the next layer of managers.
