Community Behavioral Health Advisory

Our community behavioral health consulting helps mental health and substance use providers reduce the barriers between people and care, from first contact to ongoing treatment, using behavioral science, service design and honest measurement.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Community behavioral health organizations serve people at difficult moments, often with limited funding, staffing shortages and complex reimbursement rules. Many people who reach out never complete intake. Others miss early appointments and disengage before treatment can help. Referrals from schools, primary care and hospitals are lost in transition. Staff work hard to fill gaps, but the system itself makes it difficult for people in distress to take each necessary step.

These are behavioral and design problems as much as resource problems. Stigma, fear, competing responsibilities and confusing processes all stand in the way of care. Waiting periods weaken motivation at the very moment it is highest. Clinicians and care coordinators face burnout that affects engagement. Our roots in clinical and health psychology and behavioral medicine shape how we approach this work, with respect for clinical judgment, lived experience and the realities of community settings.

Our approach

How we deliver community behavioral health advisory.

  1. 01Observe

    Walk the path to care

    We interview people who have sought care, family members, peer specialists, clinicians, intake staff and referral partners. We map the path from first contact through intake and early treatment, noting where people hesitate, wait, feel confused or feel judged. Lived experience is central to this research, and we handle it with care and confidentiality.

  2. 02Signal

    Find where people drop off

    Using intake, scheduling, electronic health record and referral data, we measure conversion from inquiry to intake, intake to first session and early session attendance. We identify the drop-off points and the early behaviors that predict ongoing engagement, and we look for differences across populations that may indicate equity gaps in access.

  3. 03Design

    Redesign access and engagement

    We co-design improvements with staff and people with lived experience: simplified intake, warm handoffs from referral partners, reminders and outreach timed to reduce missed appointments, bridging support during waits, and language that reduces stigma. Service blueprints clarify roles so improvements fit staff capacity rather than adding to burnout.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot practical changes

    We pilot changes in a single program or site, measuring effects on intake completion, attendance and experience. We monitor staff workload as well, since changes that exhaust teams will not last. Results guide refinement, and we document what works in forms that are useful for grant reporting and funder conversations.

  5. 05Scale

    Sustain and share

    We help leadership expand successful changes, build access and engagement dashboards, and coach supervisors to maintain improvements. We also help organizations communicate results to funders, partners and county or state agencies, strengthening the case for sustained investment in community mental health. Lessons are documented so peer organizations in the region can adapt them for their own communities.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We work with executive directors, clinical and operations leaders, quality improvement teams and boards of community mental health centers, substance use treatment providers, certified community behavioral health clinics, school-based mental health programs and the agencies and foundations that fund them. Triggers include long waitlists, high no-show rates, a new funding or certification requirement, a crisis services expansion, or partnerships with schools and primary care.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Every step removed from the path to care can mean someone receives help sooner. Better access and engagement in community behavioral health can affect wellbeing, family stability, school success and safety. Staff benefit from processes that reduce frustration and burnout. We design with people who have lived experience, protect privacy rigorously and avoid approaches that pressure or stigmatize. Behavioral science here must serve autonomy and dignity first.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does community behavioral health consulting involve?

It typically involves research with clients, staff and referral partners, analysis of access and engagement data, co-design of process improvements, pilot testing and support for sustained implementation. The focus is on reducing barriers to care and improving engagement while respecting clinical expertise, staff capacity and the dignity of the people being served.

Do you provide clinical services?

No. We are advisors, not a treatment provider. Our background in clinical and health psychology and behavioral medicine informs our work, but clinical decisions remain with your licensed professionals. We focus on access, engagement, processes, communication and measurement around clinical care. We collaborate closely with clinical leaders so every recommendation fits clinical standards, supervision structures and the needs of the people in care.

How do you protect client privacy?

We work within your privacy and compliance policies and applicable regulations, use de-identified data whenever possible and limit access to sensitive information. Interviews with people who have used services are voluntary, confidential and conducted with informed consent, with attention to trauma-informed practice. Participants can decline or stop at any time without any effect on their care.

Can this help with no-show rates?

Often, yes. Missed appointments usually reflect practical barriers, ambivalence or long waits rather than lack of interest. We identify the causes in your setting and test interventions such as well-timed reminders, shorter waits for first appointments, bridging contact and simpler rescheduling, measuring which ones actually improve attendance. Each improvement we test is judged by whether more people actually receive the help they sought.
