Community Co-Design

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Institutions that serve communities, from public agencies and health systems to foundations, developers and employers, increasingly recognize that decisions made without community input often fail. Programs go underused, new facilities sit in the wrong place, and well-funded initiatives provoke opposition. Many respond with consultation: a public meeting, a survey, a comment period. But consultation typically asks people to react to decisions already largely made, reaches those with the most time and confidence to participate, and leaves communities feeling heard but not influential.

Genuine co-design is harder. It requires sharing power over decisions, compensating people for their time and expertise, working in accessible languages and locations, and being honest about constraints. It also requires institutions to handle disagreement and to be accountable for what they do with community input. Without those elements, participatory processes can deepen mistrust, especially in communities with long histories of extractive research or broken promises. Effective community co-design treats community members as partners and builds the relationships needed to sustain that partnership.

Our approach

How we deliver community co-design.

  1. 01Listen

    Build relationships before projects

    We start by understanding the community's history with the institution and the issue, including past engagement and its outcomes. We work with trusted local organizations and community connectors to listen through conversations, gatherings and visits in familiar settings. We clarify what is genuinely open for community influence and what is fixed, so that participation is honest from the start.

  2. 02Measure

    Understand the community with data

    We assemble relevant data on demographics, needs, service use and outcomes, and share it with community partners in accessible formats so they can interpret it too. Community members often identify gaps in official data and add knowledge it cannot capture. This shared fact base helps groups focus on root causes and ensures the experiences of smaller or less-visible groups are considered.

  3. 03Design

    Co-design with a representative group

    We convene a co-design group that reflects the community affected, with compensation, childcare, interpretation, accessible venues and flexible times. Using participatory methods such as community mapping, storytelling, scenario testing and prototyping, members work alongside institutional staff to generate and refine options. Decision rights are explicit, and the group shapes recommendations rather than simply commenting on them.

  4. 04Deliver

    Implement with community oversight

    We help institutions translate co-designed recommendations into plans, budgets and operational changes, and we support community members in reviewing implementation. Where recommendations cannot be adopted, we help leaders explain why. We test changes through pilots with community feedback, so adjustments are made before full rollout and community partners can see their influence.

  5. 05Sustain

    Close the loop and keep partnership going

    We report back to the wider community on what was heard, what changed and what did not. We help institutions establish standing mechanisms, such as community advisory boards with real authority, compensated feedback panels or participatory budgeting, and measures of community trust and outcomes. These structures keep community voice in decisions beyond a single project.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Community co-design is a direct expression of social good: it gives people affected by decisions a meaningful role in making them. Compensating participants recognizes their expertise and removes a barrier for people who cannot afford to volunteer time. Institutions gain solutions that work better and relationships that make future work easier. Employees who participate alongside community members often gain a deeper understanding of the people they serve, which improves everyday service long after a project ends.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is community co-design?

Community co-design is a participatory approach in which community members work alongside institutions as partners to design programs, services, policies or places that affect them. Unlike consultation, which asks people to react to proposals, co-design involves community members in defining the problem, generating options and shaping decisions. It requires shared decision rights, fair compensation, accessible processes and accountability for what happens with community input.

How is co-design different from community engagement?

Community engagement is a broad term covering many activities, from information sharing and surveys to deep partnership. Co-design sits toward the shared-power end of that range. Participants are not only asked for opinions, they help make decisions and develop solutions. Many institutions benefit from both: broad engagement to hear from many people and co-design with a smaller representative group to shape specific recommendations.

Should community members be paid to participate?

We recommend it. Community members contribute expertise about their own lives and neighborhoods, and their time has value. Compensation also makes participation possible for people who cannot afford to volunteer, which improves representation. We help clients set fair rates, choose payment methods that do not create problems for people receiving public benefits, and cover practical costs such as childcare, transportation and meals.

What if the community wants something we cannot deliver?

That is why we define constraints and decision rights at the start. Being clear about what is fixed, such as budget limits or legal requirements, and what is open helps keep co-design honest. When a community recommendation cannot be adopted, leaders should explain why and what alternatives are possible. Transparent disagreement builds more trust than vague promises, and it often leads to creative solutions within real constraints.
