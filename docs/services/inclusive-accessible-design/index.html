Inclusive & Accessible Design

Our inclusive and accessible design consulting helps organizations meet WCAG and legal obligations while designing services, products and workplaces that work for people with disabilities, older adults and others too often designed out.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Accessibility is often treated as a late-stage compliance check. A product or service is designed, built and launched, then audited against WCAG success criteria and patched where it fails. That approach is expensive, leaves gaps, and misses barriers that automated tools cannot detect, such as confusing content, inaccessible phone trees, forms that time out, or in-person processes that assume everyone can stand in line, read small print or hear announcements. Legal risk is real, and so is the quieter cost of customers and employees who simply give up.

Inclusion extends beyond disability. Language, literacy, age, digital access, caregiving responsibilities, neurodivergence and cultural context all shape whether people can use a service. Organizations often design for a default user who looks like their design team, then wonder why uptake is low among the people they most want to reach. Effective accessible design consulting addresses both: rigorous conformance with recognized standards, and design practices that include people with diverse needs from the beginning.

Our approach

How we deliver inclusive & accessible design.

  1. 01Listen

    Research with people who face barriers

    We conduct research with people with disabilities, including users of screen readers, magnification, voice control and switch access, as well as older adults, people with limited literacy or English proficiency, and others who often face exclusion. We observe real use of products, services and spaces, and we interview employees with disabilities about workplace tools and processes. Participants are compensated and sessions are accessible.

  2. 02Measure

    Audit against standards and outcomes

    We audit digital properties against WCAG 2.2 at the conformance level you require, combining automated scanning with manual expert review and assistive technology testing. We assess documents, communications, physical environments and service processes, and review relevant legal obligations with your counsel. We also look at data on usage, completion and complaints to see where exclusion shows up in outcomes.

  3. 03Design

    Design inclusively from the start

    We remediate priority barriers and redesign experiences using inclusive design principles: solve for one, extend to many; offer multiple ways to complete a task; use plain language. We co-design with people who have lived experience of exclusion, update design systems with accessible components and patterns, and test prototypes with assistive technology users before release.

  4. 04Deliver

    Embed accessibility in delivery

    We build accessibility into product and service development: acceptance criteria, testing in delivery pipelines, procurement requirements for vendors, and content guidelines. We train designers, developers, content writers and frontline staff on their specific responsibilities, including how to support customers with disability-related needs, and we help prioritize remediation backlogs by user impact and legal exposure.

  5. 05Sustain

    Govern accessibility as a program

    We help establish an accessibility policy, roles and an operating model, with a maturity roadmap, recurring audits and monitoring. We support accessibility statements and feedback mechanisms that let users report barriers and receive timely responses. Regular reporting to leadership covers conformance, remediation progress and user feedback, so accessibility becomes ongoing practice rather than a periodic project.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Accessible design is a matter of equal participation. When services, products and workplaces are accessible, people with disabilities can manage their finances, health, education and work on equal terms. Inclusive design often benefits everyone: captions help in noisy places, plain language helps people under stress, and flexible processes help caregivers. Accessible workplaces widen the talent pool and help employees stay in work as their needs change. For communities, accessible public services are a basic condition of fair access.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is WCAG and which level should we meet?

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines are the internationally recognized standard for digital accessibility, published by the World Wide Web Consortium. They are organized into conformance levels A, AA and AAA. Level AA is the most commonly referenced target in laws, regulations and procurement requirements. We recommend confirming your specific obligations with legal counsel, and we typically audit against WCAG 2.2 AA while addressing additional barriers users identify.

Are automated accessibility tools enough?

No. Automated tools are useful for catching certain issues quickly, such as missing alternative text or color contrast failures, but they detect only a portion of WCAG issues. Many barriers, including logical focus order, meaningful link text, understandable error messages and usable screen reader flows, require manual expert review and testing with assistive technologies. Testing with disabled users adds another layer by revealing real-world usability problems.

What is the difference between accessible design and inclusive design?

Accessible design focuses on removing barriers for people with disabilities and is often measured against standards such as WCAG. Inclusive design is broader, considering the full range of human diversity, including language, age, culture, literacy, income and circumstance, and involving excluded people in the design process. Our accessible design consulting addresses both: meeting standards reliably and designing experiences that work for a wider range of people from the start.

Does accessibility apply to services and workplaces, not just websites?

Yes. Accessibility applies to every way people interact with an organization: phone systems, documents, physical locations, customer service interactions, hiring processes and workplace tools. A conformant website does not help if the follow-up letter is unreadable or the in-person appointment is inaccessible. We look at the full service and employee experience, including reasonable accommodations processes, so that accessibility holds across channels.
