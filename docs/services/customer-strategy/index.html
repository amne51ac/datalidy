Customer Strategy

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Many organizations claim to be customer-centric but make decisions based on internal convenience, product features or historical segments. Customer research sits in separate pockets across marketing, product and service teams, rarely synthesized into a shared view. Segmentation is based on demographics or account size rather than needs and behaviors. As a result, organizations try to serve everyone equally, value propositions blur, and investments in channels or features are made without a clear understanding of what customers actually value.

Customer experience is also shaped by employees, yet customer strategy often ignores them. Frontline staff are asked to deliver a promised experience without the tools, authority, training or staffing to do so. Policies designed to control cost create friction that employees must explain to frustrated customers. The connection between employee experience and customer experience is well established in service research. Customer strategy consulting must account for the operating realities and people who determine whether customer promises are kept.

Our approach

How we deliver customer strategy.

  1. 01Listen

    Understanding customer needs in depth

    We conduct in-depth interviews, contextual observation and jobs-to-be-done research to understand what customers are trying to accomplish, the circumstances that trigger a purchase or service request, and the anxieties and trade-offs involved. Frontline employee interviews provide a parallel view of where customers struggle and what employees wish they could do differently.

  2. 02Measure

    Quantifying needs and value

    We validate qualitative insights through surveys and analysis of transaction, behavioral, complaint and retention data. Needs-based segmentation identifies distinct customer groups by the outcomes they seek, and customer lifetime value and cost-to-serve analysis show the economics of each segment. Journey analytics identify where customers drop off, complain or require repeated contact.

  3. 03Design

    Choosing where and how to win

    We help leadership decide which segments to prioritize, define a differentiated value proposition for each and identify the critical moments where the experience must excel. We design the customer experience principles, service standards and supporting employee experience, testing concepts with customers and frontline staff before committing resources.

  4. 04Deliver

    Aligning the organization

    We translate the customer strategy into initiatives across product, marketing, sales, service and operations, with clear owners and metrics. Frontline employees receive the training, decision authority and tools needed to deliver the promised experience. Policies and processes that create unnecessary friction are redesigned, and incentives are aligned with customer outcomes rather than activity volume.

  5. 05Sustain

    Listening continuously

    We set up a customer insight system that combines relationship and transactional feedback, operational data and frontline insight, with governance that routes findings to owners and tracks action. Customer and employee experience metrics are reviewed together in leadership routines, so the organization notices changing needs early and adjusts before customers leave.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

A thoughtful customer strategy treats customers as people with goals, constraints and dignity, not only as revenue. Customers gain products and services that fit their lives, with less friction and clearer communication. Employees gain the authority and support to solve problems rather than enforce rules they cannot explain. When strategy deliberately includes underserved customers, organizations can expand access and inclusion while building loyalty that supports durable financial performance.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is jobs-to-be-done?

Jobs-to-be-done is a framework for understanding customer behavior based on the progress people are trying to make in a particular circumstance, rather than their demographic characteristics or product features. It examines functional, emotional and social dimensions of the job, along with the forces that encourage or prevent switching. It helps organizations design offerings and experiences around what customers are actually trying to achieve.

What is the difference between customer strategy and customer experience?

Customer strategy determines which customers to serve, what value proposition to offer each and how the organization will differentiate. Customer experience concerns how customers perceive their interactions across the journey. Strategy sets direction for experience design; experience design and operations make the strategy real. Without strategy, experience investments are scattered. Without good experience delivery, strategy remains an aspiration.

How does employee experience affect customer experience?

Employees who have the tools, information, training, authority and workload to do their jobs well are better able to serve customers. Service research has long linked employee engagement and capability to customer satisfaction and loyalty. In practice, many customer pain points trace back to internal problems, such as policies, systems or staffing, that employees experience first and can often help solve.

What is needs-based segmentation?

Needs-based segmentation groups customers by the outcomes they seek and the circumstances they are in, rather than only by demographics, firmographics or spending. It typically combines qualitative research with survey and behavioral data to identify segments with distinct priorities. Because each segment values different things, it supports sharper value propositions, product decisions and experience design than traditional segmentation.
