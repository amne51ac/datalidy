Exit Readiness

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Buyers increasingly scrutinize human capital during diligence. They want to know whether the management team will stay and can scale, whether key person dependency has been reduced, whether compensation and incentives are sound, and whether the culture will survive another ownership change. Businesses that cannot answer these questions with clean data and a coherent story risk lower valuations, extended diligence, heavier earn-outs or retention demands that shift value away from sellers.

Exit preparation also creates its own people risks. Rumors of a sale unsettle employees, executives with significant equity may begin to disengage, and management bandwidth is consumed by the process while the business still needs to hit its numbers. Leaders who are critical to the equity story may not have retention arrangements that extend past close. Sponsors need to address these risks well ahead of launching a process, while there is still time to build bench strength and evidence of performance.

Our approach

How we deliver exit readiness.

  1. 01Listen

    See the business as a buyer will

    We interview the leadership team, board and selected managers to understand the equity story and how people contribute to it. We anticipate the questions buyers and their advisors are likely to ask about leadership, talent, culture and incentives, and identify where current answers are weak.

  2. 02Measure

    Vendor-side human capital review

    We conduct a sell-side human capital review: workforce data quality, attrition trends, compensation benchmarking, incentive and change of control arrangements, key person dependencies and leadership bench depth. This mirrors buyer diligence so issues are found and addressed by the seller first. Each issue is rated by buyer sensitivity.

  3. 03Design

    Close gaps and shape the narrative

    We prioritize fixes that can be completed before launch, such as strengthening the second layer of leadership, formalizing succession plans, cleaning HR data or rebalancing incentives. We help build a factual people narrative for the information memorandum and management presentation, grounded in metrics the business can substantiate.

  4. 04Deliver

    Prepare leaders and protect talent

    We prepare the management team for diligence sessions on organization and talent, design retention arrangements for critical employees through the transaction, and plan internal communications for the moment the process becomes known. We help leadership balance process demands with running the business. Executives rehearse likely buyer questions.

  5. 05Sustain

    Data room and transition support

    We organize people data for the data room, support responses to buyer questions and prepare handover materials so the incoming owner inherits a clear view of the organization. This continuity benefits employees and supports a smoother transition. We also help the leadership team set out how the people agenda would continue under new ownership.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

Exit readiness engagements are typically commissioned by private equity sponsors, portfolio company CEOs and CFOs, and boards preparing for a sale, recapitalization or public listing. Founder-owned businesses preparing for an external sale or a transition to employee ownership also use this work. Ideal timing is a year or more before launch, which allows meaningful improvements in leadership depth and data quality, though shorter preparation windows can focus on the highest-priority risks.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

A sale process can be an unsettling time for employees, who may hear rumors without explanation and worry about their future. Thoughtful exit readiness reduces that uncertainty through clear communication and fair retention arrangements. Building leadership depth and documenting how the business works also protects customers from disruption when ownership changes. For companies considering employee ownership or mission-aligned buyers, preparation helps sellers evaluate options that preserve jobs, culture and community commitments alongside price.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does exit readiness mean for human capital?

It means the business can show buyers a capable, committed leadership team with depth below it, reduced dependence on any single person, sound incentive and compensation structures, clean workforce data and a healthy culture. It also means having retention arrangements in place for critical employees and a plan for communicating with the workforce once the sale process becomes known.

When should exit preparation start?

Ideally a year or more before a planned process. That window allows time to hire or develop leaders, reduce key person dependency, address compensation issues and build a track record that substantiates the people story. Shorter windows are workable but focus on documentation, data quality, retention arrangements and diligence preparation rather than structural improvements.

What do buyers look for in a management team?

Buyers look for leaders who have delivered results, understand the business deeply and can credibly execute the next owner's plan. They also look for depth below the top team, alignment among leaders, and evidence that key executives intend to stay. Clarity about each leader's role, track record and incentives after the transaction helps buyers assess management risk.

How do you retain key employees during a sale?

Retention starts with identifying who is genuinely critical to value and continuity, then designing arrangements such as transaction bonuses or extended vesting that reward staying through close and beyond. Equally important are honest communication, clear roles during the process and visible leadership. Financial incentives work best when employees also trust how the transition is being managed.
