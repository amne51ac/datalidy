The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Boards are expected to oversee more than ever: strategy, risk, executive performance, culture, workforce issues, cybersecurity, sustainability and stakeholder relationships. Yet many boards spend most meeting time on backward-looking reports and compliance items, leaving limited room for strategic discussion. Board composition may not reflect the skills the organization now needs, committee responsibilities can overlap or leave gaps, and the relationship between the board and management may be either too distant or too operational.

Human capital and culture oversight is a particular gap. Boards often receive limited workforce data beyond headcount and engagement scores and may learn of cultural problems only when they become public. Nonprofit boards face additional challenges around fundraising expectations, role clarity and recruitment, while boards of sponsor-backed companies must balance investor priorities with independent oversight. Boards need practical, candid support to assess how well they work and to govern the issues that matter most.

Our approach

How we deliver board advisory & governance.

  1. 01Listen

    Confidential board interviews

    We interview each director and key executives confidentially about board effectiveness, dynamics, information quality, agenda focus and the board-management relationship. This surfaces candid perspectives that rarely emerge in the boardroom and identifies differences in how directors view their role. All input is reported in aggregate to protect confidentiality.

  2. 02Measure

    Board evaluation and governance review

    We complement interviews with a structured board evaluation survey, observe board and committee meetings, and review charters, agendas, board materials and governance documents. We assess composition against a skills matrix tied to strategy and compare governance practices with recognized standards for the organization's sector. Findings are benchmarked against peers.

  3. 03Design

    Governance improvements

    We recommend changes to board composition, committee structure and charters, agenda design, board materials and the delegation of authority between board and management. We help design oversight of people and culture, including the metrics the board should receive and how often they should discuss them.

  4. 04Deliver

    Facilitate and implement

    We facilitate board retreats and feedback discussions, support director recruitment with defined profiles, design onboarding for new directors and help management improve board reporting. For nonprofit boards, we clarify roles, expectations and the board's relationship with the executive director. Board materials are redesigned to focus on decisions and insight rather than volume.

  5. 05Sustain

    Regular evaluation and development

    We help the board establish a regular evaluation cycle, an annual governance calendar and ongoing director education on topics such as human capital oversight and stakeholder impact. This makes board effectiveness a continuous practice rather than an occasional review. Periodic external facilitation keeps the evaluation candid and the board accountable to its own commitments.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

Our board governance consulting serves board chairs, lead independent directors, governance committee chairs, corporate secretaries, chief executives and executive directors. Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Boards set the tone for how an organization treats its people, customers and communities. When boards oversee culture, workforce practices and stakeholder outcomes thoughtfully, those issues receive sustained attention from management. Well-functioning nonprofit and public boards help organizations deliver on their missions and use resources responsibly. Diverse, well-informed boards are better equipped to anticipate how decisions will affect different groups. Good governance protects the organization's long-term health and the trust that employees and communities place in it.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is a board evaluation?

A board evaluation is a structured assessment of how effectively a board and its committees fulfill their responsibilities. It typically examines composition, structure, processes, information quality, dynamics and the board-management relationship. Evaluations may use surveys, confidential interviews and meeting observation. An independent facilitator often draws out more candid input and offers an external perspective on governance practices.

How should boards oversee human capital and culture?

Boards should define which workforce and culture issues are material to strategy and risk, receive regular metrics such as turnover in critical roles, engagement, safety, pay equity and succession readiness, and discuss them with management in depth. Many boards assign this oversight to a compensation or people committee with a broadened charter. Directors may also gain insight through site visits and conversations beyond the executive team.

What is a board skills matrix?

A board skills matrix maps the expertise, experience and perspectives the board needs against what current directors bring. It helps identify gaps, guide recruitment and plan for director succession. A useful matrix is tied to the organization's strategy and risks rather than a generic list, and it is updated as strategy and board composition change.

Do you work with nonprofit boards?

Yes. Nonprofit boards face distinct challenges, including clarifying the board's role relative to staff, setting fundraising expectations, recruiting directors with relevant skills and lived experience, and overseeing mission impact. We help nonprofit boards evaluate their effectiveness, clarify roles and responsibilities, strengthen the partnership with the executive director and govern mission outcomes.
