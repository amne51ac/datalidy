Human Capital Due Diligence

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most deals receive thorough financial, legal and commercial diligence, while the people who will execute the plan receive a few management presentations and a census file. Yet many post-close problems trace back to human factors that were visible, or at least discoverable, before signing: a founder whose relationships hold the customer base together, a compensation structure that cannot survive a change of control, a second layer of management that is thinner than it appears, or an engineering team with a history of quiet attrition.

Diligence timelines are short, access is limited and sellers present the business in its best light. Deal teams need a disciplined way to test people assumptions within those constraints, and to separate material risks from ordinary organizational noise. They also need findings expressed in terms an investment committee can act on: price adjustments, retention requirements, conditions to close, or priorities for the first hundred days. Human capital due diligence provides that discipline and translates it into decisions.

Our approach

How we deliver human capital due diligence.

  1. 01Listen

    Structured management sessions

    We conduct structured interviews with the CEO and leadership team, focused on how decisions are made, how the team works together and how each leader thinks about the plan. Where permitted, we add conversations with selected second-layer managers and reference sources. We listen for consistency, depth of operating knowledge and where authority truly sits.

  2. 02Measure

    Data room people analytics

    We analyze the employee census, payroll, turnover history, compensation and benefits, organization charts and available engagement data. We look for attrition concentrated in critical roles, pay compression or market gaps, key person dependency, unusual spans and layers, and change of control or severance liabilities that affect the deal model.

  3. 03Design

    Risk register and deal implications

    We consolidate findings into a human capital risk register rated by likelihood and impact, with each risk linked to a deal implication: valuation, structure, retention arrangements, earn-out design, representations, or a 100-day action. This gives the investment committee a concise view of what matters and what does not.

  4. 04Deliver

    Retention and day-one planning

    For risks that must be addressed at close, we help design retention arrangements for critical talent, outline management incentive plan principles and draft day-one communication priorities. Where a leadership change is likely, we define the role requirements and transition approach so that the business does not lose momentum during the ownership change.

  5. 05Sustain

    Handover to the value creation plan

    Diligence findings should not sit in a binder. We hand over a set of baseline people metrics and open questions to the post-close team, so that the value creation plan builds directly on what diligence revealed and progress can be measured from day one. Nothing learned is lost.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

Human capital due diligence is commissioned by private equity deal teams, corporate development leaders, family offices and impact investors evaluating control or significant minority investments. It is especially valuable for founder-led businesses, carve-outs from larger corporations, services businesses where talent is the core asset, and cross-border or cross-sector deals where cultural fit is uncertain. Buyers often engage us after a letter of intent is signed and confirmatory diligence begins, though earlier screening is also possible.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Ownership changes create real uncertainty for employees, and poorly planned transitions can disrupt livelihoods, customer relationships and local communities. Rigorous human capital due diligence helps buyers anticipate those effects and plan responsibly: identifying fair pay issues before they become legal or reputational problems, recognizing which employees hold customer trust, and planning communication that treats people with respect. Buyers who understand the workforce they are acquiring are better positioned to keep commitments to employees and customers alike.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is included in human capital due diligence?

Typical scope covers leadership team assessment, workforce demographics and structure, turnover and retention patterns, compensation and benefits benchmarking, incentive and change of control arrangements, HR compliance indicators, and cultural fit or integration risk. Scope is tailored to the deal thesis, so a software business might emphasize engineering retention while a multi-site services business might focus on frontline turnover and labor cost structure.

How long does human capital due diligence take?

Most engagements run in parallel with confirmatory diligence and are scoped to fit the deal timeline, often a few weeks. A focused red flag review can be completed faster when timing is tight. The depth of work depends on data room quality and access to management. We agree on scope and key questions with the deal team at the outset so findings arrive in time to inform the investment committee decision.

Can you assess culture during diligence?

Yes, within the limits of access. We combine management interviews, available survey data, review platform signals, turnover patterns and observation of how the leadership team operates together. We avoid vague labels and instead describe specific cultural characteristics, such as how decisions get made or how performance is managed, that are relevant to the plan or to integration with an existing business.

How do findings affect the deal?

Findings can inform price, structure and planning. Examples include retention or rollover arrangements for key leaders, earn-out terms aligned to people dependencies, specific representations and warranties, conditions related to leadership hires, and priorities for the 100-day plan. Not every finding changes the deal, and part of our job is distinguishing material risks from ordinary issues that any business of similar size would have.
