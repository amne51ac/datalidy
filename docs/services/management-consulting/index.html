The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Leaders often face problems that cut across functions and resist simple answers: a business that is growing but losing margin, a service organization whose quality varies widely across sites, a public agency asked to deliver more with fewer resources, or a nonprofit whose programs have expanded faster than its operations. Internal teams are busy running the organization and may be too close to the issue to see it clearly. Traditional consulting can provide analytical horsepower, but recommendations often stall because they underestimate the human side of implementation.

Many organizations have experienced consulting projects that produced impressive analysis and little lasting change. The slide deck identified the right answer, but managers were not involved in shaping it, employees did not understand it and the organization lacked the capability to sustain it. Leaders need advisors who bring rigor and independence, and who also understand that the way a solution is developed, and who develops it, largely determines whether it succeeds.

Our approach

How we deliver management consulting.

  1. 01Listen

    Frame the right problem

    We start by defining the problem precisely with leaders, then test that definition through interviews and site visits with managers, front-line employees and, where relevant, customers. Many engagements change direction at this stage, because the presenting problem is a symptom of something deeper. We also review prior work.

  2. 02Measure

    Hypothesis-driven analysis

    We build a fact base using operational, financial, customer and workforce data, testing hypotheses rather than collecting data for its own sake. We look for variation across sites, teams or segments, which often reveals both the root causes of a problem and internal examples of what good looks like.

  3. 03Design

    Solutions shaped with the organization

    We develop options with working teams that include the managers and employees who will carry them out. Options are evaluated against clear criteria, including financial impact, feasibility, risk and effect on employees and customers. Leaders receive a recommendation with explicit trade-offs, not a single answer presented as inevitable.

  4. 04Deliver

    Implementation with internal owners

    We support implementation through pilots, detailed planning, project management and change support. Internal owners lead each workstream with our support, building the capability to continue after we leave. We adapt the plan as pilots reveal what works in practice. Steering committees review progress and decide on adjustments with clear evidence in hand.

  5. 05Sustain

    Embed and hand over

    We establish performance measures, governance routines and documentation so that improvements become standard practice. A structured handover ensures internal teams own the results and know how to respond if performance drifts. We also document the methods used, such as root cause analysis and pilot design, so internal teams can apply the same disciplined problem solving to the next challenge without outside support.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

Our management consulting clients are chief executives, chief operating officers, executive directors, agency heads and boards across companies, health systems, universities, nonprofits and government. Typical triggers include declining performance without a clear cause, a growth plan that requires new capabilities, inconsistent service quality across locations, cost pressure that leaders want to address without damaging service or morale, and complex decisions that would benefit from independent analysis.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Management decisions about structure, process and resources shape the working lives of employees and the experiences of customers. Our management consulting approach involves the people affected and weighs the human consequences of each option alongside financial ones. That reduces the risk of improvements that cut costs while degrading service or burning out staff. For public agencies and nonprofits, better management translates into more effective services for the communities they serve, which is the ultimate test of good advice.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does a management consultant do?

A management consultant helps leaders solve complex problems related to strategy, operations, organization and performance. Work typically involves defining the problem, gathering and analyzing data, developing and evaluating options, recommending a course of action and supporting implementation. The value lies in independent perspective, structured problem solving and the ability to bring experience from other organizations.

How is your management consulting different?

We place equal weight on analytical rigor and on the people who will carry out the answer. We involve managers and employees in shaping solutions, assess the human impact of each option and build internal capability during the engagement. This approach is grounded in the belief that how a solution is developed strongly affects whether it is adopted and sustained.

Do you work with nonprofits and government agencies?

Yes. Mission-driven and public organizations face many of the same management challenges as companies, often with tighter resources and more complex stakeholder environments. We adapt our methods to those contexts, including governance structures, funding constraints and public accountability requirements, and we consider community outcomes as a central measure of success.

How long does a typical engagement take?

Duration depends on scope. A focused diagnostic can take several weeks, while a full engagement from diagnosis through implementation support may run several months. We often structure work in phases with decision points, so leaders can confirm the direction before committing to implementation and adjust scope as the problem becomes clearer.
