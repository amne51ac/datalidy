Stakeholder Engagement

Our stakeholder engagement consulting helps leaders map who holds influence and who bears impact, understand their interests with evidence, and build engagement routines that inform strategy and earn durable trust.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Most organizations engage stakeholders reactively. Investor relations, government affairs, communications, HR and sustainability teams each maintain their own relationships, priorities and messages, with little coordination. When a controversy, regulatory change or major transaction arrives, leaders discover they do not have a clear view of who cares, what they care about or how much trust exists. Engagement becomes a defensive exercise in messaging rather than a source of insight that could have shaped the decision earlier.

There is also a structural blind spot. Stakeholders with power and urgency, such as large investors or regulators, get attention, while those who bear the greatest impact, such as frontline employees, suppliers' workers or nearby communities, are often consulted last or not at all. That imbalance creates risk, because overlooked groups can gain influence quickly, and it weakens decisions, because the people closest to the consequences often see problems first. Effective stakeholder engagement consulting corrects for both.

Our approach

How we deliver stakeholder engagement.

  1. 01Listen

    Hearing stakeholders directly

    We conduct structured interviews and listening sessions across stakeholder groups, internal and external, to understand their interests, expectations, concerns and perceptions of your organization. Internal interviews with functional leaders reveal where relationships are held, what is already known and where messages to different audiences have drifted out of alignment over time.

  2. 02Measure

    Mapping salience and materiality

    We build a stakeholder map using salience criteria of power, legitimacy and urgency, alongside an assessment of how significantly each group is affected by your decisions. Combined with a materiality assessment of the issues they raise, this shows where attention is concentrated, where it is missing and which issues carry both business and societal significance.

  3. 03Design

    Building an engagement architecture

    We design an engagement strategy that assigns each priority group an owner, a purpose, appropriate channels and a cadence, from information sharing to formal advisory panels. We set principles for consistency across functions, define escalation paths for emerging issues and build a mechanism for bringing stakeholder insight into leadership and board decision-making.

  4. 04Deliver

    Engaging and integrating insight

    We support launch of priority engagement activities, from investor and employee forums to multi-stakeholder roundtables, and coach executives who will lead them. Insights are synthesized and routed into the relevant decisions, such as strategy, product, capital allocation or policy positions, and stakeholders are told how their perspectives were considered.

  5. 05Sustain

    Tracking relationships over time

    We set up a shared stakeholder relationship register, trust and sentiment indicators, and a periodic materiality refresh. Governance assigns oversight to a named executive and, where appropriate, a board committee, so stakeholder engagement remains a managed discipline rather than an activity that fades when the immediate pressure passes.

Who we serve

We work with chief executives, chief sustainability and corporate affairs officers, investor relations and general counsel teams, and boards of companies, public institutions and large nonprofits. Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Stakeholder engagement done well distributes attention more fairly. Employees, suppliers' workers and communities who are affected by corporate decisions gain a channel to raise concerns before harm occurs. Customers and investors benefit from an organization that understands their expectations and keeps its commitments. And decisions informed by a wider range of perspectives tend to be more resilient, which supports long-term value for shareholders alongside the broader social good the organization affects.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is stakeholder salience?

Stakeholder salience is a framework from management research that prioritizes stakeholders by three attributes: power to influence the organization, legitimacy of their relationship or claim, and urgency of their concerns. Groups holding more attributes typically receive more attention. We use salience alongside a separate assessment of how significantly each group is affected, because purely salience-based maps can overlook those who bear consequences but lack formal power.

How does stakeholder engagement support a materiality assessment?

A materiality assessment identifies which sustainability and business issues matter most, and stakeholder input is central to it. Under a double materiality approach, organizations assess both how issues affect enterprise value and how the organization affects people and the environment. Structured engagement provides the evidence for both perspectives and helps ensure the assessment reflects real stakeholder concerns rather than internal assumptions.

Who should own stakeholder engagement inside an organization?

Relationships will always be held by multiple functions, but someone must own the overall picture. We usually recommend a named executive accountable for coordination and insight, with functional owners for each priority group and periodic board oversight. The right structure depends on your size and sector; the key is that insights reach decision-makers and commitments are tracked across functions.

How do you measure the effectiveness of stakeholder engagement?

We combine relationship indicators with decision indicators. Relationship indicators include trust and sentiment measures, participation from priority groups and responsiveness to concerns. Decision indicators track whether stakeholder insight was considered in significant decisions and whether stakeholders were told how. Periodic qualitative check-ins with key groups provide context that numbers alone cannot, and help reveal emerging issues early.
