Decision-Making Under Pressure

We help leaders and teams improve decision-making under pressure by combining research on expert intuition and cognitive bias with practical tools, deliberate practice and coaching on the emotional side of high-stakes choices.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Leaders in growing organizations make consequential decisions every week with incomplete data, tight timelines and real uncertainty. Under pressure, predictable patterns emerge. Some leaders freeze and seek more information than the situation allows. Others decide too quickly, anchoring on the first plausible option. Stress narrows attention, emotions color risk perception, and teams defer to the most senior or most confident voice in the room. The cost of these patterns rarely shows up immediately, which makes them easy to overlook.

Most advice on decision-making assumes calm, analytical conditions that rarely exist in practice. Research on naturalistic decision-making shows that experienced professionals often decide by recognizing patterns and mentally simulating a course of action, a process described as recognition-primed decision-making. That intuition can be excellent when it has been trained in a stable environment with good feedback, and dangerous when it has not. Leaders need to know which situation they are in, and they need team processes that catch errors before they become costly.

Our approach

How we deliver decision-making under pressure.

  1. 01Observe

    Study real decisions

    We review recent consequential decisions with the leader and team, reconstructing what was known, how options were framed, who was involved and how the choice was made. Interviews and observation of decision meetings reveal patterns: where intuition worked well, where bias crept in, and where unclear decision rights slowed things down or caused rework.

  2. 02Signal

    Map decision risks

    We identify the decision types that matter most to the business, and for each we assess time pressure, reversibility, available feedback and the leader's experience. This shows where fast, intuitive judgment is appropriate and where a more structured process is warranted. We also identify early signals that a decision is going off track.

  3. 03Design

    Build decision tools and habits

    We design lightweight tools that fit the pace of the business: decision rights matrices, pre-mortems that imagine failure in advance, red-team roles, explicit criteria agreed before options are debated, and short decision logs. We pair these with individual coaching on managing stress responses, noticing emotional state and slowing down for a moment when it matters.

  4. 04Test

    Practice under realistic conditions

    Leaders and teams rehearse decisions through tabletop scenarios and simulations built from their own context, receiving feedback on both process and outcome. We then apply the tools to live decisions and debrief afterward. Decision logs let leaders compare their predictions with what actually happened, building calibration over time.

  5. 05Scale

    Embed decision discipline

    We help the organization embed effective practices into planning cycles, investment reviews and incident response, and we train internal facilitators to run pre-mortems and after-action reviews. The aim is a culture where decisions are made at the right level, at the right speed, and where the organization learns systematically from both good and poor outcomes.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

This work suits chief executives, founders, operating leaders and executive teams who face frequent high-stakes choices: capital allocation, product bets, hiring and restructuring, crisis response and clinical or safety decisions. It is also valuable for investment committees and boards. Common triggers include a costly decision that was made too fast or too slowly, a crisis that exposed unclear authority, rapid growth that pushes more decisions onto new leaders, or a leader who reports feeling overwhelmed by the volume and weight of choices.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Decisions made under pressure ripple outward. In health organizations they shape patient safety and care quality. In financial services and lending they affect customers' financial lives. Inside any organization, better decisions mean fewer whiplash reversals, more stable jobs and less stress for employees who must carry out the choices. Leaders who understand the emotional dimension of judgment also make fairer, more humane decisions about the people affected, which builds trust in the organization and the communities it serves.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is recognition-primed decision-making?

Recognition-primed decision-making is a model from naturalistic decision research describing how experienced professionals decide quickly in real settings. Rather than comparing many options, they recognize a situation as familiar, identify a workable course of action and mentally simulate it to check for problems. It works well when experience was built with reliable feedback. Coaching helps leaders know when their intuition can be trusted and when it cannot.

What is a pre-mortem, and why is it useful?

A pre-mortem asks a team to imagine that a decision has already failed and then explain why. Because it frames dissent as a creative exercise rather than disloyalty, it surfaces risks that people might otherwise keep to themselves. It counters overconfidence and groupthink with minimal time investment. We teach teams to run pre-mortems for significant decisions and to turn the identified risks into specific mitigations.

Can coaching really help with stress during difficult decisions?

Yes. Stress narrows attention and can push leaders toward either rash action or avoidance. Drawing on our background in clinical and health psychology, we help leaders recognize their own stress signals, use brief techniques to regain focus and build routines that protect their judgment, such as sleep, preparation and trusted thinking partners. The goal is not to eliminate pressure but to decide well within it.

Is this service for individuals or teams?

Both. Many decision-making problems are team problems: unclear decision rights, deference to hierarchy or a lack of genuine dissent. We often combine individual coaching for the senior leader with team sessions that build shared decision tools and norms. Some clients start with a decision audit to see where the biggest gaps lie before choosing the right mix.
