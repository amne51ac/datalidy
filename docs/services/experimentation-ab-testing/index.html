Experimentation & A/B Testing

A/B testing is the most reliable way to learn what actually changes behavior. We design experiments and pilots with sound hypotheses, adequate power and guardrail metrics, whether you test web pages, sales scripts, care programs or policies.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Many organizations run tests that cannot answer their own questions. Sample sizes are too small to detect realistic effects. Tests are stopped the moment results look favorable, which inflates false positives. Several metrics are checked until one shows significance. Variants change many things at once, so even a clear winner explains little. Over time, leaders notice that winning tests do not add up to better performance, and confidence in experimentation erodes.

Outside digital products, experimentation is often considered impractical. Yet branch processes, outreach scripts, onboarding programs, clinical reminders and training initiatives can all be tested through randomized pilots, stepped rollouts or carefully matched comparisons. Organizations that only test button colors miss the larger opportunity: learning, with evidence, which operational and service changes actually shift customer, patient and employee behavior. A well-run experimentation program treats every significant change as a chance to learn, and builds a record of what works that compounds over time.

Our approach

How we deliver experimentation & a/b testing.

  1. 01Observe

    Ground tests in real behavior

    We review your current testing practice, past results and decision process, and gather qualitative insight into the behavior each test is meant to change. Good experiments start from an understanding of why people act as they do, which leads to bolder, better-targeted variants than incremental guesswork.

  2. 02Signal

    Choose metrics that matter

    We select a single primary metric linked to a predictive KPI, establish baselines and variance from historical data, and define guardrail metrics that would reveal harm, such as cancellations, complaints, error rates or downstream disengagement. Segment-level baselines help anticipate where effects may differ across behavioral types.

  3. 03Design

    Write the hypothesis and the plan

    Each experiment receives a written plan before launch: the behavioral hypothesis, the mechanism expected to drive change, randomization unit, minimum detectable effect, power calculation, test duration, stopping rules and analysis method. Internal pre-registration of this plan prevents after-the-fact metric shopping and makes results credible to skeptical stakeholders.

  4. 04Test

    Run and analyze with rigor

    We support implementation, monitor sample ratio and data quality during the test, and analyze results with appropriate methods, including confidence intervals, variance reduction where suitable and corrections for multiple comparisons. Results are reported in plain language with effect sizes and uncertainty, not just a pass or fail verdict.

  5. 05Scale

    Build an experimentation capability

    We help establish a test backlog prioritized by expected value, templates for plans and readouts, a knowledge base of past results, and review practices that keep quality high. Training for product, marketing and operations teams turns experimentation from a specialist activity into a routine way of making decisions.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Rigorous experimentation protects people from well-intentioned changes that do not work or that cause unintended harm. Guardrail metrics surface effects on vulnerable groups before rollout. In health, education and community programs, testing ensures limited resources are spent on approaches that genuinely help. We follow ethical review practices appropriate to the setting, avoid deceptive variants, and ensure that experiments respect the autonomy and interests of participants.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is a guardrail metric?

A guardrail metric is a measure you monitor during an experiment to make sure a change does not cause harm, even if it improves the primary metric. Examples include cancellation rates, complaint volume, page load time, error rates, refund requests or downstream retention. If a guardrail deteriorates beyond a predefined threshold, the variant is not shipped, regardless of how well it performs on the main goal.

Why does statistical power matter so much?

An underpowered test has a low chance of detecting a real effect, so it often reports no difference when one exists. Worse, when an underpowered test does show a significant result, the effect size is usually exaggerated. Calculating power before launch tells you how many participants and how much time you need, or whether a bolder change is required to make the test worthwhile.

Can we run experiments outside of digital products?

Yes. We regularly design randomized pilots for outreach scripts, branch processes, appointment reminders, onboarding programs, training curricula and pricing communications. Randomization can occur at the level of customers, locations, teams or time periods. When full randomization is not feasible, stepped-wedge rollouts and matched comparison designs can still provide credible evidence.

Do we need a specific testing platform?

No. We work with whatever tools you already use, from commercial experimentation platforms to feature flag systems or manual assignment in a CRM. The methodology matters more than the platform. If your current tooling limits what you can learn, we will identify the gaps and recommend proportionate options rather than a wholesale replacement.
