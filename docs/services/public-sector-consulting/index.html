Public Sector Consulting

Our public sector consulting helps government agencies improve resident-facing services, strengthen their workforce and operating models, and deliver policy priorities with the evidence, transparency and accountability that public institutions require.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Public agencies are asked to deliver more with constrained budgets, aging systems and a workforce facing significant retirement and recruitment pressures. New funding often arrives with tight timelines and complex compliance requirements. Residents compare government services with the best digital experiences they encounter elsewhere, yet agencies operate within statutes, procurement rules, legacy technology and multiple layers of oversight. The result is services that are difficult to navigate, especially for residents with limited time, language access, disabilities or digital connectivity.

Inside agencies, the people challenges are equally pressing. Hiring processes are slow, career pathways are unclear and frontline staff often absorb the friction of poorly designed processes. Policy teams, program staff, IT and procurement work in separate silos with different incentives. Change initiatives must survive election cycles and leadership transitions. Effective public sector consulting must respect these constraints, including civil service rules, collective bargaining and public records requirements, while still finding practical ways to improve service delivery.

Our approach

How we deliver public sector consulting.

  1. 01Listen

    Understanding residents and public servants

    We conduct research with residents who use, or struggle to access, the service, including those most likely to be excluded. We also spend time with frontline staff, supervisors and partner organizations to understand how policy is actually implemented. This dual view reveals where statutory requirements end and where avoidable administrative burden begins, which is often surprisingly different.

  2. 02Measure

    Establishing a service baseline

    We analyze administrative data on application volumes, processing times, error rates, approvals, denials and churn, disaggregated where lawful and appropriate to identify inequities. We map the service journey and calculate where time and effort concentrate for residents and staff, and assess workforce data on vacancies, turnover and capacity to see which constraints matter most.

  3. 03Design

    Co-designing feasible improvements

    We design changes with residents and staff, within legal and budget limits: simplified forms and notices, clearer eligibility communication, revised workflows, data sharing to reduce duplicate requests and role redesign. Where technology or services must be procured, we help define outcomes-based requirements and evaluation criteria that reflect the needs of the people using them.

  4. 04Deliver

    Implementing within public constraints

    We support pilots with clear success measures, then plan phased rollout, staff training, communications and labor-management engagement. We work alongside agency teams rather than in place of them, and we document decisions to support transparency, audit and public records requirements. Procurement and vendor management support helps keep implementation on schedule and accountable.

  5. 05Sustain

    Building lasting public capacity

    We help the agency establish service performance dashboards, regular review routines and a small internal team skilled in service design and data use. Playbooks and training materials ensure the approach survives leadership transitions, and public-facing reporting gives residents and elected officials a transparent view of progress toward the agency's goals.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We work with agency directors, deputy secretaries, chief operating and performance officers, program administrators and human resources leaders in city, county and state government, along with public authorities and quasi-governmental bodies. Typical triggers include new legislation or funding with implementation deadlines, persistent service backlogs, audit findings, workforce shortages, a modernization initiative, or a new administration seeking to translate policy priorities into measurable improvements for residents. We follow applicable procurement and conflict-of-interest requirements.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Public services shape whether people can access food, housing, health care, income and opportunity. Reducing administrative burden helps eligible residents receive the support intended for them, especially those with the least time and fewest resources to navigate complex systems. Public servants gain workflows that respect their expertise and reduce burnout. And taxpayers and elected officials gain clearer evidence of what public investment achieves, which strengthens trust in government institutions.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How do government agencies hire public sector consultants?

Agencies typically procure consulting services through competitive solicitations such as requests for proposals or qualifications, existing cooperative or statewide contracts, or, for smaller engagements, simplified procurement thresholds defined by their jurisdiction. Requirements vary by level of government and funding source. We work within the agency's procurement rules and can explain our approach to scope, deliverables and pricing in terms evaluators need.

What is administrative burden in public services?

Administrative burden refers to the learning, compliance and psychological costs people experience when interacting with government, such as figuring out eligibility, gathering documents, completing forms and dealing with stigma or uncertainty. Research in public administration shows these burdens can deter eligible people from receiving benefits. Reducing them through simpler processes, data sharing and clearer communication is a central aim of resident-centered service improvement.

Can human-centered design work within government regulations?

Yes. Statutes and regulations define what must happen, but agencies usually have considerable discretion over how: the wording of notices, the sequence of steps, which documents are requested and how staff are organized. Human-centered design focuses on that discretionary space. We work with legal and policy staff early to confirm what is required, so proposed changes are both resident-friendly and compliant.

How do you sustain improvements after leadership changes?

We build capability inside the agency rather than relying on consultants, document methods in playbooks, embed performance measures in routine management reviews and involve career staff throughout. Changes that are visible to residents and supported by frontline employees tend to have constituencies that outlast any single administration. Public reporting on progress also creates accountability that encourages continuity.
