Survey & Measurement Design

Good survey design is a measurement discipline. We apply psychometric methods to build customer, employee, patient and member instruments that are reliable, valid and short enough to complete, so the results support real decisions.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Surveys are among the most common tools organizations use to understand people, and among the most poorly built. Items are written by committee, double-barreled questions ask two things at once, scales shift between sections, and response options nudge people toward favorable answers. Instruments grow longer each year as new stakeholders add questions, and response rates fall. The resulting scores are tracked carefully, yet no one can say whether they measure what they claim.

The consequences are significant. Engagement scores that do not predict turnover, satisfaction scores that do not predict retention, and needs assessments that miss the most important needs all lead to misdirected investment. Psychometrics offers well-established tools for testing reliability and validity, but they are rarely applied outside academic research. Survey design grounded in measurement science changes what organizations can learn from the people they ask, and how much confidence they can place in the answers.

Our approach

How we deliver survey & measurement design.

  1. 01Observe

    Define the construct and the decision

    We begin by clarifying what you need to measure and what decision the measurement will inform. Through interviews and cognitive pretesting with members of the target population, we learn how people interpret the concepts in their own language. This ensures items reflect real experience rather than internal jargon.

  2. 02Signal

    Assess existing instruments and data

    If you already run surveys, we analyze historical responses for internal consistency, factor structure, ceiling effects and item redundancy, and test whether scores relate to behavioral outcomes such as retention or adherence. This identifies items worth keeping, items to revise and gaps where key constructs are not measured at all.

  3. 03Design

    Build a sound instrument

    We write or adapt items using established principles: one idea per item, balanced response scales, neutral wording, logical flow and minimal burden. Where validated scales exist, we use them appropriately. We plan sampling, timing and mode to reduce nonresponse bias, and specify how scores will be calculated and interpreted.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot and validate

    The instrument is piloted with a representative sample. We evaluate reliability with internal consistency and test-retest methods, examine construct validity with factor analysis, and assess criterion validity by linking scores to subsequent behavior where possible. Underperforming items are revised or removed before full launch, and the final length is checked against completion data.

  5. 05Scale

    Field, report and maintain

    We support fielding, design reports that show uncertainty and meaningful change rather than noise, and connect survey results to behavioral data for richer analysis. A measurement manual documents scoring and interpretation so the instrument remains consistent as staff change and can be revalidated periodically against new behavioral outcomes.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Every survey asks people for their time and candor. Well-designed instruments honor that by being shorter, clearer and more likely to lead to action. Employees and patients are more likely to be heard accurately when items are unbiased and accessible. Communities benefit when needs assessments capture real priorities. We design for readability, accessibility and cultural fit, and we protect respondent confidentiality throughout.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What do reliability and validity mean for a business survey?

Reliability means a survey produces consistent results: items intended to measure the same thing agree with each other, and scores are stable when nothing has changed. Validity means the survey measures what it is supposed to measure and relates to the outcomes it should predict. A reliable survey can still be invalid, so both must be tested before results are used for important decisions.

Can you improve our existing engagement or satisfaction survey?

Yes. We start with a psychometric audit of your historical data to see which items are performing, which are redundant and whether the overall scores relate to outcomes like turnover or retention. We then recommend targeted revisions that preserve trend comparability where it matters. Often a shorter, sharper version measures more effectively than the original.

Should we use a published validated scale or build our own?

When a well-validated scale fits your construct and population, using it saves time and allows benchmarking. Many organizational questions, however, are specific enough that custom items are needed. We often combine both, using established scales for core constructs and carefully developed items for context-specific topics, then validate the full instrument with your population.

How do you reduce survey fatigue and nonresponse?

We cut items that do not inform decisions, use adaptive logic so people only see relevant questions, design for mobile completion and explain how results will be used. Timing and invitation design also matter, and both can be tested. Closing the loop by showing respondents what changed based on their input is one of the strongest drivers of future participation.
