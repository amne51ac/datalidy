Strategic Workforce Planning

Our strategic workforce planning helps leaders model the skills, capacity and workforce costs their strategy will require under realistic scenarios, then build practical plans to close the gaps in ways that are fair to employees.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Strategies are usually built around markets, products and capital, while workforce implications are worked out later through annual headcount budgets. That approach treats people as a cost line rather than a capability, and it struggles with the questions that determine whether a strategy can be executed. Do we have the skills to enter this market? What happens to our frontline workforce as automation and AI change the work? Where will retirements leave critical knowledge gaps? Which roles will be hardest to fill, and how long will it take?

Many workforce planning efforts stall because they try to model everything. Large spreadsheets forecast every role in detail, take months to build, and are outdated when finished. Others never move beyond headcount to skills. Meanwhile, decisions about hiring, restructuring and reskilling are made reactively, with real costs to employees who are surprised by changes they could have prepared for. Effective strategic workforce planning focuses on the roles and skills that matter most to the strategy and on decisions that leaders can act on now.

Our approach

How we deliver strategic workforce planning.

  1. 01Listen

    Connect workforce to strategy

    We interview executives and business unit leaders to understand strategic priorities, expected changes in demand, operating model shifts and technology plans. We identify critical roles and skills: those with the greatest impact on strategy and the longest lead time to develop. Conversations with frontline managers and employees reveal how work is actually changing on the ground.

  2. 02Measure

    Baseline supply and model demand

    We build a baseline of current workforce supply by role, skill, location, tenure and cost, and project it forward using attrition, retirement and internal movement patterns. We model future demand under several scenarios tied to business drivers, such as volume, productivity, automation and new market entry. The gap between supply and demand shows where action is needed and when.

  3. 03Design

    Design build, buy, borrow and redeploy options

    For each critical gap we evaluate options: developing internal talent, external hiring, contingent or partner arrangements, redeploying people from declining roles, or redesigning work. We assess options for cost, speed, risk and effect on employees and communities. The result is a workforce plan with clear choices, investments and timing that leaders can debate and approve.

  4. 04Deliver

    Put the plan into motion

    We help translate plans into recruiting strategies, reskilling pathways, internal mobility programs, location decisions and budget changes. Where roles will decline, we support transparent planning that gives employees time and options. We work with HR, finance and business leaders to integrate workforce planning into annual planning and budgeting rather than treating it as a separate exercise.

  5. 05Sustain

    Make planning a recurring discipline

    We set up a lightweight, repeatable planning process with a scenario model your team can update, a small set of indicators to monitor, and triggers that prompt plan revisions. We help define roles and governance across HR, finance and the business, and train internal teams to maintain the model and lead future planning cycles.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Workforce planning decisions shape people's livelihoods. Planning ahead gives employees time to reskill, move into growing roles, or plan transitions, rather than facing sudden layoffs. It can open opportunities for internal candidates and for local talent pools, including people without traditional credentials, when skills-based approaches are used. For communities, especially where an employer is a major source of jobs, thoughtful planning reduces economic disruption and can support partnerships with schools and workforce programs.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is strategic workforce planning?

Strategic workforce planning is the process of determining the workforce an organization will need to execute its strategy, comparing that to the workforce it is likely to have, and deciding how to close the gap. It looks beyond annual headcount budgets to critical skills, multi-year time horizons and alternative scenarios. The output is a set of decisions about hiring, development, redeployment, partnerships and work design.

How is strategic workforce planning different from headcount planning?

Headcount planning sets the number of positions and associated costs for the next budget year, usually by department. Strategic workforce planning covers a longer horizon, focuses on skills and critical roles rather than position counts, and tests multiple scenarios. It informs headcount budgets but also addresses questions they cannot, such as how automation will change roles or where future skill shortages will appear.

How far ahead should we plan?

Most organizations benefit from a planning horizon of three to five years for critical roles and skills, with more detail for the near term and broader scenarios further out. The right horizon depends on how long it takes to develop key capabilities. Roles that require years of training or licensure need longer horizons. We revisit plans at least annually and when significant changes occur.

How do you account for AI and automation?

We analyze roles at the task level to estimate which activities are likely to be automated, augmented or unchanged, and how quickly, given your technology plans. We model the resulting changes in capacity and skill needs under several adoption scenarios. Just as important, we plan for how affected employees can move into new or redesigned roles, and we involve workers in redesigning work where possible.
