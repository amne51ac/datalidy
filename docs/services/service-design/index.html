Service Design

Service design aligns everything behind the customer experience: frontline roles, backstage processes, policies and technology. We use behavioral research, service blueprints and pilots to design services that work for customers and for the people delivering them.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Customers experience a service as one continuous journey, but organizations deliver it through separate departments, systems and incentives. The website promises one thing, the call center follows a different script, the branch or clinic has its own procedures, and the billing system operates on rules no one remembers writing. When these parts are not designed together, customers fall into the gaps: repeating information, waiting for callbacks, receiving contradictory answers and wondering who is responsible.

Frontline employees feel these failures most directly. They absorb customer frustration, invent workarounds and spend time fixing problems created elsewhere. Improving only the visible touchpoint, such as a new app or a redesigned lobby, rarely helps if the processes behind it remain unchanged. Service design addresses the whole system, connecting frontstage experience to backstage operations, roles and incentives so that good experiences are delivered reliably, by design, rather than occasionally through individual heroics.

Our approach

How we deliver service design.

  1. 01Observe

    Study the service end to end

    We observe service delivery directly through service safaris, shadowing, contextual inquiry and interviews with customers and staff at every touchpoint. We document handoffs, policies, systems and workarounds, capturing both the customer's experience and the conditions frontline employees work within, including the informal practices that keep the service running.

  2. 02Signal

    Find where the service breaks

    Operational data, such as wait times, repeat contacts, handoff delays, rework and complaint categories, reveals where the service fails and how often. We link these signals to customer behavior and outcomes, identifying the failure points that most affect retention, satisfaction and cost, and the behavioral types most affected.

  3. 03Design

    Blueprint and redesign the service

    We create service blueprints that show customer actions, frontstage interactions, backstage processes and supporting systems together. With customers and staff, we co-design improvements: clearer roles, redesigned handoffs, simplified policies, better information flow and behavioral cues that help both customers and employees do the right thing at the right time.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot in real conditions

    Redesigned services are piloted in selected locations, teams or customer groups, with clear measures of experience, operational performance and staff workload. We compare pilots with similar sites or periods, gather qualitative feedback and refine the design before broader implementation, avoiding costly rollouts of untested changes.

  5. 05Scale

    Implement and sustain

    We support implementation through updated procedures, training, technology requirements and leadership coaching. Service performance dashboards track the key moments and leading indicators. Clear ownership for cross-functional handoffs keeps the improved service from drifting back to old habits as staff, leadership and priorities change over time.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Service design improves daily life for both customers and staff. Patients navigate care with less confusion, members get answers without repeated calls, and citizens access public programs more easily. Employees benefit when their insight shapes the redesign and when they are no longer forced to compensate for broken systems. We pay particular attention to people with the least time, resources or ability to navigate complex services.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is service design?

Service design is the practice of planning and organizing the people, processes, policies, information and technology involved in delivering a service, so the experience is consistent, efficient and valuable. It considers both the customer-facing frontstage and the backstage operations that support it. The goal is a service that works reliably for customers and for the employees who deliver it.

What is a service blueprint?

A service blueprint is a diagram that maps a service over time, showing customer actions, frontstage employee interactions, backstage processes and supporting systems in parallel layers. It makes visible how internal activities shape the customer experience and where handoffs or dependencies create risk. Blueprints are practical tools for diagnosing problems and planning improvements across departments.

How is service design different from customer journey mapping?

Journey mapping focuses on the customer's experience and is often a first step. Service design goes further, examining and redesigning the internal operations, roles, policies and systems that produce that experience, then implementing and testing changes. Journey maps describe what customers experience. Service design changes what the organization does to create a better experience.

How do you involve frontline employees?

Frontline staff are essential partners. We shadow them, interview them and invite them into co-design sessions, because they understand the real service better than anyone. Involving them improves design quality and builds ownership, which is critical for successful implementation. We also measure staff workload and experience as part of every pilot.
