Predictive Retention Analytics

Our employee retention analytics use survival analysis and carefully governed predictive models to explain why people leave, identify rising attrition risk early, and help managers act on the factors they can actually influence.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Turnover is one of the most expensive and disruptive people problems an organization faces. Each departure carries recruiting and training costs, lost productivity, knowledge loss and strain on remaining colleagues, and in customer-facing roles it erodes service quality and relationships. Yet most organizations respond to attrition after the fact. Exit interviews arrive too late, are often candid only in part, and are rarely analyzed systematically. Monthly turnover reports show what happened but not why, or who is likely to leave next.

Predictive models can help, but they also carry risks. A model that flags individuals as flight risks without explanation can lead to unfair treatment, damage trust, or encode historical bias. Simple models built on annual turnover rates ignore timing: an employee who leaves at three months differs from one who leaves at five years. Effective employee retention analytics use methods suited to time-to-event data, explain the drivers in actionable terms, are tested for fairness, and are embedded in clear governance about how predictions can be used.

Our approach

How we deliver predictive retention analytics.

  1. 01Listen

    Learn why people stay and leave

    We analyze exit interview and survey comments, and conduct stay interviews and focus groups with current employees, including recently hired and long-tenured staff. We talk with managers about what they observe before people leave. This qualitative evidence generates hypotheses about drivers, such as scheduling, manager relationships, pay competitiveness or career paths, and informs which data to include in models.

  2. 02Measure

    Model attrition with survival analysis

    We assemble data on tenure, role, pay position, manager, performance, schedule, commute, mobility and engagement. Using survival analysis, including Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox proportional hazards models, we estimate how risk changes over tenure and which factors accelerate or delay departure. Where useful we add machine learning models, validated on holdout data and tested for disparate impact across demographic groups.

  3. 03Design

    Design targeted retention interventions

    We translate drivers into interventions aimed at the factors the organization controls: onboarding improvements for early-tenure risk, pay adjustments where market position drives exits, scheduling changes, manager coaching or clearer career paths. We size the expected effect of each option and design pilots with control groups where feasible, so leaders can learn what actually reduces attrition.

  4. 04Deliver

    Equip managers and HR to act

    We deliver risk insights to HR business partners and managers through team-level views that focus on drivers and recommended actions, typically not individual scores. We train managers in stay conversations and support HR in running retention pilots. Communication with employees about how data is used helps maintain trust.

  5. 05Sustain

    Govern, monitor and refresh models

    We establish model governance covering permitted uses, access, fairness testing, documentation and periodic validation. We monitor model performance and drift, refresh models as conditions change, and track whether interventions are reducing regretted attrition. Internal analysts receive documented code and training so they can maintain the models responsibly.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Reducing avoidable turnover benefits employees first: it usually means better onboarding, fairer pay, more humane schedules, stronger managers and clearer paths to grow. Stable teams provide more consistent care and service to customers, patients and students. Lower churn reduces the burden on colleagues who cover vacancies. For communities, steadier employment supports household stability. Careful governance ensures predictive tools help employees rather than label them, preserving trust in how the organization uses people data.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Can you really predict which employees will leave?

Models can estimate the relative likelihood of departure for groups and individuals with useful accuracy, but no model predicts individual decisions with certainty. People leave for personal reasons that data cannot see. The greatest value usually comes not from individual predictions but from understanding the drivers and timing of attrition, which lets organizations address root causes. We use predictions carefully and within clear governance.

Why use survival analysis for attrition?

Survival analysis is designed for time-to-event data, so it models not just whether someone leaves but when. It handles employees who have not yet left, known as censored observations, correctly, which simpler methods do not. It shows how risk changes over tenure, such as spikes at the first anniversary, and estimates how factors like pay position or schedule shift that risk over time.

Should managers see individual flight-risk scores?

In most cases we recommend against it. Individual scores can lead to unfair treatment, self-fulfilling outcomes or privacy concerns. We typically provide managers with team-level insights about risk drivers and suggested actions, such as holding stay conversations or addressing scheduling issues. Individual-level use, where appropriate, should be limited to HR professionals with clear guidelines and a focus on support.

How do you make sure a retention model is fair?

We test model predictions and errors across demographic groups to check whether some groups are flagged more often or predicted less accurately. We review input variables for proxies of protected characteristics, document model design and limitations, and establish rules for how predictions can be used. Fairness testing is repeated whenever the model is refreshed, as part of ongoing model governance.
