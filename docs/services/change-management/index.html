Change Management

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Organizations change constantly: new technology platforms, restructurings, mergers, policy shifts, return-to-office decisions, new service models and the adoption of AI tools. Each initiative may be sensible on its own, but employees experience them together, often with limited explanation and little say. Change fatigue sets in, cynicism grows, and even well-designed initiatives struggle to win commitment. Leaders see delays, workarounds and declining engagement without always understanding why. Often the problem is not the change itself but how it was introduced.

Many change efforts focus on communication and training plans that are produced near the end of a project, after key decisions have been made. They track activity, such as emails sent or courses completed, rather than whether people have actually changed how they work. Managers, who carry most of the burden of explaining change, are rarely prepared for that role. Organizations need change management that starts early, involves the people affected, equips managers and measures adoption honestly.

Our approach

How we deliver change management.

  1. 01Listen

    Understand how change will land

    We conduct interviews and focus groups with groups affected by the change to understand their concerns, current workload, past experiences of change and what would make the transition workable. This reveals resistance that is rooted in legitimate practical issues, which often improves the change itself.

  2. 02Measure

    Impact assessment and readiness baseline

    We assess the impact of the change on each group: what changes in their roles, processes, tools and behaviors, and how significant each shift is. We measure readiness and identify concurrent initiatives to gauge change load. This shapes where to focus effort and how to sequence.

  3. 03Design

    Change strategy designed with stakeholders

    We design a change strategy covering sponsorship, stakeholder engagement, communication, training and reinforcement, tailored to each affected group. Representatives from those groups help shape the approach. We build a change network of respected employees who provide feedback and support peers. Each group's plan reflects how much its work actually changes, avoiding a uniform approach that over-serves some and under-serves others.

  4. 04Deliver

    Equip leaders and managers

    We coach sponsors on visible leadership, prepare managers with practical guides for team conversations, and deliver communications and training timed to when people need them. We run feedback loops during rollout to identify issues quickly and adjust the plan. Sponsors hear directly from the front line through regular feedback sessions and visible follow-up.

  5. 05Sustain

    Measure adoption and reinforce

    We track adoption through behavioral and operational indicators, such as system usage, process compliance and outcome measures, alongside sentiment. Reinforcement mechanisms, including recognition, performance expectations and removal of old workarounds, help new ways of working become the norm. Leaders review the evidence on a regular cadence and act where adoption is lagging.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Change done poorly takes a real toll: stress, lost confidence, disrupted relationships with customers and, in public services, disruption to people who depend on them. Change done well respects people's time, knowledge and concerns. By involving employees in shaping change and equipping managers to support their teams, we help organizations reduce unnecessary disruption. Customers and communities experience steadier service through transitions, and employees build confidence that future changes will be handled fairly.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is change management?

Change management is the structured approach to helping people adopt new ways of working, whether those involve technology, processes, structures or strategy. It covers understanding how the change affects different groups, building leadership sponsorship, engaging stakeholders, communicating, training and reinforcing new behaviors. Its purpose is to ensure an initiative's intended benefits are realized through actual adoption.

Why do change initiatives fail?

Common reasons include unclear rationale, weak or inconsistent sponsorship, too many concurrent changes, limited involvement of the people affected, underprepared managers and a focus on launch rather than adoption. Many of these are avoidable when change management begins early, alongside the design of the initiative, rather than being added after decisions have been made.

How do you measure change adoption?

We define what adoption looks like in observable terms for each group, such as using a new system for specific tasks or following a new process. We track these behaviors through operational data where possible, supplemented by pulse surveys and manager feedback. Combining behavior and sentiment indicators gives a more accurate picture than training completion or communication metrics.

How do you prevent change fatigue?

We map all concurrent initiatives affecting each group to understand cumulative change load, then work with leaders to sequence, combine or pause initiatives where load is too high. We also make change easier to absorb through clear explanations, adequate time for learning and removal of old tasks. Listening to employees regularly helps identify fatigue before it becomes disengagement.
