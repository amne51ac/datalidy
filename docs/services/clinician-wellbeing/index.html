Clinician Wellbeing & Burnout Reduction

Reducing clinician burnout requires addressing the conditions that cause it. We combine health psychology, workflow analysis and leadership coaching to help health organizations reduce burnout drivers and build environments where clinicians can practice well.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Clinician burnout, marked by exhaustion, cynicism and a diminished sense of accomplishment, affects physicians, nurses, advanced practice clinicians, pharmacists and other care team members. Its drivers are largely systemic: excessive workload, documentation burden, inefficient electronic health record workflows, staffing shortages, lack of control over schedules, misaligned values and limited support from leaders. Yet many responses emphasize individual resilience, offering mindfulness sessions or wellness days while the conditions that drive exhaustion remain unchanged.

Burnout has consequences beyond the individual. It is associated with turnover, reduced engagement and concerns about quality and safety. Replacing experienced clinicians is costly and disrupts teams and patient relationships. Health organizations also struggle to measure burnout and track change, as surveys are infrequent and results are slow to translate into action. Effective clinician wellbeing work requires diagnosing specific drivers in each setting, involving clinicians in designing solutions and measuring whether conditions improve.

Our approach

How we deliver clinician wellbeing & burnout reduction.

  1. 01Observe

    Listen to clinicians and observe work

    We conduct confidential interviews and focus groups with clinicians across roles and observe clinical workflows, including documentation, inbox management and handoffs. We identify the specific tasks and conditions that drain energy and those that restore it. Our clinical health psychology background helps us understand the emotional demands of care work with empathy and precision.

  2. 02Signal

    Pinpoint burnout drivers

    We analyze validated burnout and engagement surveys alongside operational signals such as after-hours documentation time, inbox volume, scheduling patterns and turnover. This identifies which drivers matter most for which groups and units, and defines predictive indicators that can show early warning signs before clinicians leave or disengage.

  3. 03Design

    Co-design system changes

    With clinicians and leaders, we design interventions that address the identified drivers: workflow redesign, team-based documentation and inbox support, schedule flexibility, protected time, peer support programs and leadership practices that increase voice and control. Individual supports are offered as complements to system changes, never substitutes.

  4. 04Test

    Pilot in clinical units

    We pilot changes in selected clinics or units, measuring burnout indicators, workflow signals and clinician experience compared with baselines and similar units. Short feedback cycles allow rapid adjustment. Pilots also build credibility with clinicians, who are rightly skeptical of initiatives that promise relief without changing their daily work.

  5. 05Scale

    Spread and lead well

    We help organizations spread effective changes, embed wellbeing measures into regular operational reviews and coach clinical leaders, such as department chairs and nurse managers, on practices that support their teams. Leadership coaching and team coaching help sustain improvements and create cultures in which clinicians feel heard and supported.

Who we serve

Who engages us.

We work with health systems, hospitals, physician groups, nursing leadership, academic medical centers, behavioral health providers and community health organizations. Clients include chief wellness officers, chief medical and nursing officers, human resources leaders and department chairs. Typical triggers include rising turnover or vacancy rates, concerning burnout survey results, an electronic health record transition, staffing pressures after periods of high demand, or a commitment to build a sustainable clinician wellbeing strategy.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

Clinicians who are exhausted and disengaged cannot give patients their best. Reducing burnout supports clinicians' own health and their families, helps keep experienced professionals in practice and strengthens the quality and safety of care. Patients benefit from continuity and attention. Communities, particularly those with limited access to care, benefit when health organizations retain the clinicians they depend on. Caring for caregivers is foundational to caring for everyone else.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What causes clinician burnout?

Research points mainly to systemic drivers: heavy workload, documentation and inbox burden, inefficient technology, staffing shortages, limited control over schedules, values conflicts and insufficient leadership support. Individual factors play a role, but they rarely explain burnout on their own. Because drivers vary by specialty, role and setting, we diagnose them within each organization rather than assuming a single cause.

Are resilience programs enough to reduce burnout?

Resilience and mindfulness programs can help some clinicians cope, but they do not address the conditions that cause burnout, and they can feel dismissive if offered alone. Evidence and clinician experience suggest that meaningful reduction requires organizational changes to workload, workflow and leadership. We position individual supports as complements to system change, not replacements for it.

How do you measure clinician burnout?

We use validated burnout and wellbeing instruments, supplemented by operational signals such as after-hours documentation time, inbox volume, schedule patterns and turnover. Combining survey and operational data helps identify drivers and detect early warning signs. Results are reported at aggregate levels to protect confidentiality, which is essential for honest participation.

How do you involve clinicians in designing solutions?

Clinicians are the experts in their own work. We involve them throughout: in confidential interviews, in co-design sessions where they propose and refine solutions, and in pilots where their feedback shapes adjustments. Involvement itself can improve sense of control. We are careful to respect clinicians' time, keeping participation focused and making sure their input visibly leads to change.
