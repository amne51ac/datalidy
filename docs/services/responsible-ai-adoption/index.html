Responsible AI Adoption

Our responsible AI adoption services help leaders choose valuable use cases, set governance proportionate to risk, redesign jobs and workflows, and prepare employees for change, so AI improves work and service rather than eroding trust.

The challenge

The problem behind the problem.

Organizations face pressure to adopt AI quickly, from boards, investors, competitors and employees already using tools informally. Many respond with scattered pilots that never scale, or with broad deployments lacking clear governance. Questions about data privacy, accuracy, bias, intellectual property and accountability remain unanswered. Regulatory expectations are evolving across jurisdictions. Without a deliberate approach, organizations either move too slowly to realize value or move too quickly and create risks that harm customers, employees and reputation.

The workforce dimension is often the least prepared. Employees worry about job security and whether AI will be used to monitor them. Managers do not know how to redesign roles or evaluate AI-assisted work. Expertise and judgment that took years to build may be undervalued or lost. When AI decisions affect hiring, performance or customer eligibility, fairness concerns become acute. Responsible AI adoption requires treating workforce transition, job design and employee voice as central parts of the program, not as communications afterthoughts.

Our approach

How we deliver responsible ai adoption.

  1. 01Listen

    Understanding work and concerns

    We interview leaders, managers and employees across functions to understand current AI use, including informal use, the tasks people find most burdensome and their concerns about AI. Customer and, where relevant, worker representative perspectives are included. This research identifies use cases grounded in real work and surfaces trust issues that adoption must address.

  2. 02Measure

    Assessing tasks, risks and readiness

    We conduct a task-level analysis of priority roles to identify which activities AI could automate, augment or leave unchanged, and assess data readiness, risk level and potential value of candidate use cases. A risk tiering approach, informed by frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, determines the level of oversight each use case requires.

  3. 03Design

    Designing governance, work and transition

    We design an AI governance model with policies, review processes, accountability and human oversight proportionate to risk. For priority use cases, we redesign workflows and roles with the employees involved, defining where human judgment remains essential. A workforce transition plan covers reskilling, redeployment, new roles and commitments about how productivity gains will be shared.

  4. 04Deliver

    Piloting and scaling responsibly

    We support pilots with clear success criteria, including quality, fairness, employee experience and customer outcomes alongside productivity. Training builds AI literacy for all employees and deeper skills for those whose roles change most. Managers learn to lead hybrid human and AI work. Successful pilots scale through a structured process that includes impact assessment and change management.

  5. 05Sustain

    Monitoring and adapting

    We establish ongoing monitoring of AI performance, fairness, incidents and employee and customer feedback, with clear escalation paths. Governance bodies review the use case portfolio, update policies as regulations and technology change, and track workforce transition indicators. Channels for employees to raise concerns about AI use help catch problems early and sustain trust.

Impact lens

Beyond the balance sheet.

AI will reshape work for many people, and how organizations adopt it matters. A responsible approach protects employees through reskilling, transparent communication and fair sharing of gains. Customers benefit from AI that is accurate, fair and subject to meaningful human oversight. Communities benefit when organizations avoid deploying systems that reinforce bias or exclusion. And careful adoption builds the trust that lets organizations realize lasting productivity and service improvements from AI.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is responsible AI?

Responsible AI refers to developing and using artificial intelligence in ways that are safe, fair, transparent, accountable and respectful of privacy and human rights. In practice, it involves governance policies, risk assessment, human oversight, testing for bias and accuracy, monitoring after deployment and mechanisms for people affected by AI decisions to understand and challenge them. It also includes attention to how AI affects workers.

How should organizations govern AI use?

Effective AI governance is proportionate to risk. It typically includes an inventory of AI use, a risk tiering process, policies for acceptable use and data handling, review requirements for higher-risk uses, clear accountability, human oversight and monitoring. Frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the ISO/IEC AI management system standard provide useful structure. Governance should enable valuable use, not only restrict it.

How can we prepare our workforce for AI?

Start with honest communication about intentions and how gains will be shared. Analyze roles at the task level to understand likely changes, then design reskilling and redeployment pathways. Build AI literacy for everyone and deeper skills for roles that change most. Involve employees in redesigning workflows, because they understand the work and its risks. Train managers to lead teams through the transition.

Will AI replace jobs in our organization?

AI typically changes tasks within jobs before it changes the jobs themselves, automating some activities, augmenting others and creating new ones. The net effect depends on your strategy and choices, including whether productivity gains are used to reduce headcount, grow the business or improve service. We help leaders analyze likely impacts by role and make deliberate, transparent decisions about workforce transition.
