Context

The people side of education.

Education is fundamentally a people enterprise. Student learning depends on teachers, counselors, paraprofessionals, advisors and faculty, and on the principals and academic leaders who shape the conditions for their work. Many districts are struggling to fill positions in special education, math, science and bilingual education, while experienced educators report high workloads, limited planning time and a sense that their professional judgment is undervalued. Principal turnover disrupts school culture and slows improvement efforts. In higher education, staff and adjunct faculty face similar pressures.

Students and families experience the effects directly. Chronic absenteeism, mental health needs and gaps in academic recovery remain significant concerns. Students who do not feel a sense of belonging, often first-generation students, students of color, students with disabilities and multilingual learners, are less likely to persist. In higher education, complex enrollment, financial aid and advising processes create barriers that cause capable students to stop out. Family and community trust is shaped by how schools communicate and listen.

Several forces are shaping education now. Many districts face declining enrollment and tightening budgets as temporary relief funding ends, forcing difficult decisions about staffing and school consolidation. Colleges confront demographic shifts in the number of traditional-age students, questions about value and affordability, and growing demand for adult and workforce learners. AI tools are changing how students learn and how educators work. Education consulting must help leaders navigate these pressures while protecting what matters most: relationships that help students learn.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Educator retention and shortages

Districts struggle to recruit and retain teachers in high-need subjects and schools, and early-career teachers are especially likely to leave. Workload, compensation, administrative support and school culture all play a role. Leaders need to understand which factors drive departures in their own context and which levers are within their control.

02

Principal and leader pipelines

School leaders shape teacher retention, school climate and student outcomes, yet principal turnover is common and pipelines are thin. Assistant principals and teacher leaders often lack structured preparation. Without deliberate succession planning and support, schools lose momentum each time a leader departs.

03

Student belonging and engagement

Students who feel disconnected from school are more likely to miss class, disengage and leave. Belonging is shaped by relationships with adults, peer culture, curriculum and daily routines. Measuring and improving it requires listening directly to students, particularly those who have historically been marginalized, and acting on what they share.

04

Enrollment and student journeys in higher education

Prospective and current students navigate complex admissions, financial aid, registration and advising systems. Each friction point can cause a capable student to stop out. Institutions facing enrollment pressure need to redesign the student journey end to end, coordinating offices that often operate independently.

05

Difficult budget decisions

Declining enrollment and the end of temporary funding force districts and colleges to consider consolidation, program cuts and staffing changes. These decisions affect educators, students and neighborhoods deeply. Leaders need sound data, transparent processes and genuine community engagement to make choices that are fair and that communities can understand.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Education is one of society's most powerful engines of opportunity. Strong schools and colleges help young people and adults build knowledge, skills and agency, and they anchor neighborhoods and local economies. That promise depends on educators who are supported to do their best work and on students who feel seen and capable. Better people practices, from teacher retention to leadership pipelines to belonging-centered design, help institutions deliver on that promise more equitably, so that a student's background does not determine the quality of the experience or the outcomes they achieve.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does your education consulting cover?

Our education consulting covers workforce and student experience in K-12 districts, charter networks, community colleges and universities. Typical work includes educator retention analysis, leadership development and succession planning, student belonging research, student journey redesign, strategic planning and community engagement on difficult decisions. Each engagement combines data analysis with direct listening to educators, students and families.

How do you measure student belonging?

We use a combination of established survey measures, focus groups, student-led research and administrative data such as attendance and course engagement. Disaggregating results by student group shows where experiences differ. Most importantly, we involve students in interpreting the findings and designing responses, which builds trust and produces solutions students recognize as their own.

Can you help with school consolidation or closure decisions?

Yes. We help districts gather and present enrollment, facility and financial data clearly, design inclusive engagement processes, and develop decision criteria with community input. We also support transition planning for affected students, families and staff. The aim is a process that communities can understand and trust, even when the decisions themselves are difficult.
