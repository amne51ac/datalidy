Mortgage & Consumer Lending

The mortgage borrower experience is long, emotional and full of data gaps. We help mortgage and consumer lenders understand borrower behavior between application and closing, and after it, so they can design for confidence, completion and lasting relationships.

Context

The people side of mortgage & consumer lending.

For most homebuyers, a mortgage is the largest financial commitment they will make, and it arrives during an already stressful life event. The journey from first inquiry to closing involves rate shopping, documentation, appraisal, underwriting conditions and coordination with agents, title companies and sellers. Borrowers experience uncertainty at every step. When they do not understand what is happening or what they need to do next, they delay, disengage or switch lenders, and loan officers spend their time chasing documents instead of advising.

Lenders face a distinctive data problem. Between application and closing there are long stretches where borrower behavior is barely visible, and after closing the relationship often goes quiet until a refinance or next purchase, when the borrower may not return. Point-of-sale systems, loan origination platforms, customer relationship tools, servicing and marketing each capture fragments. Without an integrated behavioral view, lenders rely on assumptions about why borrowers fall out, what drives recapture and which communications actually help.

Rate cycles, affordability pressures and digital-first competitors make these questions urgent. Volume swings test whether teams can maintain experience quality, and first-time buyers often need more guidance than processes assume. Inside lending organizations, loan officers, processors, underwriters and marketing teams are measured on different outcomes. Lenders that treat the mortgage borrower experience as a behavioral journey, with predictive measures for each stage, can improve pull-through, borrower confidence and long-term retention without relying on rate alone.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Silent gaps between application and closing

After a borrower applies, weeks can pass with only sporadic contact while underwriting proceeds. Borrowers in that gap wonder whether things are on track, respond slowly to condition requests and may entertain competing offers. Lenders often lack signals that a borrower is losing confidence until the loan is delayed or withdrawn.

02

Document requests that stall progress

Requests for pay stubs, statements and explanations are a major source of friction. Borrowers may not understand why something is needed, where to find it or how to submit it. Each round of back and forth extends timelines and frustrates everyone involved, yet the requests are rarely designed with behavioral principles in mind.

03

Relationships that go quiet after closing

Once the loan closes, many lenders have little meaningful contact with borrowers until a refinance or new purchase is possible. By then the borrower may not remember the lender or may associate it with stress. Long post-close data gaps make it difficult to know who is likely to return and what would earn that return.

04

First-time buyers who need more guidance

First-time homebuyers often feel uncertain about credit, down payments and the process itself. Standard communications assume familiarity they may not have. Without designed support, these borrowers are more likely to delay, make costly mistakes or give up, which is a loss for both the household and the lender.

05

Loan teams measured on different outcomes

Loan officers, processors, underwriters, closers and marketers each own part of the borrower experience but are judged by different metrics. Handoffs create delays and inconsistent messages. Leaders struggle to align teams around the borrower behaviors that predict funded loans, satisfaction and referral, rather than around separate departmental targets.

How we help

Where we focus.

  1. →

    Homebuyer journey mapping

    We map the homebuyer journey from first inquiry through application, processing, closing and the years after, including interactions with agents and third parties. Borrower and loan team interviews reveal moments of confusion and anxiety, while data analysis shows where borrowers stall or fall out. The map becomes a shared reference for teams that usually see only their own stage.

    Customer Journey Mapping

  2. →

    Signals for the data gaps

    Using Behavioral Signals Design, we connect point-of-sale, origination, CRM and servicing data to find behaviors that predict pull-through, delay and future return. Where data is missing, we make the underlying assumptions explicit and define testable hypotheses. The outcome is a short set of predictive KPIs for each stage of the borrower relationship.

    Behavioral Signals Design

  3. →

    Borrower communication and engagement design

    We redesign status updates, condition requests and educational content around what borrowers need to act. Clear next steps, better timing and reassurance during quiet periods can reduce uncertainty and speed responses. Messages are tested through controlled comparisons so lenders know which changes actually improve engagement and completion.

    Behavioral Design Consulting

  4. →

    Post-close retention strategy

    We help lenders design a post-close relationship that is useful rather than promotional, with communication tied to life events, homeownership milestones and financial health. Behavioral segmentation identifies which borrowers are most likely to need help next, and experiments test which forms of contact build enough trust for a borrower to return.

    Customer Retention Strategy

  5. →

    Loan process mapping and team alignment

    We map the operational process behind the borrower experience, identify handoffs that create delay and redesign roles around shared borrower outcomes. Leadership alignment sessions and team coaching help loan officers, processors and underwriters work from common measures, so improvement efforts reinforce one another instead of competing.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Homeownership is a primary path to household stability and wealth for many families, and the mortgage process can either open that path or make it feel unreachable. Behavioral science advances the social good in lending by making complex financial decisions more understandable, reducing the anxiety that leads qualified borrowers to give up and supporting first-time buyers who lack experienced guidance. It also encourages responsible persuasion: communication that helps borrowers make informed choices aligned with their own interests, rather than pressure that serves short-term volume at the borrower's expense.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How can behavioral science improve the mortgage borrower experience?

It focuses attention on the specific behaviors that move a loan forward, such as responding to condition requests, locking a rate or scheduling an appraisal, and on the uncertainty that slows them. By mapping the journey, finding predictive signals in existing data and testing better communications, lenders can reduce friction and anxiety. Results depend on each lender's context, so changes are tested before scaling.

What do you mean by data gaps in the mortgage journey?

There are long periods, particularly during underwriting and after closing, when lenders have little visibility into borrower behavior or sentiment. Decisions about outreach and recapture are then based on assumptions. We identify where those gaps occur, what signals can fill them and which assumptions need to be tested, so teams can act earlier and with more confidence.

Do you work with consumer lenders outside mortgage?

Yes. Many of the same principles apply to personal loans, auto finance and home equity products: complex decisions, documentation friction and relationships that fade after funding. We adapt the approach to each product's journey and regulatory context, always with an emphasis on informed borrower choice and on measures that reflect the borrower's long-term financial wellbeing.
