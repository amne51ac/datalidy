Context

The people side of technology.

Technology companies change shape quickly. A startup can double its headcount in a year, and a mature company can reorganize several times in the same period. Each change tests leaders who were promoted for technical excellence but may have had little preparation for managing managers, building culture or making hard tradeoffs between teams. After a period of rapid hiring followed by layoffs across much of the industry, many employees are wary, trust in leadership has been strained, and remaining teams carry wider scopes with fewer people.

Outside the company, users, customers and the public are asking more of technology. They expect products that respect privacy, work for people of all abilities and do not cause harm to vulnerable groups. Enterprise customers expect reliable service and transparency. Regulators in multiple jurisdictions are introducing rules on data protection, platform accountability and AI. Technology companies that understand their users and stakeholders deeply are better positioned to build products people trust and to anticipate scrutiny before it arrives.

Several forces are reshaping the sector now. Generative AI is changing how software is built, how support is delivered and which roles a company needs, raising questions about reskilling and job design. Distributed and hybrid work models have become permanent for many teams, with consequences for collaboration, onboarding and culture. Capital efficiency matters more than growth at any cost. Organizational consulting for technology companies must help leaders build structures, cultures and capabilities that hold up through these shifts.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Scaling leadership

Engineers and product specialists are frequently promoted into management because of technical skill, then asked to lead larger teams, manage managers and set strategy. Without deliberate development, these leaders struggle with delegation, feedback and cross-functional influence. The result is bottlenecks, inconsistent management quality and attrition among people who report to them.

02

Trust after restructuring

Layoffs and reorganizations leave remaining employees uncertain about strategy, job security and leadership intentions. Survivors often carry heavier workloads and may disengage quietly. Rebuilding trust requires honest communication, visible follow-through, clear priorities and attention to managers who must lead teams through the aftermath while processing the changes themselves.

03

Cross-functional friction

Product, engineering, design, sales and operations teams often work with different goals, rhythms and vocabularies. Decision rights are unclear, dependencies are poorly coordinated and the same debates repeat. As companies grow, this friction slows delivery and frustrates talented people who want to see their work reach customers.

04

AI adoption and the workforce

Technology companies are both building and adopting AI tools, changing how code is written, how customers are supported and which roles are needed. Employees want clarity on how their work will change and what skills they should build. Leaders need responsible adoption strategies that address productivity, job design, reskilling and governance together.

05

Building for diverse users

Products designed by homogeneous teams for imagined average users often fail people with disabilities, limited connectivity, different languages or different cultural contexts. These gaps create exclusion and reputational risk. Building inclusive products requires research with diverse users, accessible design practices and team cultures where people feel safe to raise concerns.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Technology shapes how people work, learn, communicate, access services and participate in civic life. The choices technology companies make about who they hire, how they lead and whom they design for have consequences far beyond their own walls. Companies with healthy cultures and capable leaders are better able to raise and resolve ethical concerns before products ship. Inclusive research and design practices help ensure that technology expands opportunity rather than deepening existing divides. Better people practices inside technology companies are a practical foundation for building products that serve society well.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does organizational consulting for technology companies involve?

Organizational consulting for technology companies focuses on structures, leadership, culture and ways of working that let the company deliver its strategy. Typical work includes organizational design during scaling or restructuring, leadership development for technical managers, team effectiveness, culture assessment, workforce planning for AI-driven changes, and design research that connects internal teams to user needs.

How do you help after layoffs or a major restructuring?

We start by listening to employees and managers to understand what has changed and where trust has been damaged. We then help leaders clarify strategy and priorities, redesign workloads and team structures where scope has expanded, equip managers to lead their teams through the transition, and establish regular check-ins to track recovery in engagement and retention over the following months.

Do you work with startups or only larger technology companies?

We work with growth-stage startups as well as established technology companies. For earlier-stage companies, we focus on building leadership capability, clear structures and culture foundations before rapid scaling creates problems. For larger companies, our work more often involves reorganization, cross-functional effectiveness, AI workforce strategy and cultural change across multiple business units.
