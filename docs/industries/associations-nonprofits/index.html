Context

The people side of associations & nonprofits.

Associations and nonprofits exist to serve members, professions and communities, yet many face a familiar tension. Members join for credentials, events, advocacy or community, then engage with only a small part of what the organization offers. Renewal becomes a habit for some and a question for others. Association member engagement is often measured through event attendance or email opens, which say little about whether members perceive enough value to stay, volunteer or recommend the organization to peers.

Inside these organizations, staff and volunteer leaders work with limited resources, complex governance and boards that may hold different views about priorities. Data lives in association management systems, learning platforms, event tools and email platforms that rarely connect with one another. Decisions about programs and pricing are often made on long-held assumptions about what members want. Generational shifts in how professionals seek community and learning challenge models built for earlier career patterns.

Many associations and nonprofits also run public education campaigns: encouraging health screenings, promoting safety practices or helping the public understand a profession's role. These campaigns are behavior change efforts, yet they are frequently designed around awareness alone. Current forces, including fragmented media, declining trust in institutions and misinformation, make it harder to reach audiences. Behavioral science helps organizations clarify member value, design campaigns that prompt action and measure impact credibly.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Member value that is unclear

Members often cannot articulate what they gain from membership beyond a single benefit. When the value proposition is diffuse, renewal depends on inertia, and price increases or budget pressures prompt cancellations. Organizations need to know which experiences actually create perceived value for different types of members.

02

Engagement measured by activity, not meaning

Open rates, logins and attendance are easy to count but may not predict renewal, volunteering or advocacy. Some highly valuable members engage rarely but deeply, while others participate often without strong commitment. Without behavior-based KPIs, associations invest in programs that look busy rather than those that build loyalty.

03

Public campaigns that stop at awareness

Public education efforts often aim to raise awareness, assuming action will follow. People may know a screening is recommended or a practice is safer and still not act. Campaigns that ignore ability, timing and prompts rarely change behavior, and success is hard to measure beyond reach and impressions.

04

Volunteer leaders and governance dynamics

Boards and committees bring expertise and commitment, but rotating leadership, differing priorities and limited time can slow decisions. Staff leaders must align volunteers around strategy without authority over them. Change initiatives stall when governance conversations rely on opinion instead of shared evidence about member behavior.

05

Demonstrating mission impact

Funders, members and boards increasingly ask nonprofits and associations to show the difference they make. Many organizations track outputs, such as programs delivered or people reached, rather than changes in behavior or outcomes. Building credible impact measurement requires clear logic, practical measures and analysis that resource-constrained teams can sustain.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Associations and nonprofits advance professions, protect public health and safety, share knowledge and strengthen communities. Their impact depends on engaged members and on public audiences who take recommended actions. Behavioral science advances that social good by clarifying what members truly value, designing campaigns that move people from awareness to action and measuring outcomes credibly. It helps mission-driven organizations spend limited resources where they make the most difference, and it supports ethical persuasion that respects audiences while encouraging the behaviors that protect health, safety and wellbeing.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How can associations improve member engagement and renewal?

Start by understanding which experiences create real value for different member types, then identify the early behaviors that predict renewal. Association member engagement improves when organizations design around those behaviors, for example by helping new members reach a valuable resource or community quickly, and when communications emphasize the value members have actually experienced.

What makes a public education campaign change behavior?

Effective campaigns define a specific action, understand what prevents the audience from taking it and design prompts and supports that make the action easier at the right moment. Awareness is often necessary but rarely sufficient. Testing messages with representative audiences and measuring behavior, not just reach, shows whether a campaign is working.

Can small nonprofits afford behavioral science work?

Engagements can be scoped to fit. Many organizations begin with a focused question, such as improving one renewal pathway or testing one campaign message, using data they already have. Workshops and training also help staff apply behavioral principles independently. The aim is to build lasting capability, not dependence on outside advisors.
