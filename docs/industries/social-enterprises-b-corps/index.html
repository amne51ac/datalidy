Context

The people side of social enterprises & b corps.

Social enterprises and B Corps set out to prove that business can serve people and planet while remaining financially sustainable. That ambition creates distinctive people challenges. Employees often join because of the mission and hold leaders to high standards on pay, inclusion, voice and transparency. When internal practices fall short of external commitments, disappointment can be sharp and public. Founders who built the company around a cause must learn to lead larger teams, delegate, and make tradeoffs between mission ambitions and commercial realities.

Outside the company, social enterprises answer to a wide set of stakeholders: customers who buy partly because of values, communities affected by operations, suppliers and producers in the value chain, impact investors and certifying bodies. Each group has its own expectations and definitions of success. Work-integration social enterprises that employ people facing barriers to employment, such as those returning from incarceration or experiencing homelessness, carry additional responsibility to design supportive workplaces that lead to lasting economic stability.

Several forces are shaping the sector now. B Corp certification standards have been revised with more demanding requirements across topics such as fair wages, governance and stakeholder engagement. Skepticism about greenwashing and impact-washing has increased scrutiny of claims. Impact investors want rigorous measurement. Growth capital can bring pressure that tests governance protections for mission. Social enterprise consulting must help companies grow commercially while keeping purpose embedded in strategy, structure, culture and measurement.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Mission drift during growth

Growth brings new investors, larger customers, expanded product lines and pressure on margins. Each decision can pull a company subtly away from its original purpose. Without clear governance, decision criteria and impact goals embedded in strategy, social enterprises risk becoming conventional businesses that keep the language of mission but lose its substance.

02

Living the values internally

Employees in mission-driven companies expect internal practices to match external commitments on pay equity, inclusion, wellbeing and employee voice. Gaps between brand and workplace reality damage morale and credibility. Leaders need honest assessment of how their people practices measure up and a plan to close the gaps they find.

03

Credible impact measurement

Social enterprises often rely on outputs and stories rather than rigorous evidence of outcomes. Investors, certifiers and customers increasingly expect clear, verifiable impact claims. Building measurement systems that capture real change for beneficiaries and communities, without overwhelming lean teams, is a common challenge.

04

Founder to scaled leadership

Founders bring vision and passion but may struggle to share authority as the company grows. Decision-making can bottleneck, and a leadership team may not have formed around the founder. Building leadership depth and clear governance while preserving the founder's mission commitment is essential for scaling.

05

Supporting employees facing barriers

Work-integration social enterprises employ people facing barriers such as justice involvement, housing instability or disability. Supporting them requires thoughtful job design, supervision, wraparound services and pathways to future employment. Balancing those supports with operational and financial performance requires careful design and continuous learning.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Social enterprises and B Corps aim to show that business can create shared value for workers, communities and the environment while remaining viable. Their credibility rests on whether they actually deliver on those promises. Strong people practices are central: fair pay, inclusive cultures, meaningful employee voice and supportive jobs for people facing barriers are often part of the impact itself. When these companies measure honestly and govern for mission, they strengthen trust in purpose-driven business and offer practical models that conventional companies can learn from and adopt.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does social enterprise consulting include?

Social enterprise consulting supports mission-driven companies in integrating purpose with strategy, operations, culture and governance. Our work includes mission-driven growth strategy, impact measurement and management, values-aligned people practices, governance for mission, stakeholder engagement and leadership development. We work with B Corps, benefit corporations, cooperatives, work-integration social enterprises and other companies built around social or environmental goals.

Can you help with B Corp certification?

We help companies strengthen the underlying practices that certification assesses, including governance, worker practices, community engagement and impact measurement. We can support preparation for certification or recertification, particularly in areas such as fair wages, pay equity, stakeholder engagement and governance. Our focus is on genuine improvement in practices, which tends to make certification outcomes more durable.

How do you prevent mission drift when raising capital?

We help companies define nonnegotiable mission commitments before fundraising, identify investors whose expectations align, and build governance protections such as mission-locked provisions, board representation and impact reporting requirements. We also help leaders embed impact goals in strategy and incentives, so that mission remains part of how the business is run, not just what it says.
