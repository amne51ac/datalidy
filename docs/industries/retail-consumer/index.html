Context

The people side of retail & consumer.

In retail, the employee experience and the customer experience are tightly linked. Associates on the sales floor, in distribution centers and in contact centers shape every shopper interaction. Yet frontline retail jobs often come with unpredictable schedules, limited hours, physically demanding work and few visible career paths. Turnover is high, and every departure means lost product knowledge, more training time and thinner staffing during busy periods. Store managers carry heavy responsibilities, from sales targets to loss prevention to hiring, with limited time to develop their teams.

Customers have more choice than ever and move fluidly between stores, apps, marketplaces and social channels. They expect consistent service, accurate inventory, easy returns and staff who can help when they need it. When stores are understaffed or associates are undertrained, shoppers notice immediately. Consumer brands also face growing expectations about how products are made, how workers in supply chains are treated, and how companies engage with the communities where they operate.

Several forces are reshaping retail work. Omnichannel fulfillment means store associates now pick online orders, manage curbside pickups and process returns alongside traditional duties. Self-checkout and AI tools are changing staffing models. Fair workweek laws in some jurisdictions require predictable scheduling. Margin pressure and store closures create uncertainty. Retail workforce consulting must help companies redesign frontline roles and leadership practices so that operational efficiency and good jobs reinforce each other rather than compete.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

High frontline turnover

Retail associates leave at high rates, driven by unpredictable schedules, insufficient hours, limited advancement and the quality of their manager. Constant churn raises recruiting and training costs and weakens service. Retailers need to understand which factors drive turnover in their specific stores and roles rather than relying on industry generalizations.

02

Overstretched store managers

Store managers juggle sales, operations, compliance, scheduling and people management, often with limited support from regional teams. Administrative tasks crowd out time for coaching and recognition. Because store managers strongly influence associate retention and customer experience, their capacity and capability are among the most important levers a retailer has.

03

Scheduling that works for people and the business

Last-minute schedule changes and variable hours make it difficult for associates to manage childcare, school or second jobs. Demand-driven scheduling tools can optimize labor costs while undermining stability. Designing schedules that meet operational needs and offer associates predictability and some control is a practical challenge with significant retention effects.

04

Inconsistent omnichannel experience

Customers move between online, mobile and in-store channels and expect them to work together. Inventory inaccuracies, confusing pickup processes and returns friction undermine trust. Often the root causes sit in disconnected processes, unclear roles and associates who were not trained or staffed for omnichannel tasks.

05

Limited career mobility

Many frontline associates want to grow but see few pathways beyond the store floor. Promotion criteria may be unclear, and development opportunities are unevenly available across stores and demographic groups. Without visible pathways, ambitious associates leave, and retailers lose people who could become their next store and district leaders.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Retail is one of the largest employers in many economies and often the first job for young people and the entry point for workers returning to the labor force. The quality of those jobs affects household stability, economic mobility and the vitality of neighborhoods. Retailers that offer predictable schedules, fair pay, good managers and real career paths help associates build careers, and those associates in turn deliver the service that keeps customers returning. Better people practices let retail be a genuine ladder of opportunity while strengthening the business.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does retail workforce consulting focus on?

Retail workforce consulting focuses on how retailers attract, schedule, develop, lead and retain frontline associates and store leaders. Our work includes retention analytics, store manager development, scheduling and role redesign, career pathway design, pay equity analysis and customer journey work. We connect these efforts directly to customer experience and operational performance so that investments in people are tied to business outcomes.

How does improving the employee experience affect customers?

In retail, associates create most customer interactions. When they are well trained, adequately staffed, fairly scheduled and led by capable managers, they have the knowledge and capacity to help shoppers. Lower turnover also means more experienced staff on the floor. We help retailers track employee and customer measures together so they can see these links in their own data.

Can you help with fair workweek compliance?

Yes. We help retailers understand predictive scheduling requirements in their jurisdictions and, more broadly, design scheduling practices that give associates stability while meeting operational needs. Compliance is the starting point; the larger opportunity is using scheduling as a retention and engagement lever. We work alongside legal and workforce management teams to design and test practical approaches.
