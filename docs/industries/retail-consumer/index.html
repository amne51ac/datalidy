Context

The people side of retail & consumer brands.

Retailers and consumer brands have access to vast amounts of data: point-of-sale transactions, ecommerce behavior, loyalty programs, app usage, reviews and survey results. Yet many still rely on demographic personas and stated preferences that explain little about why customers choose, switch or return. What shoppers say in surveys often differs from what they do at the shelf or in the cart, where habit, context, emotion and small frictions decide outcomes. Consumer behavior research grounded in behavioral science closes that gap.

Inside these organizations, merchandising, marketing, ecommerce, store operations and customer service each own part of the customer experience. Store associates shape impressions in ways that rarely appear in dashboards, and their engagement affects customer loyalty directly. Data sits in separate platforms, and teams often run disconnected tests that are difficult to compare. Decisions about promotions, assortment and messaging may rest on long-standing assumptions that have never been tested against behavior.

Current forces add complexity. Shoppers move fluidly between stores, apps, marketplaces and social channels. Price sensitivity and private label competition test brand loyalty. Personalization and AI-generated recommendations raise both opportunities and privacy concerns. Consumers expect brands to be honest about sustainability and value. Retailers and brands that understand the behavioral drivers of choice, and that use persuasion responsibly, are better placed to earn repeat purchase rather than buying it with discounts.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Personas that do not predict behavior

Demographic personas are easy to communicate but often fail to explain purchase patterns. Two customers with the same profile may shop entirely differently. Without behavioral segments based on how people actually buy, brands misallocate marketing spend and design experiences for an average customer who does not exist.

02

Conversion lost to small frictions

In both stores and digital channels, purchases are lost to minor obstacles: unclear pricing, crowded choices, slow checkout or uncertainty about returns. These frictions are hard to see in aggregate reports. Brands need methods that locate the specific conversion points where intention turns into abandonment.

03

Loyalty programs that reward discounts

Many loyalty programs train customers to wait for promotions rather than building genuine preference. Points and offers drive short-term activity but may erode margin and brand meaning. The behavioral question is what creates lasting attachment, such as convenience, recognition or identity, and how to measure it.

04

Messaging tested by opinion

Creative and messaging decisions are frequently made by internal preference or small focus groups. Messages that sound compelling to teams may not change consumer behavior. Without structured research and experimentation, brands risk investing in campaigns that win approval internally but have little effect on choice.

05

Frontline experience left unmeasured

Store associates and service teams influence whether customers feel welcomed, helped and confident. Their behavior is rarely measured in ways that connect to customer outcomes, and engagement programs for frontline staff are often generic. The link between employee experience and customer loyalty remains an untested assumption.

Where we focus.

    Behavioral segmentation

    We analyze transaction, loyalty and digital data to define behavioral types: groups of customers who share patterns of purchase, channel use and response to promotion. These segments guide targeting, assortment and experience design more effectively than demographic personas, and each comes with hypotheses about what would increase its value.

    We trace the path to purchase across store and digital channels to find the moments where intention is lost.     We test messaging, claims and creative concepts with representative consumers using structured research and, where possible, in-market experiments. Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Retail and consumer brands shape everyday choices about food, health, household budgets and sustainability. Behavioral science can make those choices easier and more aligned with what people value, or it can exploit attention and impulse. We advocate the former: clear information, honest messaging, fair loyalty programs and personalization that respects privacy. Designing for informed choice builds trust that lasts longer than any promotion. It also recognizes frontline associates as central to customer experience, supporting workplaces where employees can do good work and customers feel genuinely served.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How is consumer behavior research different from traditional market research?

Traditional market research often relies on what consumers say about their preferences and intentions. Consumer behavior research grounded in behavioral science also examines what people actually do, using transaction data, observation and experiments. Combining stated and observed behavior gives a more accurate picture of what drives choice and loyalty, and of which changes are worth testing first.

Can you work with both store and ecommerce data?

Yes. Customers move across channels, so we integrate store transactions, ecommerce events, app usage and loyalty data where possible. Where data cannot be linked directly, we use research and careful analysis to understand cross-channel behavior. The goal is to see the customer journey as customers experience it, not as separate channel reports.

How do you keep behavioral design ethical in retail?

We avoid tactics that rely on manufactured urgency, hidden costs or confusing choices. Our approach focuses on reducing genuine friction, clarifying value and helping customers make decisions they will be satisfied with later. We believe trust is a durable commercial asset, and we test interventions for customer satisfaction as well as conversion.
