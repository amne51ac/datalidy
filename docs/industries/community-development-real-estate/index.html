Context

The people side of real estate & community development.

Real estate and community development decisions shape where people live, work and gather for decades. Developers, housing authorities, affordable housing providers and community development corporations work at the intersection of capital, policy and neighborhood life. Inside these organizations, property managers, resident services staff and maintenance teams carry demanding roles that directly affect residents' daily lives, yet they are often underpaid and stretched thin. Turnover among site staff disrupts relationships with residents and weakens service quality.

Outside the organization, residents and neighbors want a real say in projects that change their communities. Many neighborhoods have experienced displacement, disinvestment or planning processes where input was gathered but ignored, leaving deep skepticism. Tenants want safe, well-maintained homes, responsive management and respect. Small businesses and community institutions worry about rising rents. Public meetings alone rarely reach working families, renters, non-English speakers or young people, whose perspectives are most often missing.

Several forces are shaping the sector. Housing affordability pressures are acute in many regions, and policy tools from inclusionary zoning to new tax credit programs continue to evolve. Place-based impact investors and public funders increasingly ask for evidence of community benefit and resident outcomes. Community benefit agreements and equitable development frameworks are gaining traction. Climate resilience is reshaping where and how to build. Community development consulting must help organizations engage residents authentically, support their own staff and demonstrate real neighborhood impact.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Earning community trust

Communities that have experienced displacement or broken promises are rightly skeptical of new development. Traditional public meetings tend to attract a narrow set of voices and occur after key decisions are made. Developers and agencies need engagement that starts early, reaches residents who are usually unheard and genuinely shapes project outcomes.

02

Displacement and equitable development

New investment can raise property values and rents in ways that push out long-term residents and small businesses. Organizations committed to equitable development need to understand displacement risk, design anti-displacement measures, and track whether current residents actually benefit from the changes their neighborhoods experience.

03

Resident experience in housing

Residents in affordable and market-rate housing alike experience maintenance delays, unclear communication and inconsistent management. These problems affect health, stability and trust. Improving resident experience requires understanding the full journey, from application and move-in through maintenance requests and renewal, and the staff workflows behind each step.

04

Frontline property staff retention

Property managers, maintenance technicians and resident services coordinators often face heavy workloads, difficult situations and modest pay. High turnover disrupts resident relationships and service quality. Organizations need to understand what drives departures and how to build career paths and supportive management for site-level staff.

05

Demonstrating community impact

Investors, public funders and community partners want evidence that developments deliver on promises around affordability, local hiring, community space and resident wellbeing. Many organizations track units and dollars but not outcomes for residents and neighborhoods. Building credible impact measurement for place-based work is a persistent challenge.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Housing and the built environment shape health, education, economic opportunity and social connection. Where people live affects which schools their children attend, how long they commute and whether they can remain near family and community. Development that excludes residents from decisions can deepen inequity and displacement, while development shaped with residents can expand opportunity and strengthen neighborhoods. Better people practices, from authentic engagement to well-supported site staff to honest impact measurement, help real estate and community development deliver lasting benefits for the people who call these places home.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What does community development consulting involve?

Community development consulting helps developers, housing providers, community development corporations, public agencies and investors plan and deliver projects that benefit existing residents. Our work includes community engagement and co-design, stakeholder and partnership strategy, community benefit agreement support, resident experience design, site staff retention and place-based impact measurement. We focus on making community input meaningful and outcomes measurable.

How do you reach residents who do not attend public meetings?

We work through trusted community organizations, faith institutions, schools and resident leaders. We hold sessions at varied times and locations, provide interpretation, childcare and food, and compensate participants. We also use door-to-door conversations, small group discussions and short multilingual surveys. Reaching renters, working families and young people requires going to them rather than expecting them to come to us.

Can you help with community benefit agreements?

Yes. We help developers, coalitions and public agencies design processes for negotiating community benefit agreements, identify priorities through engagement, define specific and measurable commitments, and set up monitoring and reporting. We focus on agreements that are clear about obligations, timelines and accountability, so that communities can see whether promises have been kept.
