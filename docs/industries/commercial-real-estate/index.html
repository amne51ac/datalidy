Context

The people side of commercial real estate & capital markets.

Commercial real estate and capital markets firms often offer a broad platform: debt and equity placement, investment sales, leasing, valuation, property management and advisory. Clients could benefit from more of that platform, and firms invest in integration to make it happen. Yet commercial real estate cross-selling frequently disappoints. Producers protect relationships they have built over years, worry about service quality in unfamiliar lines and lack a clear picture of what colleagues in other groups actually do. The barriers are behavioral before they are structural.

Sales in this sector are relationship-driven. Deals depend on trust, timing and market knowledge held by individual brokers and bankers. Referral behavior is shaped by reputation, reciprocity and perceived risk. Leaders often rely on incentive splits and mandates to drive collaboration, then find that the behavior they want does not follow. Meanwhile, CRM data is incomplete because producers see little reason to enter it, which leaves leadership making growth decisions with partial information.

Current forces add pressure. Capital markets cycles shift activity across service lines, making cross-referral a source of resilience. Clients increasingly expect integrated advice across their portfolios. Consolidation brings teams with different cultures under one brand. Technology and AI tools promise better pipeline visibility but depend on adoption by busy producers. Firms that understand the psychology of relationship-driven sales and measure the right leading behaviors can build collaboration that holds through market cycles.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Producers who protect their relationships

A broker or banker's client relationships represent years of work and personal reputation. Introducing a colleague from another service line feels risky if the producer cannot vouch for the outcome. Without trust between groups, cross-selling programs produce meetings and presentations but few genuine referrals that clients welcome.

02

Incentives that do not change behavior

Firms often adjust fee splits or set referral targets to encourage collaboration. Incentives matter, but they rarely overcome concerns about client experience, unclear processes or unfamiliarity with colleagues. When the incentive becomes the main lever, producers may refer superficially or game the system rather than collaborate in ways clients value.

03

Limited visibility into the pipeline

CRM systems in commercial real estate are often incomplete because producers see entry as administrative overhead. Leaders lack reliable signals about which relationships span service lines, where referrals originate and which interactions precede closed business. Growth strategy relies on lagging revenue figures and anecdote rather than predictive measures.

04

Integrating cultures after combinations

Mergers and team acquisitions bring groups with different norms around sharing information, splitting fees and serving clients. Leaders may announce an integrated platform while day-to-day behavior stays siloed. Without attention to culture and trust, the expected collaboration benefits of a combination are slow to appear.

05

Clients who see fragments of the platform

Clients often know a firm through one producer and one service line. They may not realize the firm could help with financing, leasing or valuation elsewhere in their portfolio. When integrated advice is not offered at the right moment, clients engage competitors and the firm's broader capabilities remain underused.

How we help

Where we focus.

  1. →

    Cross-sell process design

    We study how referrals actually happen in your firm, through interviews with producers and analysis of deal and CRM data, and identify the moments where collaboration succeeds or stalls. We then design a cross-sell process around trust, clear handoffs and client value, with roles and routines that producers find practical rather than bureaucratic.

    Predictive pipeline KPIs

    Using Behavioral Signals Design, we identify the producer and client behaviors that precede cross-line business, such as joint client meetings or shared market updates. These become predictive KPIs that leaders can track before revenue appears, giving earlier insight into whether collaboration initiatives are working and where to intervene.

    Leadership alignment for integrated growth

    Service line leaders often agree on integration in principle and differ in practice. We facilitate alignment on shared goals, decision rights and the specific behaviors expected from leaders, then coach them to model collaboration. Alignment work is grounded in data about current referral patterns so conversations stay concrete.

    Client experience research

    We interview clients about how they experience the firm across service lines, what they value in advice and when they would welcome introductions to colleagues. Findings inform both cross-sell design and messaging, ensuring that collaboration is framed around client needs rather than internal revenue goals.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Commercial real estate and capital markets shape where people work, shop, receive care and live, and the financing behind those properties influences the vitality of communities. Behavioral science contributes by improving how firms collaborate to give clients sound, integrated advice, which supports better investment and property decisions. It also strengthens the professional cultures behind that advice, rewarding trust and knowledge sharing over territorial behavior. When firms understand the human side of relationship-driven sales, they serve clients more completely and build organizations that remain resilient through market cycles.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Why does commercial real estate cross-selling often underperform?

Because it depends on producers trusting colleagues with relationships they value highly. Incentives and mandates do not resolve concerns about client experience, unclear handoffs or unfamiliarity with other service lines. We address these behavioral barriers directly, designing processes and leadership practices that make referral feel safe and beneficial for producers and clients alike.

How do you measure collaboration before it shows up in revenue?

We identify leading behaviors that precede cross-line business, such as joint pitches, shared client meetings and documented introductions, and validate them against historical deal data where possible. These predictive KPIs let leaders see whether collaboration is growing months before fees are recognized, and they highlight which teams or offices need support.

Do you work with capital markets teams specifically?

Yes. Debt and equity advisory teams have distinctive relationship dynamics and are often central to cross-line opportunities, since financing touches many other services. We work with capital markets leaders and producers to understand referral patterns, client expectations and the coordination required to deliver integrated advice across a firm's platform, from first introduction through closing.
