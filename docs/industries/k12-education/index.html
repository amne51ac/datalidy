K-12 Education

K-12 student engagement is built through daily routines, relationships and a sense of belonging. We help districts, schools and education organizations understand the behaviors behind attendance and learning, design practical supports and measure what helps.

Context

The people side of k-12 education.

Schools see behavior every day: who arrives on time, who participates, who completes work and who drifts toward the edges of the classroom. K-12 student engagement shows up in attendance patterns, assignment completion, extracurricular involvement and how students talk about school. Chronic absence and disengagement often begin quietly, with a few missed days or a change in friendships, and become visible as problems only after they affect learning. Districts collect much of this data, but it is spread across student information, learning and survey systems.

Belonging and relationships are foundational. Students who feel known by at least one adult and connected to peers are more likely to attend, participate and seek help. Families are essential partners, yet communication from schools can feel confusing, punitive or one-directional, especially for families who had difficult school experiences or speak languages other than English. Inside schools, teachers manage complex classrooms with limited time, and teacher wellbeing affects both retention and student experience.

Current forces include ongoing recovery in attendance and learning, rising attention to student mental health, staffing shortages, new technology and AI tools in classrooms, and pressure to show results with limited resources. Education leaders need approaches that respect educators' expertise and time. Behavioral science, grounded in learning research and careful measurement, helps schools focus on the few behaviors and moments that matter most and test supports before scaling them, so that limited time and funding go to what helps students.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Chronic absence that builds quietly

Absences accumulate a day at a time, often for reasons schools do not see: transportation, health, caregiving, anxiety or disconnection. By the time a student is flagged as chronically absent, patterns are entrenched. Communications to families can unintentionally sound punitive, discouraging the partnership that would help students return.

02

Belonging that some students lack

Students who do not feel they belong are less likely to engage, attend or ask for help. Belonging can be fragile during transitions such as entering middle or high school. Many schools value belonging but lack practical ways to build it consistently and measures that show whether efforts are reaching students who need them.

03

Teacher time, wellbeing and retention

Teachers face heavy workloads, complex student needs and frequent new initiatives. When new programs arrive without removing old demands, adoption suffers and burnout grows. Teacher turnover disrupts relationships that students depend on, making teacher wellbeing a direct factor in student engagement and learning.

04

Family communication that misses the mark

Schools send many messages, but families may receive them through the wrong channel, in an unfamiliar language or framed in ways that feel judgmental. Important information gets lost among routine notices. Effective family partnership requires communication designed around how families actually read, decide and act.

05

Data rich, insight poor

Districts collect attendance, grades, assessments, behavior incidents and survey responses, but integrating them into timely, actionable insight is difficult. Educators receive reports that describe the past rather than signals that suggest where to act next, and data dashboards can add to workload without changing decisions.

How we help

Where we focus.

    Attendance and engagement signal analysis

    We analyze attendance, academic and survey data to identify early patterns that precede chronic absence and disengagement, distinguishing behavioral types such as students with sporadic absences and those withdrawing gradually. Findings become a small set of predictive indicators and practical hypotheses that school teams can act on during the year.

    Family communication design

    We redesign attendance notices, school updates and outreach with families, applying behavioral science and plain-language principles. Impact lens

The social good at stake.

K-12 schools shape children's knowledge, relationships and sense of what is possible. When students disengage or miss school, the effects compound over years and fall unevenly on families with fewer resources. Behavioral science advances education's public good by helping schools notice disengagement earlier, communicate with families as partners and design environments where more students feel they belong. It also protects educators by focusing improvement on the few behaviors that matter. Every intervention should respect students and families and help them do what they already want: learn, grow and thrive.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How does behavioral science improve K-12 student engagement?

It identifies the specific behaviors and moments that shape engagement, such as attendance in the first weeks of school or connection with an adult mentor, and designs supports for them. Instead of broad initiatives, schools focus on a few targeted changes that fit their routines, then measure whether those changes help students attend, participate and learn. Successful approaches can then be shared across schools.

Will this add to teachers' workloads?

We design specifically to avoid that. Interventions are chosen for feasibility within existing schedules, and we often recommend removing or simplifying tasks alongside any new practice. Teachers and school leaders participate in design, and we test changes at small scale so that only approaches that prove useful are expanded across classrooms or schools.

Do you work with education nonprofits and edtech organizations?

Yes. Education nonprofits and edtech organizations face related questions about student engagement, family communication, product adoption and evidence of impact. We apply the same behavioral methods, adapting them to each organization's role in the education system and to the needs of the schools and families it serves, with evaluation designed in from the start.
