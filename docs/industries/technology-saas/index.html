Context

The people side of technology & saas.

Software companies collect more behavioral data than almost any other industry, yet many still struggle to explain why some accounts thrive and others churn. Product analytics show clicks and sessions, but SaaS user activation depends on whether users reach a meaningful outcome early enough to form a habit. Teams often define activation by convenience, such as completing setup, rather than by the behaviors that actually predict retention and expansion. The result is onboarding optimized for the wrong moment.

Adoption is also an organizational behavior. In business software, an individual champion may love a product while colleagues ignore it, and renewal depends on whether usage spreads across teams. Administrators, end users and economic buyers have different motivations and constraints. Inside the company, product, design, customer success, sales and marketing each hold part of the adoption story and are measured on different numbers, which makes it difficult to agree on what to fix first.

Current forces sharpen these questions. AI features are being added rapidly, but users adopt them only when they trust the output and understand where it fits their work. Buyers scrutinize spend and expect clear value. Product-led and sales-led motions blend, raising questions about handoffs. Growing companies also face leadership challenges as teams scale. Behavioral science offers a disciplined way to connect product data, user research and experimentation around the behaviors that drive durable growth.

Challenges

What leaders are up against.

01

Activation defined by setup, not value

Many teams count a user as activated after completing onboarding steps. Those steps may have little to do with the value that keeps customers. Without validating activation against retention, companies optimize flows that look successful while users leave before experiencing what the product is actually good at.

02

Features shipped but not adopted

Roadmaps deliver new features, including AI capabilities, that only a small share of users discover or use repeatedly. Announcements and tooltips rarely change established workflows. Adoption depends on relevance, timing and trust, and teams often lack research into how users decide whether a new feature is worth the effort.

03

Account adoption beyond the champion

In business software, a single enthusiastic user does not guarantee renewal. Value must spread to teams and become embedded in workflows. Customer success teams may see usage concentrated in a few people without knowing which behaviors signal healthy account adoption or what would help it expand.

04

Experiments without behavioral hypotheses

Many SaaS companies run A/B tests, but tests often vary button colors or copy without a clear theory of behavior. Results are inconclusive or fail to generalize. Experimentation becomes more valuable when hypotheses are grounded in behavioral models and connected to predictive KPIs that matter for retention.

05

Scaling teams and decision quality

Rapid growth adds people, layers and handoffs faster than leadership practices mature. Product and go-to-market teams may disagree about priorities, and decisions slow or become political. Leaders who were effective at small scale must learn new ways to align teams, delegate and decide under pressure.

Impact lens

The social good at stake.

Software increasingly shapes how people work, learn, communicate and access services. When technology is hard to adopt, it wastes time, frustrates users and widens gaps between those who can navigate complexity and those who cannot. Behavioral science advances the social good in technology by designing products around real human attention and ability, encouraging persuasive design that serves users' own goals and helping teams adopt AI responsibly. Companies that measure activation by genuine value create products people choose to keep using, which is better for users and for sustainable growth.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How do you define SaaS user activation?

We define activation by the early behaviors that statistically predict retention and expansion for your product, not by setup completion alone. That requires analyzing historical cohorts, testing candidate definitions and confirming them with user research. A good activation metric is specific, observable early and clearly connected to the value customers pay for.

How is this different from what our product analytics team does?

We complement internal analytics rather than replace it. Our contribution is a behavioral science lens: theories about why users act as they do, qualitative research to explain patterns, measurement design and experiments built around clear hypotheses. We often work alongside product analysts to strengthen the link between data, design and decisions.

Can you help with AI feature adoption?

Yes. AI features raise distinctive adoption questions around trust, explainability and fit with existing workflows. We research how users evaluate AI output, design onboarding and prompts that build appropriate trust and help teams define standards for responsible use. Adoption is measured by sustained, valuable use rather than first trials or one-time experiments.
