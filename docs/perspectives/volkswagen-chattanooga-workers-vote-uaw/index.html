Volkswagen's Chattanooga Workers Vote to Join the UAW on the Third Attempt

Workers at Volkswagen's Chattanooga plant voted by a wide margin to join the United Auto Workers in April 2024, a reversal of two earlier defeats. The shift says as much about how employees weigh voice and fairness as about labor strategy.

Event: April 2024 · Organizations: Volkswagen, United Auto Workers, National Labor Relations Board
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • NPR reported that about 73% of ballots cast favored joining the UAW, with turnout of 83.5%.
  • The union had lost two earlier votes at the same plant, in 2014 and 2019.
  • Workers described wanting a say in schedules, benefits and pay, which points to voice as a core motivator.
  • Card-signing momentum and turnout are leading indicators that leaders can read long before an election.

What happened

Workers at Volkswagen's assembly plant in Chattanooga, Tennessee, voted to join the United Auto Workers in an election held April 17 to 19, 2024. NPR reported that 2,628 workers voted yes and 985 voted no, about 73% in favor, with roughly 4,300 hourly workers eligible and turnout of 83.5%. The UAW had lost two earlier elections at the same plant, in 2014 and 2019.

In a March 25 announcement, the UAW said a supermajority of workers had signed union cards and that a majority had signed by early February, about two months after the public campaign began. The National Labor Relations Board scheduled the secret-ballot election. One assembly worker quoted by the union said, "We need a say in our schedules, benefits, pay, and more."

The vote drew opposition from state leaders. NPR reported that Tennessee Gov. Bill Lee and the governors of five other Southern states issued a joint statement opposing the union drive. Volkswagen said, "Volkswagen thanks its Chattanooga workers for voting in this election," and had earlier said it respected employees' right to decide through a democratic process. NPR noted the Chattanooga plant had been the only Volkswagen plant worldwide without worker representation.

The human dimension

The same plant, the same union and broadly the same workforce produced a very different result than in 2019. NPR quoted a worker who voted no in 2019 and yes in 2024, and another who said the company seemed neutral before the election. Those accounts suggest that the decision was shaped by changes in perceived fairness, trust and the credibility of the alternative, factors that can move quickly when conditions shift.

Self-determination theory offers a useful lens. People are motivated by autonomy, competence and relatedness, and the language workers used centered on autonomy: having a say. When employees feel that decisions about their schedules and pay are made without them, collective representation becomes a way to restore voice. Employer neutrality may also have reduced the sense of risk associated with voting yes, which matters because fear of consequences is a powerful brake on stated preferences.

The signals that mattered

Union elections are lagging events. By the time an election is scheduled, the underlying shift in employee sentiment has usually been building for months, and the UAW's own timeline shows a majority signing cards within about two months of the public campaign.

  • Speed and breadth of card-signing or petition activity across shifts and departments
  • Employee survey items on voice, fairness and trust in decision-making
  • Changes in how former skeptics describe the company to colleagues
  • Scheduling and overtime complaints raised through informal channels
  • Participation rates in company forums compared with union meetings

Lessons for leaders and investors

For leaders and investors in manufacturing, the result is less about whether a workforce unionizes than about what the vote revealed: a strong, measurable desire for voice that existing channels were not satisfying. Organizations that measure employee voice well can respond to concerns early, and can build labor relationships, union or not, on clearer information.

The vote also marks the start of a new relationship rather than the end of a contest. NPR reported that the UAW's next target was a Mercedes-Benz plant in Alabama, which signals that other employers in the region will face similar questions. Companies that invest early in listening, transparent communication and fair processes are better prepared for any outcome, and for the bargaining that follows.

  • Measure employee voice directly, with validated survey items, and act visibly on results.
  • Treat a rapid shift in sentiment as a strategic signal, not only a human resources matter.
  • In diligence, assess labor relations readiness and the quality of management communication.
  • Prepare leaders to bargain and collaborate constructively if representation is chosen.

The public-good dimension

Elections like this one are a democratic process inside the workplace, and their legitimacy depends on workers being able to choose without fear. High turnout and a clear result give both the company and the union a mandate to build a working relationship. When management and labor both invest in that relationship, the benefits reach families and communities that depend on stable, fairly paid work. Datalidy's view is that organizations perform best when employees have real voice, whatever form it takes.
