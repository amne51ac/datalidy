Threads Hits 100 Million Sign-Ups, Then Faces the Retention Test

Meta's Threads reached 100 million sign-ups within days of its July 2023 launch. Within weeks, third-party estimates showed steep declines in daily use. The gap between those two numbers is a lesson in which metrics predict lasting behavior.

Event: July 2023 · Organizations: Meta, Instagram, Sensor Tower, Similarweb
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • Sign-ups measure curiosity and ease of entry; repeat visits and time spent measure whether a product has become a habit.
  • Meta's leadership publicly shifted focus to retention, which is the metric most closely tied to long-term value.
  • Missing core features can depress early retention even when acquisition is exceptional, because ability limits repeat behavior.
  • Investors should weight cohort retention and engagement depth over headline acquisition milestones when evaluating launches.

What happened

Meta launched Threads, a text-based social app linked to Instagram accounts, in early July 2023. Bloomberg reported on July 10 that the app had reached 100 million users days after launch. Because people could sign up using existing Instagram accounts, the barrier to entry was low, and the milestone drew wide attention as one of the fastest consumer app launches on record.

Usage data soon told a more complicated story. TechCrunch reported on July 18 that Similarweb estimated Threads' daily active users had fallen from 49 million to 23.6 million within a week. On August 4, Fortune, citing CNN's reporting on Sensor Tower data, reported that daily active users were down 82 percent from launch as of July 31, falling from a peak of 44 million to fewer than 8 million. Average daily time spent fell from 19 minutes to 2.6 minutes, and daily opens fell from about 14 to about three, according to that report.

Meta's leaders framed the decline as expected. Mark Zuckerberg wrote that "early growth was off the charts, but more importantly 10s of millions of people now come back daily," TechCrunch reported, and said the focus for the year would be "improving the basics and retention." Instagram's head said growth, retention and engagement were ahead of company projections. TechCrunch listed features Meta said were still to come, including a following feed, post search and an edit button. Third-party estimates use different methods and should be read as directional.

The human dimension

The launch shows how different behaviors are driven by different conditions. Signing up required one tap for an existing Instagram user, plus curiosity and a sense that others were joining. Coming back daily required something else: a reason to return, a feed worth reading, and the basic tools people had come to expect from similar apps. When those conditions are thin, novelty fades and the original prompt to visit loses its force.

Meta's leadership appeared to recognize this quickly, publicly setting retention as the priority rather than celebrating acquisition alone. That is a meaningful choice. Organizations often reward teams for the metric that moves fastest at launch. Shifting attention to the slower, harder behavior, whether people return without being reminded, aligns incentives with long-term value even when the numbers look less impressive in the short term.

The signals that mattered

The most useful signals were visible within days: time spent per session, daily opens and the share of new users who returned in the first week. Each indicated whether early interest was turning into a routine.

  • Day-one, day-seven and day-thirty return rates for each launch cohort
  • Average daily sessions per active user, tracked weekly after launch
  • Share of new users who follow, post or reply within their first visit
  • Requests for missing features, segmented by user activity level

Lessons for leaders and investors

A launch milestone is a lagging indicator of marketing reach and a weak predictor of durable value. Leaders who define early retention and engagement depth as the primary success measures before launch are better placed to make sound decisions about investment and product sequencing afterward.

Investors evaluating consumer products should be wary of launch milestones presented without cohort data. A large number of sign-ups can reflect the strength of an existing distribution channel more than the appeal of the new product. The more predictive question is how many people return on their own, how deeply they engage, and whether those patterns stabilize. Meta's public shift toward retention offers a useful model: name the behavior that matters, then report on it even when the trend is unflattering.

  • Set cohort retention targets before launch so acquisition success does not redefine the goal
  • Treat missing core features as a constraint on user ability, not only a roadmap item
  • Report time spent and repeat visits alongside sign-ups in board materials
  • In diligence, request cohort curves rather than cumulative user counts

The public-good dimension

Social platforms shape how people find information and talk to one another, so the behaviors they cultivate matter beyond any one company's results. Designing for healthy return visits, rather than for maximum compulsion, is both an ethical and a strategic choice. Measuring whether people find lasting value, not just whether they arrive, is how product teams can align growth with the wellbeing of the people they serve. That is the kind of predictive measurement Datalidy encourages leaders to build in from the start.
