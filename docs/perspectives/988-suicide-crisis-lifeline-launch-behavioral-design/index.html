The 988 Lifeline Launch: Designing a Crisis Line People Will Use

On July 16, 2022, the United States moved its national suicide prevention line to a three-digit number, 988. Making help easier to reach is a behavioral design choice, and it only works if the system behind the number is ready to answer.

Event: July 2022 · Organizations: U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • A short, memorable number reduces the effort required to seek help at a moment when ability is lowest.
  • Lowering barriers increases demand, so capacity and answer rates become the decisive measures of success.
  • Federal officials projected that contact volume would at least double, making staffing and funding leading indicators.
  • Sustained local funding and planning, not the launch itself, determine whether callers reach a person quickly.

What happened

On July 16, 2022, the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline transitioned to 988, a three-digit number people can call or text for 24/7 crisis support. NPR, in a report published by OPB, said calls are routed to a crisis center near the caller within a network of more than 200 local centers, with 16 backup centers taking calls when a local center is busy. The previous ten-digit number remained active and routes to 988.

According to NPR, HHS said more than $400 million had gone to local and backup call centers and related services, up from $24 million, including support for a Spanish-language subnetwork. The lifeline handled 3.6 million calls, chats and texts in 2021, and officials projected that volume would at least double in the first full year. HHS Secretary Xavier Becerra marked the transition at a crisis center in Philadelphia on July 15.

NPR also reported readiness concerns. A 2021 SAMHSA report found the existing system answered 85 percent of calls, 56 percent of texts and 30 percent of chats. A RAND survey of public health officials found that 16 percent had created a budget for 988 operations, and only four states had added a phone fee to fund the service. Advocates quoted by NPR described the transition as the beginning of a longer effort rather than a finished system.

The human dimension

In a crisis, the ability to act is constrained. Behavioral science, including B.J. Fogg's model that behavior happens when motivation, ability and a prompt converge, suggests that reducing friction matters most precisely when people are least able to work through it. A number as easy to remember as 911 lowers the effort of seeking help, and the same design logic underlies text and chat options for people who are unable or unwilling to speak aloud.

The human dimension also includes the people answering. NPR reported that many crisis centers had closed in recent years for lack of resources, and that advocates were calling for sustained federal, state and local investment. Crisis counselors work in emotionally demanding conditions, and a surge in demand without matching staffing risks burnout and longer waits. An HHS official quoted by NPR said the vision is crisis services that work "much the way emergency medical services work," which requires people, not only a number.

The signals that mattered

Because 988 was designed to increase help-seeking, success depends on whether supply keeps pace with demand. Several measures could serve as leading indicators for policymakers and funders.

Volume alone is an ambiguous measure. Rising contacts may mean the number is working as designed or that other services are failing. Pairing volume with answer rates, wait times and follow-up connections gives a clearer picture of whether people in crisis are actually being served.

  • Answer rates for calls, texts and chats, tracked separately by channel
  • Share of contacts rolled over from local centers to backup centers
  • Counselor staffing, turnover and vacancy rates at local centers
  • State funding mechanisms and budgets in place for ongoing operations
  • Public awareness of 988 among groups at elevated risk

Lessons for leaders and investors

The 988 launch is a useful case for any leader who reduces friction in a service. Easier access changes behavior, and the downstream system must be designed for the demand it creates. For health systems, payers and impact investors, the crisis continuum around 988, including mobile response and stabilization services, is where outcomes will be determined.

  • Forecast demand changes before reducing barriers, and fund capacity accordingly.
  • Measure the full journey, from first contact to follow-up care, not only call volume.
  • Invest in workforce wellbeing as an operating requirement for crisis services.
  • Use channel-level data to design for people who prefer text or chat.

The public-good dimension

Few public services show the link between design and wellbeing more directly than a crisis line. A number that is easy to remember, staffed by trained people who answer quickly, can connect someone to support at a decisive moment. Measuring answer rates, wait times and follow-up honestly, and funding the workforce that makes the promise real, is how behavioral design serves public health rather than only signaling good intentions. At Datalidy, we regard 988 as a model of how lowering barriers must be paired with readiness.
