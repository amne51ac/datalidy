WHO Declares Mpox a Global Health Emergency as Clade Ib Spreads

On August 14, 2024, the World Health Organization declared the mpox upsurge in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and neighboring countries a public health emergency of international concern. The response depends as much on behavior, trust and access as on vaccines.

Event: August 2024 · Organizations: World Health Organization, Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, Pan American Health Organization
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • Rising case counts in the DRC for over a decade were a long-running signal before the 2024 emergency declaration.
  • Spread through close contact and sexual networks makes stigma a direct barrier to testing, reporting and care.
  • Undertesting means reported numbers understate reality, so surveillance capacity is itself a key performance indicator.
  • Funding, vaccine access and community trust determine whether an emergency declaration changes behavior on the ground.

What happened

On August 14, 2024, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus declared the upsurge of mpox in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and a growing number of African countries a public health emergency of international concern under the International Health Regulations, following advice from an emergency committee of independent experts, according to WHO. It was the second such mpox declaration in two years; the previous one ran from July 2022 to May 2023.

WHO said that more than 15,600 cases and 537 deaths had been reported in the DRC so far that year, exceeding the previous year's total. A newer strain, clade Ib, had emerged in the DRC the year before and appeared to spread mainly through sexual networks. WHO reported that more than 100 laboratory-confirmed clade Ib cases had been identified in the prior month in Burundi, Kenya, Rwanda and Uganda, none of which had previously reported mpox, and that the true number was likely higher because many cases were not tested.

The day before, Africa CDC declared a public health emergency of continental security, its first such declaration since its founding in 2017, KSL reported. WHO said it anticipated an immediate need of 15 million US dollars for surveillance, preparedness and response, released 1.45 million dollars from its emergency contingency fund, and had begun an Emergency Use Listing process to speed vaccine access for lower-income countries.

The human dimension

Outbreak control is fundamentally behavioral. People must recognize symptoms, decide to seek care, be willing to disclose contacts and accept vaccination when it is offered. Each of those steps can be blocked by stigma, cost, distance or mistrust. When a disease spreads through close and sexual contact, fear of judgment can discourage the very disclosures that contact tracing depends on. Health communication that is respectful, specific and delivered by trusted local voices is therefore not a soft add-on, it is part of the core response.

Health workers and public health teams face their own pressures: limited testing capacity, scarce supplies and the challenge of reaching communities affected by displacement and poverty. WHO's note that many clinically compatible cases were not tested captures a recurring problem. When the system cannot see a case, it cannot respond to it, and the people most affected are often those with the least access to care.

The signals that mattered

WHO noted that reported cases in the DRC had risen steadily for more than a decade before increasing significantly in 2023. That long trend, combined with a new strain appearing in countries with no prior history of mpox, formed the leading indicators that justified escalation. In outbreak response, the capacity to detect is as important a measure as the count of what is detected.

  • Multi-year growth in reported cases in endemic regions, not only sudden spikes
  • Detection of a new strain in countries with no prior reported cases
  • Share of suspected cases that receive laboratory testing
  • Time from symptom onset to care seeking, a proxy for stigma and access
  • Vaccine doses available relative to estimated need in affected areas

Lessons for leaders and investors

The mpox emergency is a reminder that health systems, philanthropies and health-focused investors must treat surveillance and trust as infrastructure. Declarations raise attention and open funding channels, but outcomes depend on whether people change behavior: seeking testing, disclosing contacts and accepting vaccines. Leaders who design for those behaviors, and measure them, convert emergency attention into lasting capacity.

  • Invest in testing and surveillance capacity before emergencies, since undercounting delays every later decision.
  • Design health communication with affected communities to reduce stigma and encourage early care seeking.
  • Measure vaccine uptake and access barriers, not only doses shipped.
  • For impact investors, evaluate health programs on behavioral outcomes and equity of reach.

The public-good dimension

Infectious disease does not respect borders, and outbreaks that go unseen in under-resourced regions can become global problems. The 2024 mpox emergency underscores that equitable access to testing, vaccines and respectful care is both a moral obligation and a practical one. Behavioral science contributes by helping public health systems understand why people do or do not seek care, and by designing messages and services that make the healthy choice the easier one, without blame or coercion.
