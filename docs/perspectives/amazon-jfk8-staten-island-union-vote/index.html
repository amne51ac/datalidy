Amazon Workers on Staten Island Vote for the Company's First U.S. Union

On April 1, 2022, workers at Amazon's JFK8 warehouse in Staten Island voted to be represented by the Amazon Labor Union, a newly formed independent group. The outcome made employee voice at large operations a strategic topic for leaders.

Event: April 2022 · Organizations: Amazon, Amazon Labor Union, National Labor Relations Board
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • Collective action is often the visible end of concerns that were measurable earlier through feedback and turnover.
  • A worker-led organizing effort can build trust that outside campaigns struggle to match.
  • Perceptions of safety, workload and fairness shape engagement and retention as strongly as pay does.
  • Investors increasingly see labor relations as a factor in operating risk and long-term value.

What happened

On April 1, 2022, workers at Amazon's JFK8 fulfillment center on Staten Island voted 2,654 to 2,131 to be represented by the Amazon Labor Union, according to vote counts reported by Fortune and HR Dive. The union would represent more than 8,300 workers, Fortune reported, making JFK8 the first Amazon facility in the United States to vote to unionize, a milestone for organizing efforts at the company.

Amazon said it was "disappointed with the outcome of the election in Staten Island" and was "evaluating our options, including filing objections," according to Fortune. HR Dive reported that the Amazon Labor Union was formed in 2021 by a former employee who said he was fired after organizing protests over COVID-19 safety, and that workers had raised concerns about safety protocols, job security and working conditions.

The same week, a recount at Amazon's Bessemer, Alabama facility left the outcome of a separate union election dependent on challenged ballots, HR Dive reported. In January 2023, a regional director of the National Labor Relations Board dismissed Amazon's objections to the JFK8 election and certified the union as the workers' representative, the Amsterdam News reported; Amazon said it intended to appeal.

The human dimension

The JFK8 campaign was led by current and former workers rather than an established national union. That matters behaviorally. People tend to trust messengers who share their experience, speak their language and face the same conditions. Peer-to-peer organizing can build credibility that formal communication from either management or outside groups finds harder to establish.

The concerns workers raised were about daily experience: safety during the pandemic, workload, job security and whether their voice influenced decisions. In large operations, these experiences are shaped by systems such as productivity targets, scheduling and the reach of frontline managers. When people feel those systems treat them as interchangeable, collective representation becomes a way to restore a sense of agency.

The signals that mattered

Signals of this kind rarely appear suddenly. Large logistics operations produce detailed data about attendance, injuries, turnover and feedback that, read together, can show where trust is eroding.

The difficulty for large employers is not a lack of data but a lack of integration and attention. Productivity systems are monitored minute by minute, while measures of trust and fairness are often collected annually and reviewed far from the floor. When people metrics receive the same timeliness and leadership attention as throughput, managers can see where strain is concentrated and respond before frustration becomes collective action.

  • Early-tenure turnover and rehire rates by site and shift
  • Injury and near-miss reports relative to productivity targets
  • Survey scores on safety, fairness and being heard, by team
  • Volume and themes of employee complaints and escalations
  • Participation in walkouts, petitions or informal organizing

Lessons for leaders and investors

For leaders, the lesson is that employee voice will find a channel. Organizations that build credible ways to hear and act on frontline concerns, and that measure trust as carefully as throughput, are better placed to address problems early. For investors, labor relations at scale affect operating continuity, regulatory exposure and reputation. Boards can ask management how it measures frontline experience and what it has changed as a result.

It is also fair to recognize the constraints. Amazon operates very large facilities with demanding service promises, a workforce that grew rapidly during the pandemic and safety challenges that few organizations had faced before. Those conditions make consistent frontline leadership difficult. They also make structured listening more valuable, because problems that are local and solvable can otherwise accumulate into a broader loss of trust.

  • Measure trust and fairness with validated instruments and report results to the board
  • Link productivity system design to injury, turnover and engagement data
  • Respect workers' legal right to organize and train managers accordingly
  • Treat high turnover as a cost and a signal, not as a feature of the model

The public-good dimension

Warehouse and logistics work supports the goods many households rely on, and it employs large numbers of people in physically demanding roles. Safety, fair treatment and a meaningful voice at work affect health and family stability. The JFK8 vote renewed public discussion about the quality of jobs in the logistics economy. Companies that design work around human limits and listen to the people doing it serve both their workforce and the communities in which they operate.
