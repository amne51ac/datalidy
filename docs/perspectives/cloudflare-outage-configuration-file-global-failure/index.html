Cloudflare Outage Traced to an Oversized Configuration File

Cloudflare's detailed account of its November 18 outage shows how an internal change, a hard limit and a coincidental status page failure combined. It is a case study in how teams read signals under pressure.

Event: November 2025 · Organizations: Cloudflare
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • Cloudflare said a database permissions change caused a bot management file to double in size and exceed a software limit.
  • Failures fluctuated every five minutes and the status page went down by coincidence, leading some engineers to suspect an attack.
  • Cloudflare committed to treating its own generated configuration files with the same caution as user input.
  • Diagnosis under uncertainty is a team behavior that improves with practice, clear roles and independent signals.

What happened

On November 18, 2025, Cloudflare, which CNBC reported manages and secures traffic for about one-fifth of the web, suffered a major outage. CNBC reported that sites including X, Shopify, Indeed and Anthropic's Claude were affected, and that OpenAI's status page attributed issues with ChatGPT and Sora to a third-party provider. Cloudflare's shares fell more than 2%, according to CNBC. CNBC reported that Downdetector itself was briefly inaccessible for some users.

In its post-incident report, Cloudflare said a permissions change to a ClickHouse database cluster at 11:05 UTC caused a query to return duplicate rows. That doubled the size of a "feature file" used by its Bot Management system. The proxy software enforced a limit of 200 features, and the oversized file exceeded it, causing errors across the network. Cloudflare said the outage "was not caused, directly or indirectly, by a cyber attack or malicious activity of any kind."

Cloudflare said core traffic was largely flowing again by 14:30 UTC and all systems were normal at 17:06. The company committed to treating its own generated configuration files with the same caution as user input, adding more global kill switches, and reviewing failure modes in its core proxy modules. "On behalf of the entire team at Cloudflare, I would like to apologize for the pain we caused the Internet today," the report said.

The human dimension

The most instructive part of Cloudflare's account concerns diagnosis. The feature file was regenerated every five minutes, and only some database nodes produced bad data, so the network alternated between recovering and failing. At the same time, Cloudflare's status page, hosted outside its own infrastructure, went down by coincidence. Cloudflare said these signals led some engineers to suspect an attack. When evidence fluctuates and an unrelated failure appears at the same moment, even expert teams can form a plausible but wrong hypothesis.

This is a well-documented pattern in decision-making under pressure. Early hypotheses shape where people look next, and intermittent symptoms make it harder to rule explanations in or out. Teams that assign someone to challenge the leading theory, and that have independent ways to check what changed recently, tend to converge faster.

The signals that mattered

The triggering change was internal and routine, and the file it affected was generated by Cloudflare's own systems. Inputs that a system trusts because they come from inside are often less guarded than external ones, which makes them worth measuring.

Hard limits deserve particular attention. A limit exists to protect a system, but when an input exceeds it without a graceful fallback, the protection becomes the point of failure. Tracking headroom against limits turns a hidden cliff into a visible trend.

  • Share of internally generated configuration validated against size and schema limits
  • Headroom between typical configuration size and hard limits in critical software
  • Correlation of recent internal changes with error spikes, available to responders
  • Time to confirm or rule out an attack hypothesis during incidents

Lessons for leaders and investors

CNBC noted that the outage followed a daylong AWS outage less than a month earlier. For leaders and investors, the pattern is a reminder that a few infrastructure providers carry a large share of online activity, and that their internal habits affect everyone downstream.

Cloudflare's commitment to add global kill switches mirrors a broader principle: the ability to switch off a feature quickly is one of the most valuable controls an organization can have. Leaders can ask how many critical features have such a switch, and how recently each was tested.

  • Treat internal data and configuration as untrusted input in critical systems.
  • Train incident teams to test competing hypotheses explicitly, not sequentially.
  • Assess customers' dependence on single providers for security and delivery services.
  • Value transparent post-incident reporting as a signal of a learning culture.

The public-good dimension

When a provider that sits in front of a large share of websites fails, people lose access to news, work tools, transit services and support at once. CNBC reported that some NJ Transit digital services went down. Datalidy sees Cloudflare's candid account as a useful public resource: it shows that reliability depends on how people interpret signals and challenge assumptions, not only on code.
