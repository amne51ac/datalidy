Bed Bath & Beyond Files for Bankruptcy After Years of Decline

Bed Bath & Beyond filed for bankruptcy on April 23, 2023, after a decade of struggles, strategy reversals and supply problems. Its story is a case study in how customer habits, supplier confidence and testing discipline interact.

Event: April 2023 · Organizations: Bed Bath & Beyond, Buy Buy Baby
This analysis examines the event as it unfolded, based on the reporting cited below. Later developments may not be reflected.

Key takeaways

  • Bed Bath & Beyond filed for Chapter 11 on April 23, 2023, planning an orderly wind-down, PBS NewsHour reported.
  • Analysts called the shift away from coupons and toward store brands a major misstep, Fortune reported.
  • Changing the prompt and the reward of a shopping habit at once gives loyal customers a reason to reconsider.
  • Turnaround diligence should examine customer habits and supplier confidence, not only margins and store counts.

What happened

On April 23, 2023, Bed Bath & Beyond filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in New Jersey, saying it planned "to implement an orderly wind down of its businesses" while seeking buyers for some or all of its assets. According to PBS NewsHour, 360 Bed Bath & Beyond stores and 120 Buy Buy Baby stores remained open at the time of filing, with closures expected, and the company secured about $240 million in financing from Sixth Street Specialty Lending to operate during the process.

The filing followed years of decline. Fortune reported that for about a decade the chain struggled with a cluttered product mix and a weak online strategy against competitors such as Target, Walmart and Wayfair. In late 2019 the company hired a new chief executive who cut coupons and shifted toward store-label brands, a move analysts considered a major misstep; he was ousted in June 2022, and the company returned its focus to national brands. In August 2022 it announced it would close about 150 stores and cut 20 percent of its workforce.

Supply problems compounded the decline. PBS reported that pandemic closures and supply chain issues left the chain without many best-selling items, and that after the company defaulted on its loans in January 2023, suppliers were reluctant to commit merchandise. Fortune reported that sales fell 33 percent in the fiscal third quarter. A GlobalData Retail analyst, quoted by PBS NewsHour, called the filing "the death of an icon."

The human dimension

Bed Bath & Beyond's well-known coupon was more than a discount. For many customers it functioned as a habit loop: a prompt that arrived in the mailbox and a reason to visit. Removing coupons and shifting the assortment toward private labels changed both the prompt and the reward at the same time. Customers who came for familiar national brands found a different store. When the cue and the payoff of a routine change together, people have a reason to reconsider the routine, and competitors were ready to receive them.

The later stages show a reinforcing loop. Financial stress made suppliers cautious, which left shelves short of key items, which reduced visits, which deepened financial stress. Each participant in that loop, from suppliers to shoppers, behaved reasonably given what it could see. Leadership turnover and strategy reversals added uncertainty for employees and partners, making it harder to rebuild the confidence any turnaround needs.

The signals that mattered

Retail decline often shows up first in customer and partner behavior, well before it appears in quarterly results. Some of these signals were visible in public reporting, such as supplier reluctance and shelves short of best-selling items. Inside a company, behavioral data on lapsed customers can show far sooner how a promotional change is landing.

  • Visit frequency and lapsed-customer rates after changes to promotions
  • Share of baskets containing the national brands customers came in to buy
  • In-stock rates on best-selling items, tracked weekly
  • Supplier terms and credit limits as signals of partner confidence
  • Online repeat purchase behavior relative to competitors

Lessons for leaders and investors

For leaders, the case shows the risk of changing several behavioral drivers at once without testing. Coupons, assortment and brand mix can each be piloted in a subset of stores with clear behavioral KPIs before a chainwide shift. For private equity and other investors in retail turnarounds, the case highlights the need to diligence customer habits, not only margins, because a strategy that improves unit economics on paper can erode the visits those economics depend on.

  • Pilot major promotional or assortment changes before rolling them out chainwide
  • Track lapsed loyal customers as a predictive KPI during turnarounds
  • Treat supplier relationships as a leading indicator of financial resilience

The public-good dimension

When a national retailer closes, the effects reach employees, landlords, suppliers and communities that lose a store. PBS reported that the company had 32,000 employees as of February 2022. Earlier, testable decisions and honest measurement of customer behavior do not guarantee survival, but they give organizations more time and more options, which is ultimately what protects jobs and communities. Datalidy's perspective is that customer habits are assets worth measuring as carefully as inventory.
